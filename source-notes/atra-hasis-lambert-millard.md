# Atra-ḫasīs: The Babylonian Story of the Flood

## Source identity and access

- Wheel source ID: `atra-hasis-lambert-millard`
- Bibliographic record: W. G. Lambert and A. R. Millard, 1969
- Inspected representation: `data-library/atrahasis-woh/chapter-1.json`
- Access level: `project_digitization`

The inspected Wheel representation describes itself as a best-effort Akkadian
reconstruction drawing on Lambert and Millard and named later scholarship. It
is not a substitute for direct inspection of the copyrighted monograph.

## Source type and purpose

The registered work is a scholarly edition and study of the Babylonian
*Atra-ḫasīs* tradition. The relevant ancient composition concerns divine labor,
the creation of humankind, population, plague, flood, and survival.

## Reported content

The inspected project representation at `ATRA-WOH-I:195` reads:
“let man bear the labor of the gods.” Its transliteration is recorded as
`a-wi-lum li-iš-ši du-ul-li i-li`.

The surrounding project apparatus treats the line as part of a sequence in
which a primeval human is created to assume divine labor.

## Basis or method

The Wheel translation metadata identifies Lambert–Millard line numbering and
records reviewer approval while retaining a pending sign-by-sign collation

checked the repository representation and its status metadata, not the full

## Limitations and criticism

- The current Wheel translation is explicitly a best-effort reconstruction
  pending fuller collation.
- A single anthropogony line cannot establish the identity of the Anunnaki and
  Elohim.
- Narrative-function comparison must preserve differences between
  Mesopotamian labor anthropology and biblical image/likeness language.
- Names and collective rosters vary across Mesopotamian periods and texts.
