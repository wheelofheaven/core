# The Book Which Tells The Truth

## Source identity and access

- Wheel source ID: `the-book-which-tells-the-truth`
- Public record: Raël, dated 1973 in the Wheel registry
- Inspected representation:
  `data-library/the-book-which-tells-the-truth/chapter-1.json` and
  `chapter-2.json`
- Access level: `project_digitization`

This note does not establish which printed edition controls every English
wording. Edition and translation comparison remain open review tasks.

## Source type and purpose

Wheel's bibliography classifies the work as Raëlian scripture and foundational
canon. The text presents an encounter narrative and messages attributed to a
member of the Elohim civilization.

## Reported content

Two passages are immediately relevant to claim `woh-claim-0001`:

- `TBWTT-1:53` reports: “We are men like you, and we live on a planet quite
  similar to Earth.”
- `TBWTT-2:5` reports the source's gloss of `Elohim` as those who came from the
  sky, describes scientists selecting Earth, and describes the creation of
  artificial life.

These are source reports. They establish the content of the inspected project
representation, not the historical occurrence or external origin of the
reported encounter.

## Basis or method

The work presents revelatory or contact testimony rather than an independently
documented empirical study. The project digitization contains French source
text and multilingual renderings, but this pilot did not collate the quoted
English passages against every printed French and English edition.

## Limitations and criticism

- Authorship, transmission, edition history, and translation need a dedicated
  source-critical record.
- The source is the principal basis for the claim it reports, so later works
  dependent on it are not independent corroboration.
- Internal detail, coherence, or anticipation claims require separate records
  and cannot be inferred from the two passages above.
- Alternative explanations of the report have not been systematically
  reviewed in this pilot.

## Wheel interpretation

**Interpretation:** Wheel treats the reported speakers as a real biological
civilization and uses the report as the foundational lens for biblical and
comparative material. The source note records that project use without treating
canonical centrality as empirical validation.

The source does not by itself establish the Hebrew semantics of `Elohim`, the
identity of other ancient collectives, or the occurrence of the reported
biological creation programme.

- Later Raëlian works and combined editions may clarify or extend the report.
- Biblical and comparative sources are independent textual corpora but become
