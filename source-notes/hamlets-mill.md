# Hamlet's Mill

## Source identity and access

- Wheel source ID: `hamlets-mill`
- Registered authors: Giorgio de Santillana and Hertha von Dechend
- First published 1969
- Access level: `metadata_only`

Only the Wheel bibliographic record and the public corpus's citation
descriptions were inspected for this pilot. The monograph itself was not
inspected; no passage-level locators are recorded here.

## Source type and purpose

A comparative-mythology monograph arguing that a large body of world myth
preserves, in narrative form, technical knowledge of the precession of the
equinoxes transmitted from a preliterate archaic source.

## Reported content

No substantive report is recorded because the work was not inspected. The
public corpus cites the book for its general thesis, not for verified
specific passages.

## Basis or method

Not assessed at this access level. The registered critical literature
describes the method as associative accumulation of motifs across cultures.

## Limitations and criticism

The Wheel registry includes contemporary scholarly reviews — among them the
*Journal for the History of Astronomy* review (3, 1972, pp. 206–211) and the
*Folklore* review (85.4, pp. 282–283) — that dispute the book's philology,
its handling of dates, and its inference from motif resemblance to
transmitted astronomical knowledge. Until the monograph and its reviews are
inspected at passage level, this note cannot support any specific
myth-to-precession correspondence.

## Wheel interpretation

**Interpretation pending source access.** The book is the framework's main
modern source for the contested layer of claim `woh-claim-0003` — that
precessional knowledge predates Hipparchus and is encoded in myth. The core
records that use without adopting the thesis as an observation: claim 0003
carries the layer as a contested interpretation, and the mainstream
attribution of the discovery to Hipparchus stands unless a reproducible
review changes it.
