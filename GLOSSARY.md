# Glossary

## Accepted

### Claim kind

What a statement is doing epistemically: recording a source report or
observation, proposing a model or hypothesis, offering an interpretation, or
making a normative recommendation.

### Evidence status

The maturity and condition of the evidence map for a precisely stated claim.
Evidence status is independent of lifecycle and framework relation.

### Foundational canon

The Raëlian source corpus that Wheel of Heaven explicitly adopts as its primary
interpretive lens. Foundational status describes the source's role in the
project; it does not establish external truth or consensus.

### Framework relation

How a claim functions within Wheel of Heaven: foundational, derived,
comparative, contextual, or critical.

### Lifecycle

The repository status of a document or record: draft, proposed, accepted,
deprecated, or superseded. Lifecycle does not measure evidential support.

### Public label

The reader-facing `direct`, `framework`, `inferred`, or `speculative` badge used
by Wheel's publishing surfaces. It is a presentation classification, not a
substitute for the core's orthogonal research fields.

### Source note

An inspectable record of source access, reported content, limitations, and
Wheel interpretation, keyed to an existing Wheel source ID.

### Source report

A statement about what an identified source explicitly says or depicts. A
source report does not establish that the reported event or explanation
occurred.

## Draft

### Elohim-civilization hypothesis

The foundational Wheel of Heaven proposition that the beings identified as the
Elohim in its reading were a technologically advanced biological civilization
from elsewhere and were responsible for designing terrestrial life.

### Anunnaki–Elohim identity claim

The comparative Wheel of Heaven proposition that the Mesopotamian Anunnaki and
the Elohim refer to the same underlying civilization. It does not automatically
identify every named deity across traditions or adopt Sitchin's reconstructions.
