# RFC 0008: A standing claim-review cycle

## Review question

Should core adopt a recurring, ranked cycle for reviewing accepted claims,
with a canon passage index and an insight log as standing instruments, so
that the working hypothesis is refined by routine rather than by occasional
campaigns?

## Summary

Core has the parts of a review system and no rhythm. RFC 0007 defines
signed review records and the rule for advancing `evidence_status`; the
datasets have shown how passage-level audits find compressed claims. But
all 79 claims are `accepted` and `scoped`, 66 are also `contested`, two
have review records, and 126 of 313 source references are `metadata_only`.
The labels no longer separate strong claims from weak ones.

This RFC proposes:

1. a **review cycle**: one claim per cycle, chosen from a ranked queue, put
   through fixed stages and closed by a signed RFC 0007 review record and,
   where needed, an ADR;
2. a **canon passage index**: the three canonical books indexed by topic and
   voice, so that claims quote the canon instead of summarizing it;
3. an **insight log**: candidate readings that arise during audits, held as
   research questions until the passage they rest on is in hand;
4. a **contact-point register**: for each foundational claim, the parts that
   meet checkable evidence (texts, dates, the physical record) and the parts
   that do not;
5. a **quarterly state-of-the-hypothesis note** recording what was
   strengthened, narrowed, corrected or left open.

## Problem and scope

**What happened on 2026-11-20/21.** Two passage-level audits (the flood
concordance and the Divine-Council Index) and one canon re-reading produced
more correction than any argument had: `woh-claim-0010`'s canonical layer
contradicted TBWTT 2:55–68 and LWTE 2:25–28; fourteen of seventeen council
rows were corrected; four lines in the library's Atrahasis were found to
have no counterpart in the edited tablet. Each finding came from the same
loop: a structured artifact, a named edition per cell, a compressed claim
found, a revision by ADR. The loop works; it is run by hand, case by case.

**What the catalog shows.** Generated by
`scripts/research/review_queue.py` on 2026-11-21:

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Claims | 79, all `accepted` |
| `evidence_status` | 79 `scoped`, 66 also `contested`; none `reviewed` |
| Review records | 2 (0070, 0071) |
| Revised past 0.1.0 | 17 |
| Source references | 313; 126 `metadata_only` |
| Most depended on | 0001 (38 dependents), 0020 (13), 0008 (10), 0003 (9) |

**Scope.** Accepted claim records, their specifications and evidence maps,
the canonical books, and the datasets that feed claims. Out of scope:
public prose (it follows records through the derivation contract), the
translation programme, and any change to RFC 0007's advancement rule.

## Claim kinds and evidence

The cycle treats claim kinds differently, because what counts as progress
differs:

- `source_report` (e.g. 0070): progress is exact locators and edition
  identity.
- `interpretation` (64 claims): progress is located passages, alternatives
  at full strength, and narrowing where a passage does not carry the claim.
- `hypothesis`, `model`, `empirical_observation`: progress is contact
  points: what would favour, narrow or count against the claim.
- `normative_recommendation`: progress is stating the grounds; these rank
  low in the queue.

The canon is the declared interpretive centre. The cycle tests whether a
claim states the canon correctly; it does not test the canon against
outside authority. Comparative, scientific and critical layers keep their
own voice, as `content-editing.md` requires.

## Detailed proposal

### 1. The queue

`scripts/research/review_queue.py` ranks claims by dependents, share of
unread sources, number of public pages, absence of a review record, absence
of any revision, and time since `last_reviewed`. The weights are in the
script; changing them is a reviewable diff. The score is triage, not a
verdict. The founder may override the order.

Queue on 2026-11-21:

| # | Claim | Dependents | Unread | Pages |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0001 Elohim-civilization hypothesis | 38 | 0% | 7 |
| 2 | 0003 Precessional world-age chronology | 9 | 83% | 23 |
| 3 | 0020 The contact reports as the canon's provenance | 13 | 25% | 2 |
| 4 | 0008 The Council–Serpentine conflict | 10 | 33% | 8 |
| 5 | 0015 The Great Return as conditional arrival | 5 | 33% | 3 |

0001 leads on dependents alone. It is a compound record whose full split
(subclaims B–D) is already deferred by ADR 0007 until a review advances;
reviewing it is a programme of several cycles, best begun after the canon
passage index exists. **The first cycle should be 0003**: it combines the
highest share of unread sources with the most public pages, and the canon
close-reading notes (core branch `research/canon-close-reading`) already
bear on it (the canon day of about 2,000 years against the 2,160-year
convention).

### 2. The stages of one cycle

Each cycle produces one RFC 0007 review record,
`docs/reviews/woh-claim-NNNN-review-MMMM.md`, with these components:

1. **Canon re-read.** Every canonical locator opened and quoted, voices kept
   apart: Yahweh's dictation (TBWTT), the narrated revelations (ETTMTTP,
   LWTE 2), Raël's commentary (LWTE 1), third-party essays (LWTE 4,
   Terrusse). A claim that summarizes the canon is checked against the
   quotation.
2. **Source access.** Each `metadata_only` reference is opened, replaced, or
   marked as an acquisition need (RFC 0007 "feedback into source
   references").
3. **Cell check.** Where a dataset or evidence map supports the claim, every
   cell it relies on is checked against a named edition, as in the flood and
   council audits.
4. **Alternatives at strength.** Each alternative restated in its best form
   with its best source; new ones added.
5. **Contact points.** What in the claim meets checkable evidence, and what
   does not (see section 4).
6. **Outcome.** Confirm, narrow, correct, split, or advance; corrections to
   records go through an ADR; affected pages bump `core_versions`.
7. **Independent review** of the findings by an agent or person that did not
   produce them, given the record and the sources, not the conclusions.
8. **Founder sign-off** (RFC 0007).

### 3. Canon passage index

A research dataset in `docs/research/canon-index/`: one row per passage
(TBWTT, ETTMTTP, LWTE, by refId), with topic tags, voice (dictation,
narrated revelation, Raël's commentary, third-party essay), the claims that
cite it, and cross-references where the books differ or one corrects
another. It starts from the close-reading notes and grows with each cycle;
it is never a paraphrase layer. Claims then cite the index rows they rely
on.

### 4. Insight log and contact-point register

`docs/research/insights.md` holds candidate readings that arise during
cycles, each in the shape of `research-question-template.md`: the exact
question, the passage it rests on, competing answers, and what would
discriminate between them. An entry becomes a claim proposal only when its
passage is in hand and an alternative has been stated at strength. Example
from ADR 0015: the possible correspondence between the Mesopotamian
rescuing god and the canon's exiled creators, considered and not adopted.

`docs/research/contact-points.md` lists, for each `foundational` and
high-dependency claim, the subclaims that are in principle checkable
(textual, chronological, archaeological, astronomical, biological) and
those that are not, with the current state of each. This is where new
research questions with a chance of a definite answer are found.

### 5. Cadence and reporting

- One cycle every two weeks, alternating with dataset work.
- A quarterly note, `docs/research/state-YYYY-QN.md`: claims reviewed,
  revisions, status changes, new insight-log entries, open acquisition
  needs, and the next queue.

## Alternatives

- **Campaigns only.** Continue as now: audits when a topic comes up. Cheap,
  but leaves most claims unexamined and the labels uninformative.
- **Breadth-first.** Run a light pass over all 79 claims before any deep
  review. Finds gross errors quickly, but the 2026-11 work showed that the
  significant errors only appear at passage level.
- **Rank by public traffic.** Prioritize claims behind the most-read pages.
  Serves readers sooner, but ignores dependency: an error in 0001 or 0020
  propagates further than a popular page.

## Risks, limitations, and dissent

- The score can be gamed by its own weights; its output is a proposal to
  the founder, not an order.
- Cycles cost founder attention at sign-off. Records may be signed with
  components outstanding (RFC 0007), which keeps the cadence realistic.
- Agent-run stages can return confident errors. Stage 7 exists for that,
  and the 2026-11 reviews caught several.
- The insight log could become a back door for speculation into claims. The
  passage-in-hand rule is the guard.
- Some sources cannot be opened (paywalled or unpublished editions). The
  cycle records them as acquisition needs rather than treating them as read.

## Compatibility and migration

No schema change. Uses RFC 0007 records and the existing `evidence_status`
vocabulary. Adds one script (`scripts/research/review_queue.py`) and, on
acceptance, three research documents (`canon-index/`, `insights.md`,
`contact-points.md`). Existing review records (0070, 0071) count as
completed cycles.

## Validation plan

After two quarters: how many claims have signed reviews; how many moved to
`reviewed`, were narrowed, or were corrected; how many `metadata_only`
references were opened; how many insight-log entries became claim
proposals or were closed. If cycles produce no corrections and no status
changes, the stages or the queue are wrong and this RFC should be revised.
