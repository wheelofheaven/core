# The comparative datasets as tabulated registrations

## Exact proposition

The corpus's curated comparative datasets are tabulated
registrations: each row a per-tradition registration at its stated
grade against a named primary source, each dataset a research
instrument serving its governing claim(s). Tabulation is never
accumulation — rows sharing a documented textual stream are one datum
under the independence-of-evidence principle, and no dataset's size is
an argument.

An `interpretation`, `contextual`, `inferred`.

## The service mapping and the forbidden move

Each dataset serves named claims: the flood-myth concordance serves
`woh-claim-0010`, the divine-council index `woh-claim-0011`, the
theomachy cross-references `woh-claim-0008`, the prophets-and-religions
table `woh-claim-0038`, the world-ages table `woh-claim-0003`, and the
motif-index derivatives the `woh-claim-0056` surface. The record is
the sibling of `woh-claim-0056` at the tabular grain: where 0056
governs the motif inventory as an evidence surface, this record
governs what a *row* is and what a *table* may never become. The
forbidden move is counting — reading row-count or coverage as
evidence for any claim. A dataset registering a tradition's divergence
is doing its job exactly as much as one registering convergence. The
technical datasets (the content graph) are infrastructure, outside
this record. The curation-selection objection — tables shaped by the
framework's questions cannot claim neutrality — and the
instrument-drift objection — publishing tables invites size-reading
whatever the record forbids — are carried in full.
