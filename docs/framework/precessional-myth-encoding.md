# Precessional knowledge encoded in myth

## Exact proposition

A recurring family of world myths — the great mill that turns and is
wrecked, the axle unhinged, the ages that grind and break — encodes
observational knowledge of the precession of the equinoxes in narrative
form, carried by tellers who no longer understood the technical content.
This is the Hamlet's Mill thesis; the corpus adopts a bounded version of it
as the mythological layer of its precessional chronology.

An `interpretation` with framework relation `comparative` and public label
`inferred` — matching the published Explainer's own label.

## Provenance

Distilled at founder direction from *The Mill That Grinds the Ages*. The
record supplies the mythological layer `woh-claim-0003` references; the
astronomy and the age-lattice stay in 0003.

## The bound on "bounded"

The record adopts the thesis's *shape* — a motif family with astronomical
content, transmitted past its own comprehension — and none of its
instances severally: Santillana and Dechend's specific etymologies and
identifications are outside scope, and the standard scholarly demolition
of their method (the 1972 JHA review) is carried as the leading
alternative, not footnoted away. The canon's cycle symbolism (the emblem
as "infinity in time"; the cycle sign in ancient writings) is read
*alongside* the encoding thesis, deliberately unfused with it.

**The Doubled Signature principle is excluded.** The corpus's named
extension beyond Hamlet's Mill is reserved as its own future claim with a
`speculative` label; nothing in this record licenses it.

## A note on access honesty

*Hamlet's Mill* itself remains `metadata_only` in the project's holdings —
cited by 0003, engaged closely by the Explainer, never inspected inside
core's own discipline. The wantlist carries it, and its inspection is this
record's first revision trigger in practice.

## Alternatives and challenges

Method failure (the mainstream review position — motif-hunting without
controls); agricultural-and-cosmological symbolism needing no measurement;
and the partial thesis (post-Hellenistic encodings only).
