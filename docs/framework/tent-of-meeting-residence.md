# The tent of meeting as the creators' residence

## Exact proposition

Per the canon, the sanctuary dictated at Sinai (the canon cites Exodus
26) was a residence the Israelites built to the creators' own plans for a
team staying on Earth for a time. It was the tent of meeting, where the
creators met the people's representatives and where food and goods were
brought, read as provisioning and "a pledge of submission". Moses'
face-to-face exchanges at the tent door (Exodus 33:9–11) are read as
conversation with a suited creator. The corpus records the reading at the
canon's level, with the mainstream account of the Tabernacle as a
priestly sanctuary tradition in its own voice.

## What the canon says, and where

- TBWTT-3:34–35: the team "was going to reside for a time on Earth and
  wanted fresh food", with riches to take back to its planet ("a bit of
  colonization, if you like"), citing Exodus 25:2–7.
- TBWTT-3:36: « une résidence suivant leurs plans. C'est ce qui est dicté
  au chapitre XXVI de l'Exode. Dans cette résidence ils devaient
  rencontrer les représentants des hommes : c'est la tente du
  rendez-vous où les hommes apportaient nourriture et présents en gage
  de soumission. »
- TBWTT-3:38–42: Exodus 33:9, 33:11 and 33:20, glossed by the difference
  in atmosphere and the suit.
- TBWTT-3:43–45: Leviticus 21:17 read as provisioning discipline.
- TBWTT-3:95–96: telepathy only when the creators were near; otherwise
  the Ark's transmitter-receiver. This is the device reading of
  woh-claim-0040, which this record rides on and does not restate.

**Unlocated quotation.** TBWTT-3:37 quotes « Il entrerait dans la tente
du rendez-vous » with no citation. The quoted passage has not been
located. Per woh-claim-0066 the canon's quotations come through Dhorme,
so the next step is a check against the Dhorme Exodus. The record does
not rely on 3:37 for any clause.

## Boundaries

- **The two-tents problem.** The canon merges the tent Moses pitched
  "without the camp" (Exodus 33:7) with the priestly Mishkan at the
  camp's centre. The record registers that the canon merges them and
  does not settle the source-critical question.
- **Glory as craft.** The canon reads the "glory" as the craft at Sinai
  (TBWTT-3:33; Exodus 24:17). It does not make that reading at the
  Tabernacle's cloud and glory (Exodus 40:34–38), and this record does
  not extend it there.
- **No sequence of reception sites.** The canon heads its Solomon section
  « La première résidence pour l'accueil des Elohim » (TBWTT-3:102),
  which sits uneasily beside its calling the tent a « résidence ». Any
  continuity from Tabernacle to Temples to Embassy is unrecorded, and the
  Embassy = Third Temple identification belongs to woh-claim-0016 alone.
- **No reconstruction** of the structure, its materials, or its
  dimensions.
- **Other furnishings.** The Ark is woh-claim-0040 and the Menorah
  woh-claim-0069; the altars, table, and laver have no record.
- **Traditions in their own voice.** Jewish (Shekhinah) and Christian
  (Hebrews 8–9) theology of the Mishkan is preserved in its own voice.

## Primary text

The primary text is the ASV (1901) `exodus` digitization. There is no
Wheel of Heaven translation of Exodus 25–40. When one exists, the
renderings of *mishkan*, *ohel moʿed*, and *tavnit* ("pattern") are
revision triggers.

## Alternatives and challenges

- Priestly retrojection: the Tabernacle is the Temple projected into the
  wilderness (the Wellhausen line).
- Two tents: source criticism separates an oracle tent from the
  Mishkan, a split the canon's harmonization ignores.
- Cultic idiom: "food of God" and sacrificial gift language are ancient
  Near Eastern temple idiom, not logistics.
- Speculation lineage: the reading belongs to the 1970s
  ancient-astronaut register (compare alt-0040-speculation-lineage).
