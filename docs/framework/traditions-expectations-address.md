# The address to the traditions' expectations

## Exact proposition

In the second message's closing address, Yahweh speaks to each tradition
in turn:

- the Christian who has read "a hundred times that Jesus would return"
  (`ETTMTTP-3:226`);
- the child of Israel who still waits for the messiah (`3:227`);
- the Buddhist: "your writings indicate that the new Buddha must be born
  in the West; recognize the foreseen signs!" (`3:228`);
- the Muslim, told to "welcome the last of the prophets" (`3:229`).

Read in that context, and with `ETTMTTP-3:257` ("the last of the line of
the prophets"), the corpus takes the Buddhist clause as pointing at
Raël. The clause is carried in the canon's own words.

An `interpretation`, `derived`, `inferred`.

## Why inferred

Only the Muslim clause names "the last of the prophets". The Buddhist
clause points at Raël by parallel. That step is the corpus's reading, so
the label is `inferred`, not `direct`.

## Exclusions

- **The name Maitreya** is excluded (founder direction, 2026-11-08). The
  canon does not use it.
- **What Buddhist scripture predicts.** The record carries what the canon
  says the writings indicate, not what the writings say. The strongest
  objection (`alt-0078-no-western-birth`) is that the classical accounts
  of the next buddha place him in India. No Buddhist primary text is held
  in the project library, so the check is outstanding.
- **Raël's later titles and publications** (post-canon reception) are not
  used.
