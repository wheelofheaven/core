# Samsara re-read through recreation

## Exact proposition

The canon re-reads the Buddhist rebirth cycle at the scale of humanity.
In the second message, Yahweh takes the Buddhist account of the dying
"soul" that must escape the "demons" or fall back into the cycle, and
applies it "not to the individual but to humanity entire"
(`ETTMTTP-2:24`):

- the demons are aggressiveness against one's fellows and against nature;
- falling back into the cycle is a civilization's return to the
  primitive stage and a new slow progression;
- "the state of beatitude through awakening" is the golden age of
  civilizations, where science is at the service of humanity.

Separately, and on its own passages, the canon promises individual
"scientific reincarnation" on the planet of the Eternals (`ETTMTTP-3:208`,
`ETTMTTP-3:214`). That is the recreation mechanism of `woh-claim-0029`.
The canon does not attach it to the Buddhism passage, and the record does
not either.

An `interpretation`, `comparative`, `framework`.

## Two layers, kept apart

The humanity-scale reading is what the Buddhism passage says. It couples
to the threshold choice (`woh-claim-0031`) and to the emblem's cycle sign
(`woh-claim-0043`, which already cites `ETTMTTP-2:24`). The individual
layer is the canon's recreation doctrine and is cited only where the
canon states it. Prose must not merge the two.

## The honesty clauses

The canon credits the Buddhist account as "a very good description" and
then changes its subject from the individual being to humanity. The
record carries that move by name (`alt-0048-subject-substitution`). Where
the individual promise is set beside Buddhist rebirth, *anatta* makes "the
same being recreated" precisely what Buddhist philosophy denies
(`alt-0048-frame-mismatch`). The traditions' irreducible articulations
(karma theory, liberation goals) are preserved. Karma's moral accounting
lies outside scope. Liberation is re-described only as far as
`ETTMTTP-2:24` goes: awakening as the golden age. The corpus supplies no
account of nirvana's metaphysics, of moksha, or of Jain and Sikh
liberation.

## Revision 0.2.0 (2026-11-08): brought into line with ETTMTTP-2:24

Version 0.1.0 described the passage as rendering the cycle as
"recreation from preserved records rather than metaphysical
transmigration". It also excluded liberation re-description "which the
canon does not perform". Both were at variance with `ETTMTTP-2:24`. That
passage applies the cycle to humanity entire and re-describes awakening
as the golden age of civilizations. Its derivative `wiki/samsara.md`
already followed the canon.

The revision, approved by the founder on 2026-11-08, makes four changes:

- it restates the proposition at humanity scale;
- it adds the awakening re-description to scope;
- it replaces the contradicting exclusion with a bounded one;
- it keeps individual scientific reincarnation only as a separately cited
  layer (`ETTMTTP-3:208`, `ETTMTTP-3:214`).

`woh-claim-0031` is added as a dependency, and one alternative is added
(subject substitution). Lifecycle and label are unchanged. The record's
title still says "recreation" and is retained for identifier continuity.
It now names the separately cited individual layer, not the Buddhism
passage.
