# Elohim-civilization hypothesis

## Exact proposition

Wheel of Heaven proposes that the beings it identifies as the Elohim were a
technologically advanced biological civilization from elsewhere and were
responsible for designing terrestrial life, including humanity.

This proposition is the project's foundational interpretive premise. It is
recorded as a `hypothesis`, has framework relation `foundational`, and retains
the reader-facing label `framework`.

## Claim decomposition

The proposition depends on several claims that must not be silently merged:

1. **Source report:** *The Book Which Tells the Truth* presents its speakers as
   people like terrestrial humans who live on another planet.
2. **Source report:** the same source identifies its creators as scientists and
   reports the artificial creation of terrestrial life.
3. **Interpretation:** Wheel identifies those reported beings with the referent
   behind selected biblical uses of `Elohim`.
4. **Hypothesis:** the source report and interpretive identification describe
   historical and biological reality rather than revelation, fiction,
   allegory, error, or another explanatory category.

Parts 1–2 — the report layer — are now recorded separately as
[`woh-claim-0070`, the canonical contact report](canonical-contact-report.md),
on which this claim depends. The partial split was adopted by
[RFC 0006](../../rfcs/0006-split-canonical-contact-report.md) and
[ADR 0007](../../decisions/0007-split-canonical-contact-report.md); this
record retains the interpretive identification (3) and the hypothesis (4) as
one bundle. The full four-way decomposition is deferred until a review of
the identification or design-of-life subclaims actually advances toward
`reviewed`.

## Current project role

The proposition is foundational in the existing public corpus: it determines
how Wheel reads creation accounts, named figures, chronology, interventions,
and comparative traditions. `Foundational` describes project dependency. It
does not mean that the proposition is established by mainstream history,
archaeology, biology, astronomy, or religious studies.

## Current basis

### Canonical source report

The report layer this hypothesis stands on is carried by
[`woh-claim-0070`](canonical-contact-report.md):

- `TBWTT-1:53`, reporting that the speakers are people like humans and live on
  a similar planet;
- `TBWTT-2:5`, reporting the source's gloss of `Elohim`, the search for a
  suitable planet, and the artificial creation of life.

These locators establish what the project source reports. They do not
independently establish that its report corresponds to historical events —
that hypothesis step is exactly what this record, not `woh-claim-0070`,
asserts and must defend.

### Context and analogy

Other texts, philological observations, scientific proposals, and comparative
traditions may supply context, analogies, or challenges. Each requires its own
typed claim. In particular:

- the morphological plurality of Hebrew `Elohim` does not by itself identify
  the referent as extraterrestrial;
- the existence of directed-panspermia proposals does not establish that
  directed creation occurred on Earth;
- technological progress toward genetic engineering does not validate a
  specific ancient account;
- repetition of the central proposition by later authors is not independent
  confirmation when their work depends on earlier alternative-history sources.

## Alternatives and challenges

Relevant alternative categories include:

- theological readings of `Elohim` as a designation of deity;
- historical-critical accounts of Israelite religion and divine-council
  language;
- literary, revelatory, psychological, sociological, fictional, or mistaken
  accounts of the Raëlian source report;
- naturalistic accounts of the origin and development of terrestrial life;
- non-Raëlian embodied-deity interpretations that do not imply the full Wheel
  proposition.

The pilot has identified these categories but has not systematically reviewed
them.

## Evidence status

`scoped` and `contested`.

Anchor sources and dispute families are known, but no reproducible systematic
review has been completed. The project must not use `reviewed`, `replicated`, or
`validated` for this proposition on the strength of the current corpus.

A passage-level evidence map decomposing the proposition into subclaims A–D
(canonical report, biblical identification, off-world origin, design of life),
with source-language observations and competing readings, is recorded in
[Evidence map: Elohim-civilization hypothesis](../evidence/woh-claim-0001-evidence.md).

## Scope and exclusions

This claim does not by itself establish:

- the reliability of every Raëlian source statement;
- the project's precessional chronology;
- one-to-one mappings among biblical, Mesopotamian, or other named figures;
- the Anunnaki–Elohim identity claim;
- the motives, political structure, population, lifespan, or present location
  of the proposed civilization;
- any obligation to believe, worship, join, or act.

Those require separate claims or are outside the research core.

## Revision triggers

Revise or split this specification when:

- the canonical contact report record (`woh-claim-0070`) materially changes;
- the `Elohim` identification changes independently of the civilization claim;
- a reproducible review materially changes the state of relevant historical,
  philological, biological, or source-critical evidence;
- a review of the biblical-identification or design-of-life subclaims
  advances toward `reviewed`, triggering the full four-way decomposition
  deferred by ADR 0007;
- a public derivative strengthens or narrows the claim beyond this record.
