# The Flood as deliberate reset

## Exact proposition

The ancient flood narratives share a decision *procedure* rather than a
divine mood — assembly resolution, oath, internal dissent, a warned survivor
under precise instructions to preserve the seed of living things — and the
canon reads the event as a deliberate reset of the antediluvian project,
with the genetic stock preserved aboard a craft.

This is an `interpretation` with framework relation `derived` and public
label `framework`.

## Provenance

Distilled at founder direction from the published Explainer *The Flood Was
a Reset, Not a Punishment* and `wiki/great-flood.md` (39 in-corpus links).
The article's own label is `inferred` because its main textual claim — the
shared procedure — is a reasonable reading of the sources; this record
carries the corpus *position*, whose canonical layer (the reset, the
cell-line, the staged craft) is framework. The two labels are consistent
because they label different claims.

## Two layers, kept distinct

1. **The procedural reading (inferred):** across Genesis 6–9, Atrahasis,
   Gilgamesh XI, the Sumerian Flood Story, and the Book of the Watchers,
   the event is decided in assembly, sworn, opposed by a dissenter — the
   Atrahasis "wrong instrument" argument names the targeted alternatives
   that should have been used — and executed with seed-preservation
   instructions. This is close reading of texts the project largely holds
   in its own translations.
2. **The reset reading (framework):** the canon's account (TBWTT ch. 2)
   of a deliberate decision within the Council's governance, the cell-line
   preserved aboard a staged craft, the remnant re-established — operating
   within the conflict recorded as `woh-claim-0008`.

The record deliberately does not assert geological historicity, does not
extend the procedure to all two-hundred-plus worldwide flood traditions
(the flood-myths dataset's expansion is the test bed for how far the
procedure actually reaches), and does not erase the fact that the Genesis
redaction itself moralizes the event the canon de-moralizes.

## Alternatives and challenges

The literary-dependence account is the strongest: Atrahasis → Gilgamesh XI
→ Genesis is the standard documented stream, and a common source text
explains the shared procedure with no shared event. Judgment-theology,
independent local floods, and the canon-retrofit objection complete the
set.
