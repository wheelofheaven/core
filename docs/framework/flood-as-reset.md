# The Flood as a contested decision

## Exact proposition

In the canon, the Elohim home world decides to destroy all life on Earth.
The exiled creators, Lucifer's group, act against that decision and
preserve Noah, a few humans and a living cell of each species aboard a
craft in orbit. After the event the Council resolves never again to
destroy humanity and helps re-implant the preserved life. The reset is
the outcome of a decision and its circumvention, not the content of the
decision.

The ancient flood narratives preserve parts of this arrangement unevenly.
The Sumerian Flood Story, Atrahasis and Gilgamesh XI keep an assembly
decision, an oath and a god who saves the survivor around the decree.
Explicit objection before the flood survives only in Atrahasis; the list
of targeted alternatives survives only in Gilgamesh XI, after the flood.
Genesis 6–9 and the Book of the Watchers put destruction and rescue under
one authority.

This is an `interpretation` with framework relation `derived` and public
label `framework`.

## Provenance

Distilled at founder direction from the published Explainer *The Flood Was

0.2.0 by [ADR 0015](../../decisions/0015-flood-claim-0010-revision.md)

and a re-reading of the canon's Flood passages
([proposal](../research/flood-concordance/claim-0010-revision-proposal.md)).

0.1.0 read the five texts as sharing one sequence (assembly, oath,
irrevocability, dissent, seed-preservation instructions) and read the
canon as a "deliberate reset decision within the Council's governance".
Neither held. No single text attests the full sequence, and in the canon
the Council's decision is destruction, not reset.

## Two layers, kept distinct

1. **The canonical layer (framework).** Two accounts, in different voices:
   - *The Book Which Tells the Truth* 2:55–68 (Yahweh's dictation): the
     distant planet decides "to suppress their creation" because
     scientific progress on Earth had become dangerous (2:55–57), "to
     destroy all life on Earth by sending nuclear missiles" (2:58). The
     exiles, "forewarned of the matter", have Noah build a rocket that
     orbits during the cataclysm; the pairs are "an image" for a living
     cell of each species, male and female (2:58). The three-stage craft
     carries a couple of each human race (2:62). Afterwards the creators
     promise never to destroy humanity (2:66); races and animals are
     restored (2:68). The people of the planet are afraid on learning
     that life survived (2:74).
   - *Let's Welcome the Extraterrestrials* 2:25–28 (the "New
     Revelations", Raël's narration of the 1975 account): Satan brings
     evidence of human violence before the Council of the Eternals, and
     Yahweh decides total destruction (2:25–26). Lucifer's group helps
     Noah build the orbiting craft with "a few men and women and the
     genetic codes of some animal species" (2:27). The Council then
     decides never again to destroy humanity and helps re-implant the
     preserved life; Satan bows to the majority behind Yahweh (2:28).

   The two accounts state the motive differently: the danger of human
   progress, and the danger of human violence. Both are danger, not moral
   retribution. The record registers both and does not merge them. The
   layer sits inside the conflict recorded as `woh-claim-0008`.

2. **The textual layer (inferred).** The passage-specific distribution of
   features across the five named texts, as tabulated in the procedural
   comparison and summarized in the
   [evidence map](../evidence/woh-claim-0010-evidence.md). The
   lion/wolf/famine/Erra alternatives are cited to Gilgamesh XI
   180–197. The project's Atrahasis text prints lion and famine
   alternatives at ATRA-WOH-III:vi.21–24, but those lines are not aligned
   with a named edition and are not cited for the list.

ADR 0015 considered and did not adopt a further comparative observation:
that the Mesopotamian separation of destroying authority and rescuing god
corresponds to the canon's separation of the Council and the exiles.

## Exclusions

- The geological reading (continental breakup, swept surface, burial
  strata), which `woh-claim-0071` carries.
- Extending the arrangement to all two-hundred-plus worldwide flood
  traditions.
- Reading the Flood as punishment of wickedness simpliciter, without
  erasing that the Genesis redaction itself moralizes the event.
- Presenting the reset as the content of the Council's decision.
- Treating the five texts as five independent confirmations.
- Attributing the alternatives list to Atrahasis until the alignment is
  resolved.
- Reading the post-flood promise (Genesis 8:21–22, 9:8–17; TBWTT 2:66;
  LWTE 2:28) as an oath that bound the original decision.

## Alternatives and challenges

The literary-dependence account is the strongest for the textual layer:
Atrahasis → Gilgamesh XI → Genesis is the documented stream, and a common
source explains shared features with no shared event. Judgment theology
and independent local floods complete the textual alternatives. Against
the canonical layer, the canon-retrofit objection holds that the canon's
two-faction account is modelled on the Mesopotamian assembly narratives
and on Genesis, so that the resemblance is reception, not memory.
