# The Menorah as base-network memory

## Exact proposition

The seven-branched Menorah read as preserving the operational memory
of the creators' terrestrial base network: in the canon's account,
the original referent was a headquarters console bearing seven
indicator lights keeping contact with the other bases and with the
craft in orbit — telemetry, not liturgy, at the origin — surviving as
the lampstand of Exodus 25 through Temple Judaism to the modern
emblem, with Zechariah 4's seven lamps ('the eyes of Yahweh ranging
over the whole earth') registered as the biblical layer closest to a
monitoring-network description.

An `interpretation`, `derived`, `framework`.

## The restraints

No geography: the record asserts the canon's seven and identifies no
site — extending the network claim beyond the canon's statement is a
named revision trigger. No annexation of the corpus's own emblem,
which is `woh-claim-0043`'s territory. And no displacement of the
scholarship: Exodus 25's own vocabulary is botanical — branches,
calyxes, almond blossoms — and the ancient Near Eastern stylized-tree
iconography accounts for the form without remainder on the mainstream
reading, which is carried against this record in full, alongside the
number-mysticism objection: seven is the ancient world's most
overloaded number, and a seven-light console read from a
seven-branched lamp is exactly the pattern-match the corpus's
instrument records forbid elsewhere. The symbol's attested history
(Exodus specification, Temple furnishing, state emblem) is carried at
documentary grade.
