# The bounded prophetology and its census discipline

## Exact proposition

The canon asserts roughly forty seated representatives of the religions
created following the contacts — and supplies no list — while warning in
its own voice against false prophets. The corpus reads the pair as a
methodological obligation: a criteria-governed, revisable census of who
is seated, every seating a defensible editorial decision.

An `interpretation`, `derived` (riding 0038 and 0052), `inferred` —
matching the Forty Chairs Explainer it pre-grounds (the article binds on
publication).

## The two hard edges

No authoritative roster is ever published — the census is a maintained
instrument. And living claimants are not adjudicated: the false-prophet
warning makes that the corpus's most sensitive editorial ground, and the
record fences it rather than entering it. The criteria-circularity
objection (canon-derived criteria seat whom the canon favors) is the
standing critique.
