# The Council–Serpentine conflict

## Exact proposition

A multi-age political-military conflict took place between the Elohim
home-world Council and the exiled-creator-and-human alliance on Earth:
initiated by the Eden disclosure and the exile of the disclosing faction
(late Age of Cancer), developing through the antediluvian period and the
Flood, reaching open military expression in the late Age of Gemini, and
resolving in the negotiated pardon of the original exiles in the early Age
of Taurus. The corpus further reads the worldwide theomachy pattern as
common cultural memory of this single event.

This is an `interpretation` with framework relation `derived` and public
label `framework`.

## Two layers, kept distinct

1. **The narrative arc** is canon-articulated: the Eden disclosure, the
   exile, the unions, the Flood, Babel, the open-military phase, the pardon
   — episodes present in *The Book Which Tells the Truth* and developed in
   the timeline corpus. The strongest internal objection is recorded as an
   alternative: the canon articulates *episodes*, and the single continuous
   multi-age war connecting them is corpus systematization.
2. **The cross-cultural extension** is corpus development: the theomachy
   pattern (Titanomachy, Aesir–Vanir, deva–asura, Marduk–Tiamat, Horus–Set,
   and cognates) read as common memory carried by lineages separated after
   the event. The Greek instance is scoped separately as `woh-claim-0004`;
   this record generalizes the referent, not the per-tradition readings.

The participants are registered as the corpus registers them — Yahweh and
Satan on the Council side, Lucifer and the exiled Eloha scientists with
their human allies on the Serpentine side — with the Lucifer–Satan
*distinction* explicitly inside scope and their later biblical fusion
excluded as a separate candidate claim.

## Alternatives and challenges

Independent mythogenesis; documented diffusion (which, where routes exist,
is conceded exactly as in claims 0004/0005); the chaoskampf-theology account
of the Hebrew material (Gunkel, Day); the transmission-fidelity limit; and
the internal narrative-gap objection above. All are at least as economical
until the reading predicts a particular they do not.
