# The Year One unsealing reading

## Exact proposition

The Dead Sea Scrolls came to light in the winter of 1946/47 — the first
year of the era the canon counts from the 1945/1946 threshold — and the
corpus reads the timing as an instance of the Apocalypse-as-unveiling:
the oldest biblical manuscripts surfacing in the opening year of the
period designated for the uncovering of what was concealed.

An `interpretation` with framework relation `derived` (from the
unveiling-period claim, `woh-claim-0015`) and public label `speculative`.

## Provenance

Distilled at founder direction from the published Scrolls/Unsealed
Explainer pair.

## Where the weight sits, honestly

The documentary layer — the cobbler, the archbishop, the authentication,
the dating — is carried at full strength and is not in dispute. The
calendrical fact is arithmetic. Everything distinctive sits in one
interpretive step over one coincidence, and the record grades that step
`speculative` without blinking: the selection-effect objection (a century
of Near Eastern archaeology produces discoveries every year) and the
retrofit objection (the era was announced decades later, so the
alignment could not have failed) are the default accounts and are carried
as such. The record asserts a *reading of a timing*, never an
intervention — and date-matching further discoveries into a pattern is
excluded; any candidate instance needs its own record or none.

## Alternatives and challenges

Selection effect; era retrofit; date-fuzziness on both sides of the
alignment.
