# Lucifer and Satan as distinct figures

## Exact proposition

Lucifer and Satan are distinct. Biblically, "Lucifer" renders *helel ben
shahar* — a taunt against the king of Babylon in Isaiah 14 with no Devil in
the passage — while *ha-satan* in Job and Zechariah is a courtroom role in
the divine council; their fusion into one Devil is a datable development
from the Second Temple period through the Christian centuries, sealed by
Milton. In the canon the two are distinct individuals on opposite sides of
the conflict: Lucifer the exiled-creator faction leader, Satan the
home-world opposition leader.

An `interpretation` with framework relation `comparative` and public label
`framework`.

## Two layers, kept distinct

1. **The biblical layer (mainstream-supportable):** the Isaiah taunt, the
   court functionary, and the reception-history of the fusion — Pagels,
   Forsyth, and the adversary-in-heaven scholarship carry this account, and
   a critical scholar could accept it entire.
2. **The canonical layer (framework):** two named individuals, opposed
   factions, neither a devil — Lucifer condemned for the disclosure, Satan
   distrustful of humanity and commissioning the testing of Job. The
   mapping of the biblical pair onto the canonical pair is supplied by the
   framework and recorded as this claim's live internal objection.

## Alternatives and challenges

Fusion-as-insight; the neither-refers literary reading; and the
canon-mapping objection. The second is the sharpest: if both biblical
figures are genre artifacts, the canonical mapping has nothing textual to
attach to and rests on the canon alone.
