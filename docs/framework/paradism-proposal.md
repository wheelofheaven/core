# Paradism as the canon's proposed economy

## Exact proposition

The canon proposes a post-scarcity, fully automated, moneyless society —
later systematized in the movement's vocabulary as paradism — modeled on
the Elohim's home-world economy: machines perform necessary labor; human
beings are freed for thought, creation, and fulfillment. This record
carries the proposal as canon-explicit normative content, with the
incentive, transition, and feasibility objections at full strength.

A `normative_recommendation` with framework relation `derived` and
public label `framework` — the second of the normative pair with
`woh-claim-0032`, under the same discipline: recorded, not endorsed.

## The vocabulary note

"Paradism" as a term is the movement's later systematization; the
*content* is canon-stated (the home-world economy witnessed in the
second message). The record keeps the term's history distinct from the
proposal's, so that citing the canon and citing the campaign remain
different acts. Family resemblance to terrestrial post-work thought is
context and is not collapsed into identity; the borrowed-utopia
objection (the 1970s automation imaginary showing through the home-world
details) is carried in the critical layer.
