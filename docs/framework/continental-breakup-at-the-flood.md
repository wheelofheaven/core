# Continental breakup at the Flood

**Draft.** Drafted at founder direction on 2026-09-12 after a published
Explainer hedged this consequence out of existence; awaiting founder review
before promotion. Nothing below has been reviewed.

## Exact proposition

Per the canon, the single continent the Elohim raised from the seabed was
broken by the home-planet strike the canon identifies as the Flood. The
blasts sent its fragments "drifting outwards from the centre of the shock",
swept the whole land surface, and buried the living matter — forests,
animals, humans — "immediately and all together" in a ring around the
impact. The corpus reads today's continental configuration and its ongoing
drift as the residue of that displacement, and the fossil-bearing strata as
the burial sequence of what died when in the aftermath of the initial
explosion. It accepts the planet's own deep age, and it registers in its own
voice that mainstream plate tectonics and radiometric dating place the
Pangaean breakup roughly 195 million years ago.

This is an `interpretation` with framework relation `derived` and public
label `framework`.

## Provenance

The consequence was already stated across the corpus — `wiki/pangaea.md`,
`wiki/great-flood.md`, the Age of Gemini timeline chapter — but no record
carried it: `woh-claim-0010` (the Flood as deliberate reset) excludes
"historicity or dating of a global flood as a geological claim" by design.
The gap surfaced when *The Signature and the Designers* (published
2026-09-01) described the canon's Flood as "a reset and not a rewriting of
the rocks"; the founder corrected the article on 2026-09-10 and directed
that the consequence get its own record. This specification is the
reverse-pilot extraction of what the wiki entries and the corrected article
already say.

## What the canon says, and where

Two layers of source, kept distinct:

1. **The message text** (*The Book Which Tells the Truth*, ch. 2). ¶14:
   the creators studied the seabed and, "by means of fairly powerful
   explosions", gathered matter "in the same place, which formed a
   continent"; "originally there was only one continent on Earth", and
   "all the continents which drifted apart fit together perfectly". ¶58:
   the home planet decided "to destroy all life on Earth by sending nuclear
   missiles". The message text itself does not say the strike broke the
   continent; it establishes the single continent, the later drift, and the
   nuclear strike.
2. **The commentaries printed with the third message** (*Let's Welcome the
   Extraterrestrials*, ch. 4, §2 "A New Hypothesis for the History of
   Humanity"). ¶35 supplies the breakup and the burial in full; ¶37 reads
   the petroleum ring as the burial's signature; ¶39 and ¶41 follow the
   fragments (the Indian subcontinent and the Himalayas, Antarctica); ¶¶43–44
   describe the upheaval and the survivors' return to an unrecognizable
   continent.

The corpus treats both as canon. The record's third alternative keeps the
distinction visible, because the canonical *weight* of the breakup passage
depends on whose text ch. 4 is, and that has not been source-critically
settled.

## What the corpus adds

- Today's continents are the fragments, still moving; the corpus reads
  measured plate motion as the slowing tail of the original displacement.
- The fossil beds are the burial. Their sequence is read as the record of
  what died when in the aftermath of the initial explosion — not as deep-time
  deposition. The dinosaurs entry carries the corpus's compressed timeline
  for the large fauna on the same reading.
- Dating comes from the lattice, not the source: raised in Sagittarius
  (`woh-claim-0059`), broken at the opening of Gemini, c. 6,690 BCE
  (`woh-claim-0010`, `woh-claim-0019`).

## The boundary with young-earth Flood geology

On one point the corpus and the young-earth reading say the same thing: the
fossil-bearing strata are the signature of one catastrophe. Everything
around that point differs — the corpus keeps a planet roughly 4.5 billion
years old, a recent and deliberate strike rather than a year of rain, and
creators rather than a deity. Prose should state the convergence and the
difference together; the corrected Explainer is the model.

## Alternatives and challenges

Mainstream plate tectonics (steady drift over ~195 million years, with
seafloor-spreading, paleomagnetic, radiometric and GPS evidence) and deep-time
biostratigraphy are the strong alternatives, and the corpus has no mechanism
that reconciles radiometric ages with its timeline — the record says so
rather than supplying one. The commentary-not-message objection and the
Flood-geology-inheritance objection complete the set.
