# Continental breakup at the Flood

Drafted at founder direction on 2026-09-12 after a published Explainer
hedged this consequence out of existence, and promoted to `accepted` the
same day by founder ruling
([ADR 0010](../../decisions/0010-continental-breakup-promotion.md)).
Revised to 0.2.0 and then 0.3.0 on 2026-09-15 on the findings of
[review 0001](../reviews/woh-claim-0071-review-0001.md), which settled
whose text the breakup passage is, verified its one external citation, and
read the scientific and critical alternatives against their sources.

## Exact proposition

Per the canon, the single continent the Elohim raised from the seabed was
broken by the home-planet strike the canon identifies as the Flood. The
blasts sent its fragments "drifting outwards from the centre of the shock",
swept the whole land surface, and buried the living matter — forests,
animals, humans — "immediately and all together" in a ring around the
impact. The corpus reads today's continental configuration and its ongoing
drift as the residue of that displacement, and the fossil-bearing strata as
the burial sequence of what died when in the aftermath of the initial
explosion. It accepts the planet's own deep age, and it registers in its own
voice that mainstream plate tectonics and radiometric dating place the
Pangaean breakup roughly 195 million years ago.

This is an `interpretation` with framework relation `derived` and public
label `framework`.

## Provenance

The consequence was already stated across the corpus — `wiki/pangaea.md`,
`wiki/great-flood.md`, the Age of Gemini timeline chapter — but no record
carried it: `woh-claim-0010` (the Flood as deliberate reset) excludes
"historicity or dating of a global flood as a geological claim" by design.
The gap surfaced when *The Signature and the Designers* (published
2026-09-01) described the canon's Flood as "a reset and not a rewriting of
the rocks"; the founder corrected the article on 2026-09-10 and directed
that the consequence get its own record. This specification is the
reverse-pilot extraction of what the wiki entries and the corrected article
already say.

## What the canon says, and where

Two tiers of source, of different standing, kept distinct:

1. **The message text** (*The Book Which Tells the Truth*, ch. 2). ¶14:
   the creators studied the seabed and, "by means of fairly powerful
   explosions", gathered matter "in the same place, which formed a
   continent"; "originally there was only one continent on Earth", and
   "all the continents which drifted apart fit together perfectly". ¶28
   places the Israel team "on the single continent". ¶58: the home planet
   decided "to destroy all life on Earth by sending nuclear missiles". The
   message text does not say the strike broke the continent; it
   establishes the single continent, the later drift, and the strike.
2. **A signed essay printed as chapter 4 of the third book.** Marcel
   Terrusse, chemical engineer and Raëlian guide, *Raelism Through the
   Eyes of Science*, §2 "A New Hypothesis for the History of Humanity"
   (*Let's Welcome the Extraterrestrials*; 2005 English combined edition
   pp. 330–332; 1979 French text at `LWTE-4:26–46`, French column). This
   alone supplies the breakup, the fragments "drifting outwards from the
   centre of the shock", the swept surface, the burial "in a ring shape",
   the petroleum ring as the burial's signature, the upheaval, and the
   survivors' return. The French carries two sentences the English
   rendering omits — the plates' brutal separation and the *"vitesse
   résiduelle"* of present-day drift (`n=40–41`) — which is where the
   corpus's residual-momentum reading comes from. The 2005 French combined
   edition of the messages omits the chapter altogether.

The corpus has treated the whole volume as canon. Whether a guide's essay
printed with the message carries canonical weight is a stance question
the record now carries as its first revision trigger, for the founder to
rule on; the record's third alternative states the case that it does not.

## The citation

The essay grounds its petroleum ring in a study it attributes to "Professor
Nehring" at "the Hudson Institute", commissioned by the CIA. The study is
real: Richard Nehring, *Giant Oil Fields and World Oil Resources*,
R-2284-CIA, The Rand Corporation, June 1978, prepared for the Central
Intelligence Agency. Its p. 39 and Fig. 3.1 describe a "ring of oil" — an
oval band roughly 800 to 1,000 miles wide holding nearly 85% of known
petroleum resources — plotted on Dietz and Holden's reconstruction of the
continental plates at the end of the Triassic, 180 million years ago, and
Nehring leaves it as "a most interesting problem for geologic speculation".
Terrusse reports the observation faithfully and misnames the institution
(RAND, not Hudson) and the author's title; the printed English adds
"tertiary" for the French and Nehring's "Triassic". The ring is Nehring's;
the reading of it as the burial rim of a recent strike is Terrusse's. The
datum therefore supports the existence of the pattern, on a deep-time
reconstruction, and not the recent strike by itself.

## What the corpus adds

- Today's continents are the fragments, still moving; the corpus reads
  measured plate motion as the slowing tail of the original displacement
  (Terrusse, `LWTE-4:40–41`, French).
- The fossil beds are the burial. Their sequence is read as the record of
  what died when in the aftermath of the initial explosion — not as deep-time
  deposition. The dinosaurs entry carries the corpus's compressed timeline
  for the large fauna on the same reading.
- Dating comes from the lattice, not the source: raised in Sagittarius
  (`woh-claim-0059`), broken at the opening of Gemini, c. 6,690 BCE
  (`woh-claim-0010`, `woh-claim-0019`).

## The boundary with young-earth Flood geology

On one point the corpus and the young-earth reading say the same thing: the
fossil-bearing strata are the signature of one catastrophe. Everything
around that point differs — the corpus keeps a planet roughly 4.5 billion
years old, a recent and deliberate strike rather than a year of rain, and
creators rather than a deity. Prose should state the convergence and the
difference together; the corrected Explainer is the model.

## Alternatives and challenges

Mainstream plate tectonics (staged breakup from the Triassic, c. 200
million years ago; present-day GPS rates agreeing with rates averaged over
the past 0.78–3.16 million years to a few millimetres per year) and
deep-time stratigraphy (the world's petroleum from six source-rock
intervals spread across a third of Phanerozoic time; the giants clustered
in the passive margins and rifts the breakup itself produced) are the
strong alternatives, now read against their sources by review 0001, and
the corpus has no mechanism that reconciles radiometric ages with its
timeline — the record says so rather than supplying one. The commentary-not-message objection is now a
statement of fact about authorship joined to an open question about
weight; the Flood-geology-inheritance objection completes the set.
