# The merkavah as craft observation

## Exact proposition

Ezekiel 1 read as a close eyewitness description of an Elohim craft
and its party — the canon's own treatment names it the most
interesting craft description in scripture — with the mobile *kavod*
of Ezekiel 8-11 and 43 read as one physical object's departures and
returns, and the prophet's *ruach* transports as craft-borne
conveyance. The most operationally detailed theophany in the Hebrew
Bible, read operationally.

An `interpretation`, `derived`, `framework`.

## The reading and its restraints

Three restraints keep the record honest. No engineering
reconstruction: Blumrich's *Spaceships of Ezekiel* is registered as an
independent precedent and a cautionary case (his reconstruction
required selective handling of the text), and the corpus endorses no
schematic. No annexation of the temple vision: Ezekiel 40-48 and its
Embassy connection belong to `woh-claim-0016`. No displacement of the
scholarship: Greenberg's account of the vision as prophetic theophany
built from Mesopotamian iconographic vocabulary is carried against
the record in full, alongside the selective-literalism objection —
that the craft reading keeps the wheels but drops the four faces and
the enthroned likeness. The merkavah-mystical reception (Hekhalot)
is registered as reception history, its theology preserved to the
tradition.
