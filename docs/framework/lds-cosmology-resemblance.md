# LDS cosmology and the canon: resemblance

## Exact proposition

Scripture produced by Joseph Smith contains four relevant features:

- an embodied God: "The Father has a body of flesh and bones as tangible
  as man's" (D&C 130:22);
- plural creators: "they, that is the Gods, organized and formed the
  heavens and the earth" (Abraham 4:1);
- a plurality of created and inhabited worlds (D&C 76:24);
- a named governing star near God's throne, Kolob (Abraham 3:2–3).

These resemble the canon's mortal, embodied, plural creators
(`TBWTT-1:53`), its created-creators chain (`woh-claim-0030`), and its
plurality of created humanities (`woh-claim-0047`).

An `interpretation`, `comparative`, `inferred`.

## Resemblance only

Following core rule 7, resemblance, dependence, typology and identity are
different claims. This record makes only the first. Its exclusions:

- no dependence in either direction;
- no identity (founder direction, 2026-11-08): Kolob is not the Elohim's
  star, and neither Moroni nor the personages of Smith's accounts are
  Elohim or their emissaries;
- no use as evidence for the authenticity of the LDS founding, or for any
  census tier.

The difference is preserved. LDS theology remains theistic and
soteriological (Godhead, exaltation, salvation), and the canon is
explicitly atheist.

## The objections

1. **Milieu** (`alt-0079-milieu`). Plurality-of-worlds speculation was
   current long before 1830, and `woh-claim-0067` itself registers that
   tradition. This bears directly on any prose that treats the LDS
   cosmology as unexplained by its milieu.
2. **Papyri provenance** (`alt-0079-papyri-provenance`). The surviving
   papyri are identified by Egyptologists as funerary texts. Robert K.
   Ritner's edition (`ritner-joseph-smith-egyptian-papyri`, metadata only)
   is registered as the source for that identification.
3. **Restorationist development** (`alt-0079-restorationist-development`).
   The resemblance may be shared literalism rather than shared content.
