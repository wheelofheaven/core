# Greek theomachy as Council–Serpentine conflict memory

## Exact proposition

Wheel of Heaven proposes that the divine-conflict architecture of the Greek
mythological tradition — the succession of heaven, the Titanomachy and the
Typhoeus combat, the deliberating divine assembly, divine–human interbreeding,
and the punished transfer of civilisational knowledge — preserves, in
mythologically transformed shape, cultural memory of the Council–Serpentine
conflict among the Elohim.

The canonical anchor is `TBWTT-5:54`, which lists Greece among the regions
where the creators maintained bases and characterises Greek mythology as
containing "great testimonies." The anchor is generic: the canon names Greece
and calls its mythology testimony, but does not itself identify the
Titanomachy, the succession myth, or Prometheus with any specific canonical
event. That identification is corpus development, and this record labels it
accordingly.

This is a primary `interpretation` with framework relation `comparative` and
public label `speculative`.

## What is being read, and at what level

The proposition operates at the level of shared structure, not individual
identity. Five structure-families are read as transformed conflict memory:

1. **Succession of heaven** — Ouranos → Kronos → Zeus
   (`THEO-WOH-1:154–182`, `1:453–467`), with the defeated elder generation
   confined below (`1:617–720`): generational transfer of power within the
   divine class, read against the corpus account of generational political
   conflict among the creators.
2. **Storm-god–serpent combat** — the Typhoeus fight (`THEO-WOH-1:820–868`)
   as the Greek instance of the pan-Near-Eastern combat armature, read as the
   Council–Serpentine war remembered in each tradition's political vocabulary.
3. **The divine assembly** — the Odyssey's opening council (Od. 1.26–95)
   beside the Mesopotamian *puḫur ilāni*, the Ugaritic assembly of the sons of
   El, and Psalm 82, read as one deliberative institution seen from different
   provinces of its jurisdiction.
4. **Divine–human interbreeding** — Alcinous's proximity claim
   (Od. 7.201–206), the demigod genealogies, and the Gigantomachy's
   mortal's-help rule, read with the Nephilim material.
5. **The punished knowledge transfer** — Prometheus (`THEO-WOH-1:561–569`)
   as the structural counterpart of the canon's disclosing insider: a member
   of the divine class who moves civilisational knowledge to humanity against
   the ruler's embargo and is punished for it. The correspondence is
   positional (place in the plot), not an identity of name or person.

The record explicitly does not establish: transmission routes (where philology
traces a route, as with Kumarbi to Hesiod, the route explains the resemblance
and is not contested); one-to-one deity–Eloha mappings; the historicity of the
Odyssey's events; any eyewitness status for the Homeric text; or allegorical
decodings of individual monsters.

## The two-level structure of the argument

The claim's characteristic move — and its characteristic risk — is operating
one level below the philology. Mainstream scholarship explains how the
succession myth travelled from Anatolia to Boeotia; the claim proposes what
the travelling story was about before it travelled. These are different
questions. The claim survives only while it respects that division: the moment
public prose uses the claim to contest a documented route, or uses a
documented route as if it supported the memory reading, the record is being
misexpressed. A revision trigger tracks this drift.

## Required inferential steps

For the reading to advance beyond `speculative`, review would need:

1. a criterion separating *transformed memory of a real referent* from
   *typological convergence plus literary transmission* — the same
   discriminating criterion claim `woh-claim-0002` still lacks;
2. a systematic test on arbitrary particulars (unmotivated details of the
   mortal's-help type) across traditions without plausible contact;
3. an account of transmission fidelity over the required span that is
   compatible with the oral-tradition results (Parry–Lord recomposition,
   the floating gap, schema drift) rather than merely unrefuted by them;
4. at least one prediction of a textual or material particular that a
   conventional account does not make.

## Alternatives and challenges

The strongest alternative families (all currently at least as economical):

- documented literary transmission along busy routes explains the parallels;
- common Indo-European inheritance explains them from the other direction;
- the structural null: minds share architecture, so myths converge without
  memory;
- social projection: pantheons mirror palace politics wherever palaces exist;
- the fidelity limit: the demonstrated high-fidelity span of oral transmission
  (roughly four centuries, helmet-to-Homer) is far short of what the corpus
  chronology requires;
- category substitution: the technological reading modernises the myth's idiom
  while preserving its structure, explaining nothing.

The fidelity limit deserves emphasis because it is built from evidence the
public derivative itself assembles: the same oral-tradition science that makes
the tradition a credible carrier of structures makes it a demonstrably lossy
carrier of events, chronology, and causation — and the losses are heaviest
exactly where narrative explanation lives.

## Revision triggers

Revise, split, or retire this specification when:

- the motif inventory proves fully predictable from social structure and
  cognitive bias;
- the reading fails to predict any particular a conventional account does not;
- a systematic arbitrary-particulars test returns negative or proves
  impossible to construct;
- the Council–Serpentine conflict account is recorded, revised, or rejected
  as a core claim;
- claim `woh-claim-0001` or `woh-claim-0003` is revised or retired;
- public prose drifts into route claims, individual mappings, or historicity
  claims this record excludes.
