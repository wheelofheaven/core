# Greece within the Elohimian sphere

## Exact proposition

Greece lay continuously within the sphere of Elohim engagement whose centre
of gravity the biblical corpus records in the Near East. The canon names
Greece among the creator-base regions and characterises its mythology as
testimony (`TBWTT-5:54`), and later reports emissaries sent among the Greeks
(`TBWTT-3:213–215`) — two engagements centuries apart. Greek religious
tradition is therefore read as a broader extension of the same Elohimian
events, not a foreign domain touched once.

This is an `interpretation` with framework relation `derived` and public
label `framework`.

## Provenance

Drafted at founder direction as the grounding for the planned companion
Explainer to *The World Behind the Odyssey* — the forward pilot of the
[artifact derivation contract](../../rfcs/0003-artifact-derivation-contract.md).
The founder's framing controls the emphasis: what matters is that Greece was
embedded in the religious and cultural sphere of the Near East, equally
within the field of Elohim engagement, holding truths in its mythological
writings — the *Theogony* above all — before the emissaries were ever sent.
Not a one-time event; an extension of the biblical theatre.

## The structure of the claim

Three layers, kept distinct:

1. **The documented embedding** (`woh-claim-0005`, contextual, mainstream):
   archaic Greece absorbed Near Eastern literary material along human routes.
   This is scholarship's layer. The framework asserts nothing about the
   routes and proposes no alternative to them.
2. **The engagement layer** (this record): beneath the shared stories lies a
   shared referent — both spheres sat within the same field of Elohim
   activity, which is *why* the canon can call Greek mythology testimony
   rather than merely borrowing.
3. **The specific readings** (`woh-claim-0004`): which Greek structures are
   read as memory of which events is scoped there, not here.

The continuity thesis rests on the two canonical moments being *different in
kind and era*: the base-region characterisation with its testimony clause
refers to the deep engagement (pre-Homeric in reference), while the emissary
episode (`woh-claim-0006`) is classical-era and explicitly cannot explain
archaic material. Two attested engagements of different character, centuries
apart, is what "continuously within the sphere" means at minimum — the canon
does not narrate what lies between them, and this record does not invent it.

## Priority, reframed

The founder's directive reframes the priority question away from race or
language: the Near East's earlier flourishing — Babel, the cities of the
plain, the biblical theatre generally — marks where the engagement was
*concentrated first*, not a ranking of peoples, and not a claim about
Semitic languages. The scope excludes linguistic priority explicitly.
Greece is not derivative; it is a province of the same history with its own
irreducible testimony.

## Required inferential steps

For this reading to advance beyond a framework interpretation, review would
need: (1) the fuller canonical context confirming that 5:54's enumeration
can carry a continuity reading (the selective-canon alternative is the
live internal threat); (2) at least one textual particular in the Greek
corpus better predicted by a shared-referent reading than by transmission
alone — the transmission-suffices alternative is fully adequate to every
parallel currently catalogued; (3) the discriminating criterion this
record shares with `woh-claim-0002` and `woh-claim-0004`, still lacking.

## Alternatives and challenges

Recorded in the machine record at full strength: transmission suffices (the
strongest — it explains everything currently in evidence with no engagement
layer); the selective-canon objection (two brief passages may not carry a
continuity thesis); the independent-tradition position; and category
substitution, which applies with full force when the Elohimian frame is
extended over a second tradition's religion.
