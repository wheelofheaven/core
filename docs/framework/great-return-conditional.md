# The Great Return as conditional arrival

## Exact proposition

The canon anticipates an official arrival of the Elohim — the culminating
event its message is oriented toward — and makes it conditional rather
than predicted: humanity must want the return, must be unified and wise
enough to receive advanced knowledge responsibly, must build the Embassy
on extraterritorial ground, and must guarantee the visitors' safety. The
anticipation is situated in the period the canon calls the Apocalypse —
unveiling, coextensive with the Age of Aquarius, opening at the
mid-twentieth-century threshold `woh-claim-0003` anchors.

## Why conditionality is the load-bearing clause

A dated prophecy fails on a date; a conditional anticipation cannot be
audited that way, and the record refuses to pretend otherwise in either
direction. What the record asserts is the *structure* the canon states:
enumerated human preconditions, an unveiling period, a knowledge transfer
on arrival. What it excludes is everything the structure is most tempted
toward in prose — date-setting, and reading news as confirmation. The
millenarian-genre alternative is carried at full strength: deferred
culminations whose conditions explain non-arrival are a documented
pattern, and the scholarly literature reads this one as an instance.

The Apocalypse sense is fixed against the popular one: unveiling, not
destruction — the same register the corpus's published Scrolls/Unsealed
pair works in.

## Alternatives and challenges

Millenarian genre; mobilization device; period retrofit. The first is the
default scholarly account and is at least as economical.
