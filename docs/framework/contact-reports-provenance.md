# The contact reports as the canon's provenance

## Exact proposition

The canon originates in two reported contact events — December 13, 1973
at Puy-de-Lassolas, producing *The Book Which Tells the Truth*, and
October 7, 1975, producing *Extra-Terrestrials Took Me to Their Planet*.
The reports, as publication history and movement founding, are documented
fact; everything the framework builds stands on these reports **as
reports**.

A `source_report` with framework relation `contextual` and public label
`direct` — the catalog's first record of this kind, and deliberately so.

## Why this record exists

The claim model's first invalid transition is source report → historical
occurrence. This record applies that rule to the framework's own
foundation and makes the application citable: when corpus prose states
the contact dates, the publication sequence, or the founding history, it
cites this record and asserts documented fact; when it asserts the
encounters *happened*, it has left this record entirely and stands on
`woh-claim-0001`'s hypothesis discipline. The one forbidden use — citing
this record as if it established the encounters — is written into the
revision triggers.

The composition-account alternative (the messages as authored literature
with a contact frame) contests the encounters, not the reports; the
record's scope is built so that alternative can be true and the record
still stand. That is the point of the `direct` label: this is the layer
every party to the dispute can accept.
