# Precessional world-age chronology

## Exact proposition

Wheel of Heaven organizes long-range history as a Great Year of twelve equal
world ages of 2,160 years each — an idealization of the astronomical
precession cycle — anchored by project convention so that the Age of Aquarius
spans 1950–4110 CE and the cycle opens with the Age of Capricorn about
21,810 BCE.

This proposition is recorded as a `model`, has framework relation
`foundational`, and retains the reader-facing label `framework`. It is the
third pilot claim, chosen because it mixes an observed astronomical cycle, a
contested historical thesis, a modeling convention, a project anchor, and
derived narrative placement — layers that must not collapse into one another.

## Claim decomposition

The chronology depends on five layers of different epistemic kinds. None may
silently borrow the status of another:

1. **Empirical observation:** Earth's axis precesses. The equinoxes drift
   westward against the fixed stars at roughly 50.3 arcseconds per year, a
   full cycle taking approximately 25,772 years. This is established
   astronomy and would carry the public label `direct`.
2. **Empirical observation (historical):** Hipparchus of Nicaea, in the
   second century BCE, provides the earliest secure description of
   precession. This is likewise mainstream history of astronomy.
3. **Contested interpretation:** the thesis that precessional knowledge
   substantially predates Hipparchus and survives encoded in world mythology
   (de Santillana and von Dechend's *Hamlet's Mill* is the framework's main
   modern source). Mainstream historians of science and folklorists have
   sharply criticized this thesis; it is not established scholarship and this
   record does not treat it as an observation.
4. **Model convention:** dividing the cycle into twelve equal ages of 2,160
   years, named for the zodiacal constellations, with a rounded Great Year of
   25,920 years. No astronomical event individuates the onset of an age: the
   real constellations occupy unequal arcs, their modern boundaries are IAU
   drawing conventions from 1930, and the rounded figure differs from the
   measured period. The equal-twelve division is inherited from astrological
   and sexagesimal tradition and is used by the project as a working
   calendar, not as a measurement.
5. **Framework convention (anchor):** fixing the Age of Aquarius at
   1950–4110 CE, from which all other boundaries follow arithmetically back
   to the Age of Capricorn at circa 21,810 BCE. The anchor is motivated
   canonically, not astronomically: the canonical source reads the
   1945–1948 event cluster as the Aquarian threshold, `TBWTT-5:7` connecting
   the "Fish Gate" transition to a spring-equinox sunrise "in" Aquarius and
   naming 1946 as significant. The corpus rounds the working boundary to
   1950 while recording the canonical threshold reading separately. Other
   systems anchor the Aquarian onset anywhere from the twentieth to the
   twenty-seventh century.

A sixth layer — placing canonical and historical events within the ages, as
the public timeline chapters do — is a family of **derived** claims. It is
excluded from this record and depends on both this model and claim
`woh-claim-0001`.

## Current project role

The chronology is the corpus's organizing scaffold: the public timeline
renders one age per chapter, the project name itself refers to the
precessional wheel, and canonical events are narrated inside age boundaries.
`Foundational` describes that structural dependency. It does not mean that
age boundaries are astronomical facts, and the public corpus already states
the boundary years are framework conventions.

## Current basis

### Canonical source report

The inspected Wheel digitization of *The Book Which Tells the Truth*
includes `TBWTT-5:7`, reporting the "Fish Gate" as "the passage into the new
era of Aquarius," describing the sun rising on the day of the spring equinox
"in" Aquarius, and stating that a 1946 birth "is not by chance." This
establishes what the canonical source reports about the age transition. It
does not establish an astronomical boundary year.

### Astronomical and historical context

Axial precession, its approximate rate, and the Hipparchus attribution
supply real context that the model idealizes. The idealization is visible in
the numbers: twelve ages of 2,160 years total 25,920 years, against a
measured cycle of approximately 25,772 years.

### Comparative and antecedent framing

*Hamlet's Mill* supplies the framework's main modern argument that myth
encodes precession; its scholarly reception is largely negative and is
recorded as such. Jean Sendy's *L'Ère du Verseau* (1970) shows the Aquarian
periodization circulating in the francophone paleo-contact milieu before the
1973 canonical report — relevant to source-dependence questions, since the
canonical threshold reading did not arise in isolation.

## Alternatives and challenges

- Precession was discovered by Hipparchus; mythological "encodings" are
  modern retrojection onto texts that do not require them.
- If ages were defined astronomically at all, constellation boundaries would
  make them unequal and convention-dependent; the equal-twelve division is
  astrological convention.
- No astronomically privileged anchor year exists; competing systems place
  the Aquarian onset centuries apart, so any anchor is stipulated.
- The recurring numbers 72, 2,160, and 25,920 arise from Babylonian
  sexagesimal arithmetic and later astrological systematization rather than
  from a preserved ancient precessional science.

These alternatives challenge layers 3–5 in different ways; none of them
challenges layer 1 or 2.

## Evidence status

`scoped` and `contested`.

The astronomical layer is mainstream and needs no project review. The
ancient-awareness layer is contested and unreviewed. The convention layers
are not empirical claims and can only be assessed for internal consistency
and fidelity to the canonical source. No layer may inherit the astronomical
layer's standing.

## Scope and exclusions

This claim does not by itself establish:

- the placement of any specific event, figure, or intervention within an age;
- that ancient cultures possessed or encoded precessional knowledge;
- that age boundaries correspond to astronomical events;
- astral influence of any kind — the framework reads the zodiac as a
  calendar, not as causation;
- the canonical calendar's year-one (1946) as anything other than a source
  report;
- claim `woh-claim-0001` or any comparative identity claim.

## Revision triggers

Revise or split this specification when:

- the corpus changes its anchor year or age spans;
- a reproducible review materially changes the assessment of pre-Hipparchan
  precessional knowledge;
- a public derivative asserts boundary years as astronomical facts rather
  than conventions;
- event-placement claims need their own versioned records;
- the five-layer decomposition proves too compound for review, in which case
  the layers become independently versioned claims with a mapping from this
  record.
