# The canon's self-description as religion of religions

## Exact proposition

The canon presents itself as an atheist, non-supernatural religion and as
the demystified common source-tradition of existing religions — the
religion of religions — naming Buddhism, Islam, Mormonism, and others as
traditions testifying in their own ways to the same creators. This record
carries the self-description as explicit source content; the incompatible
external classifications (four governments, four headings; the scholars'
stacked labels) are registered as reception history.

A `source_report` with framework relation `contextual` and public label
`direct` — the catalog's second source-report record, paired with
`woh-claim-0020`.

## The citation function

When corpus prose calls Raëlism an atheist religion, or the religion of
religions, it is quoting the source's self-understanding — and this
record is the citation, so the assertion is `direct` (the source
verifiably says this) rather than a category claim. Whether the
self-description is *adequate* as a scholarly category is the Religion of
Religions Explainer's own argument at its own `inferred` grade, outside
this record; and whether the enumerated traditions would accept the
source's account of them belongs to the comparative claims that actually
do that work (0007, 0011, 0021, 0024). The one forbidden use — citing
this record for the category's adequacy — is written into the triggers.

## Alternatives and challenges

Category instability (the classifications' incompatibility as evidence
the self-description stabilizes nothing); legitimation strategy (the
seniority claim as standard new-movement rhetoric).
