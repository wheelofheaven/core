# The canon as serial dictation

## Exact proposition

Read as a textual corpus, the canon is a serial dictation: discrete
reported sessions (six mornings, one October night), three books across
six years, a consistent principal speaker, staged disclosure, later
books retrospectively framing earlier ones — and a reporter who retained
editorial control of his own revelation throughout. The profile is
characterizable by discourse analysis independently of the encounters'
historicity.

An `interpretation` with framework relation `contextual` and public
label `inferred`.

## Provenance

Distilled at founder direction from the draft Explainer *Six Mornings,
Six Years* — pre-grounded ahead of publication, like `woh-claim-0034`.
It extends the provenance record (`woh-claim-0020`) from *that the
reports exist* to *what shape the reported corpus has*.

## The independence clause

Every observation in this record survives both accounts of the corpus's
origin — reception and authorship. That is its design: it gives the
project's own source discipline a textual-critical footing (the tools
applied to other dictation traditions, Weber's routinization frame for
the charismatic-to-textual transition) without smuggling in an
authenticity verdict in either direction. Using this record to
adjudicate authenticity is its one forbidden use.

## Alternatives and challenges

Profile-underdetermines (fits any single-author serial publication);
genre-expectation (revelation genres stage disclosure as a rule).
