# The Embassy as the Third Temple

## Exact proposition

The canon directs the construction of an embassy — a designated structure
on land granted extraterritorial status — to receive the official arrival
and host the inaugural meetings, with specifications from the 1973 contact
elaborated later, a stated first preference for Israel near Jerusalem, and
an explicit identification of the embassy with the Third Temple of Jewish
expectation. The corpus records that identification as canon-explicit and
mainstream-unendorsed.

An `interpretation` with framework relation `derived` and public label
`framework`.

## The comparative discipline the identification requires

The Third Temple identification maps the canon's structure onto a Jewish
expectation that is its own living tradition with its own irreducible
content — halakhic, liturgical, eschatological — which the identification
does not exhaust and must not be presented as exhausting. The scope
excludes supersession outright: the mapping tells the reader what the
*canon* claims, and the temple-appropriation objection (that the mapping
tells us about the canon rather than about the expectation) is carried as
a standing alternative, not softened.

Also excluded: treating host-country decisions as validation or
invalidation, and architectural detail beyond the canon's statements. The
diplomatic-impossibility objection — that extraterritorial status of the
required kind has no realistic path, making the condition unfalsifiable in
practice — is preserved at full strength.

## Alternatives and challenges

Institutional anchor; temple appropriation; diplomatic impossibility.
