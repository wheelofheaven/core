# The emblem as infinity symbol

## Exact proposition

Per the canon, the emblem seen on the craft — the swastika within the
Star of David — signifies the two infinities: the hexagram as "as it is
above, so it is below" (space, the frame of `woh-claim-0023`), the
swastika as the cycle, infinity in time (the layer the
`woh-claim-0014` apparatus reads alongside).

An `interpretation` with framework relation `derived` and public label
`framework`.

## The reception discipline

No record in this catalog carries a heavier modern context, and the
record states it plainly: the swastika's Nazi appropriation governs the
symbol's public meaning now, and the corpus explains the canon's
reading — and the movement's later emblem revisions — without
minimizing that appropriation or its weight. Trivializing it is a named
trigger. The reception-override objection (that after the twentieth
century the ancient readings cannot be communicated past the
appropriation at all) is carried as a live alternative, not dismissed.

Equally disciplined: the symbols' documented deep histories (the
swastika's twelve-millennia distribution; the hexagram's own) are
recorded at their own strength and are **never evidence** for the
canon's reading — the simple-rotational-form explanation is the default
for the distribution, and display questions belong to the movement, not
the corpus.
