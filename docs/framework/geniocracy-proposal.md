# Geniocracy as the canon's proposed government

## Exact proposition

The canon proposes geniocracy — a selective democracy weighting
political participation by measured intelligence — delivered as the
first of the New Commandments in the 1973 contact. This record carries
the proposal as the canon's explicit normative content, together with
the standing objections, **without the corpus endorsing or
operationalizing it**.

A `normative_recommendation` with framework relation `derived` and
public label `framework` — the catalog's first record of this kind.

## What a normative record is for

The claim model's rule for this kind: a proposed goal or action must not
silently become a conclusion forced by descriptive evidence. This
record's whole function is to hold that line for the framework's most
contested proposal. What the corpus may cite it for: that the canon
proposes this, first-ranked, with these thresholds. What no artifact may
cite it for: that geniocracy is justified, workable, or entailed by
anything else the framework records. The objections — political equality
(the proposal is anti-democratic in the term's ordinary sense despite
its self-description), the psychometric gap, the documented abuse
history of capacity-gated franchise — are carried as the mainstream
position at full strength, and soft-pedaling them in prose is a named
trigger.
