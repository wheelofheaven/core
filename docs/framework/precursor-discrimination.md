# The precursor discrimination

## Exact proposition

The corpus sorts its precursor literature by method, not conclusion:
Sendy and Biglino adopted as equipment (`woh-claim-0064`); Sitchin's
direction kept while his apparatus is discarded on the cuneiform
record (the Anunnaki identification standing on `woh-claim-0002`'s
own grounds, never on the twelfth-planet philology); von Däniken's
accumulation genre declined as method; the lost-civilization
chronologies handled under the `woh-claim-0058` discipline rather
than absorbed. Agreement buys no admission; shared rejection buys no
solidarity.

An `interpretation`, `contextual`, `inferred`.

## The rulings

The Sitchin ruling is the record's sharpest edge and comes in two
halves that must not merge: the Mesopotamian direction matters, and
the Nibiru apparatus fails against the cuneiform sources — the corpus
says both. The von Däniken ruling registers the genre he launched as
reception history at its own (enormous) scale while declining the
method: a mass of weak artifacts is not a strong case. The
no-solidarity clause is the posture's point — mainstream rejection
creates no alliance among the rejected, which is what keeps the
corpus from inheriting every precursor's liabilities. Carried against
the record in full: the distinction-without-difference objection
(from outside, the sorting is invisible) and the selective-rigor
objection (the cuneiform standard, turned on the corpus's own
identifications, would cut there too).
