# The labor anthropogony convergence

## Exact proposition

The Sumerian anthropogonies (the Song of the Hoe; Enki and Ninmah), the
Babylonian Atrahasis, and Genesis 2 converge: humans shaped from the
earth, on purpose, to carry the gods' toil — with Atrahasis adding the
slaughtered god mixed into the clay, "that god and man may be mixed
together in the clay." The corpus reads the convergence as transformed
memory of a purposive creation, at the `inferred` grade.

An `interpretation` with framework relation `comparative`.

## Provenance and the pre-grounding function

Distilled at founder direction from the draft Explainer *Made from the
Ground to Bear the Labor* — the first record drafted **ahead of its
article's publication**, so that when the draft publishes it can bind on
arrival, grounding-first, rather than repeat the reverse pilot.

## The inherited discipline

This record extends the labor row the `woh-claim-0002` apparatus already
anchors (`ATRA-WOH-I:195`), and inherits that map's findings as
constraints: Genesis's motive **differs** from Atrahasis's labor-relief
(the 0002 map's own row says so, and this record preserves it), and the
step from shared structure to shared referent is supplied by the
framework, never by the comparison. The Atrahasis distinctive — the
god's flesh, blood, and intelligence in the clay — is read as the
tradition's own statement that the made being carries its makers'
substance; the genetic-engineering vocabulary stays out of the textual
layer entirely. Ovid, the Popol Vuh, and Pandora remain resemblance-only
comparanda.

## Alternatives and challenges

The potter's-craft topos; labor theology as temple economy projected;
the documented literary stream sufficing for the Near Eastern line.
