# The translation program's accuracy-first commitment

## Exact proposition

The corpus's own source-text translations (the `-woh` library
editions) are produced under a published, checkable commitment:
philological accuracy before the framework lens. Every lens-relevant
divergence is confined to the labelled commentary apparatus and never
written into the translated text; glossary decisions are recorded and
adjudicated, contested renderings escalated for review. Whether a
published edition observes the rule is verifiable by inspecting its
text against its apparatus — which is what earns the `direct` label.

A `normative_recommendation`, `contextual`, `direct`.

## The rule and its honesty

The commitment does not claim lens-free translation — the program's
honesty is the *labelled* lens, not its absence. The translated text
answers to the source language and to scholarly defensibility; the
framework's readings live in per-verse commentary, marked as such,
with glossary records carrying claim-type rationales and speculative
entries escalated past the editor to review. This is an is/ought
record in the sense the catalog's normative claims ratified: it
states the rule the project binds itself to, not a certification that
every rendering succeeds — each book's apparatus carries its own
record. The lens-leakage objection (glossary choices and rendering
defaults reach into the text however confined the commentary) and the
self-certification objection (a project checking its own rule checks
itself) are carried in full; an external review documenting leakage
into a published text is a named revision trigger requiring the
edition's correction.
