# Babel as the pardon expedition and its dispersal

## Exact proposition

The Tower of Babel episode (Genesis 11:1–9) is read, per the canon, as a
post-flood recovery project: the Eden-lineage survivors, working with the
exiled creator faction, rebuilt a technological civilization and built a
craft to carry a human delegation toward the home world — on the corpus's
reading, to seek the Council's pardon rather than to defy it — and the
Council's intervention dispersed the project, preserved in the narrative
as the confusion of tongues and the scattering.

An `interpretation` with framework relation `derived` and public label
`framework`.

## Provenance

Distilled at founder direction from `wiki/babel.md` (page label
`framework`). An episode record within the conflict carried by

## Two layers inside one record

1. **The event reading (canon-explicit):** the craft, the joint
   human-and-exile project, the intervention, the dispersal — TBWTT
   chapters 2–3.
2. **The motive reading (inferred, labelled):** the expedition as
   peace-seeking — a pardon delegation — rather than defiance. The
   compressed Genesis narrative does not state the motive; the corpus's
   reading goes beyond it and says so. This is the corpus's canonical
   example of an `inferred` step riding inside a `framework` record, and
   the record keeps the two grades visible.

The memory mapping ("one language" as the project's unity; the confusion
as administrative dispersal) operates at the transformed-memory level with
the discipline of `woh-claim-0004`: no historical-linguistic claims, no
site identifications, no claim about what the redactor understood.

## Alternatives and challenges

The aetiology reading (dispersion and language diversity, with polemic
against Babylonian tower pretension — the standard critical account); the
hubris-tradition objection, which is sharp because it claims the defiance
frame the corpus rejects is the narrative's own; and the canon-retrofit
objection (rocketry onto a ziggurat).
