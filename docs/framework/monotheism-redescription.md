# Monotheism as re-description of the plural Elohim

## Exact proposition

The Hebrew Bible preserves a divine council alongside its demands for
exclusive allegiance; the anti-idol polemic functions less as arithmetic
about how many superhuman beings exist than as a struggle over memory,
representation, mediation, and loyalty; and the corpus's stronger proposal
reads prophetic monotheism as a progressive re-description of the plural
Elohim into one immaterial God — making the monotheism/polytheism division
the wrong question to put to the sources.

This is an `interpretation` with framework relation `derived` and public
label `speculative` — the corpus's own article carries the same label, and
the record keeps it: the functional reading is defensible synthesis, and
the re-description proposal goes beyond what any single source states.

## Provenance

Distilled at founder direction from the published Explainer *Monotheism Is
the Wrong Question* and `wiki/plurality-of-gods.md`. Depends on
`woh-claim-0009`: the singular-God translation observation is the hinge the
re-description proposal turns on.

## The ladder of the claim

The record is deliberately a ladder, each rung labelled: the council and
plurality texts (mainstream); monotheism as development (mainstream —
Smith); the functional reading of the polemic across Israel, Paul, and the
Qur'an (synthesis, with each tradition's irreducible differences preserved
in scope); the re-description of the plural Elohim (framework/speculative).
An artifact may stand on any rung, and must not present a higher rung as a
lower one.

## Alternatives and challenges

The developmental-suffices objection is the strongest and is conceded in
the record's own terms: the corpus reading currently adds a referent
without adding a prediction. The flattening risk is internal and real —
the scope's preservation clause exists because of it. Category
substitution applies as everywhere.
