# The canon's quotation layer

## Exact proposition

The canon's Hebrew-Bible quotations are mediated through Dhorme's
Pléiade translation (1956/1959): the *Iahvé* orthography, the
preserved *Élohim* plural, and the literal operational register are
the textual layer through which *The Book Which Tells the Truth*
cites scripture, and the canon's own account places the Dhorme volume
in Vorilhon's hands at the 1973 contact. A checkable textual fact —
the marker instance is verified in the project digitization.

An `empirical_observation`, `contextual`, `direct`.

## Why the record exists

Two disciplines hang on it. First, honesty about mediation: the
corpus's quotation-level readings — the preserved Elohim plural above
all — inherit Dhorme's philological choices, and the corpus says so
rather than treating the quotations as unmediated text. Second, the
no-enlistment rule: Dhorme's literalism is scholarly method, and the
record claims neither the translator nor his academy for the
framework. The provenance clause (the volume at the contact) is the
canon's self-report, carried at `woh-claim-0020`'s grade; the
partial-mediation alternative — that the layer may be mixed until a
full collation exists — is carried in full, and completing that
collation is the record's named upgrade path.
