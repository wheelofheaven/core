# The literalist convergence

## Exact proposition

Independent readers with different starting points, methods, and stakes
— Sendy (1963, the first finished statement, with a falsifiable lunar
wager he lost), Biglino (the publisher-sanctioned interlinear translator
whose literalism outlived his commission), Wallis (the serving
archdeacon reading from inside the church) — arrived at plural,
physical, mortal Elohim readings of the same Hebrew texts. The corpus
registers the convergence as reception evidence for the reading's
availability in the texts, with the shared-idiom objection carried in
full.

An `interpretation` with framework relation `contextual` and public
label `inferred`.

## Provenance

Distilled at founder direction from three published Explainers at once —
*The Man Who Bet the Bible on the Moon* (Sendy), *The Translator's
Wager* (Biglino), and *The Archdeacon and the Dragon* (Wallis) — one
record where three thin ones would have fragmented a single argument.

## The Sendy wager as method exhibit

Sendy staked his reading on a falsifiable prediction — lunar
installations, decided by Apollo — and lost. The corpus treats the lost
wager as the *model* of honest staking, not an embarrassment: it is the
behavioral ancestor of this catalog's own revision triggers, and the
scope forbids both burying it and misusing it (his lost bet stakes his
claims, not the framework's, which carry their own triggers). Equally
scoped: no enrollment of the three readers, who hold their own
positions; no extension to the genre's speculative wing (Sitchin, von
Däniken); and never convergence-as-demonstration — availability in the
texts is all the registration claims.
