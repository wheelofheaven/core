# The early-qibla convergence on Petra

## Exact proposition

On Gibson's survey, the earliest datable mosques' qibla walls point
toward Petra rather than Mecca for roughly the first Islamic century, and
this sits within a convergence the corpus registers — Mecca's absence
from Ptolemy's Arabia, the earliest Ka'ba descriptions, the tradition's
own qibla-change memory — read alongside the canon's Abraham material.
The corpus **deliberately preserves the tension** between this reading
and the traditional Hijaz account.

An `interpretation` with framework relation `comparative` and public
label `speculative`, matching the published Explainer.

## The preserved tension is the record's spine

The corpus's Petra/Hanafiyya material holds an intentional tension with
its Hijaz-tradition material, and this record hard-codes the intention:
neither reading overwrites the other, and prose that resolves the tension
in either direction trips a revision trigger. The tradition's own
accounts are engaged in their own voice throughout — the qibla-change
passages (Q 2:142–150) are part of the record's primary layer, not an
embarrassment to it.

Equally hard-coded: the canon layer and the Gibson layer must not
launder each other. The canon's Abraham reading is not evidence for the
orientation survey; the survey is not evidence for the canon. Each layer
carries its own grade. Schumm and Goldstein's statistical analysis sits
inside the survey layer (it finds the orientations consistent with
aiming at Petra *under Gibson's assumptions*), and David A. King's
folk-astronomical "sacred geography" account stands against the survey
layer at full strength.

## Alternatives and challenges

King's sacred-geography account (on which the orientations reflect a
non-geographical sacred direction toward the Ka'ba and the Petra pattern
dissolves); the tradition's own change-account sufficing; and the
revisionist-overreach objection to mixed-quality convergence items.
