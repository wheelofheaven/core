# Yahweh as Council president

## Exact proposition

The biblical name Yahweh refers, per the canon, to a specific individual
Eloha: president of the Council of the Eternals, senior authority of the
alliance, personal director of the Earth project, and the canon's
principal speaker. The Hebrew Bible's YHWH material is read as memory of
this individual's office and actions — with per-passage attribution
remaining editorial work.

An `interpretation` with framework relation `derived` and public label
`framework`.

## The attribution discipline

The record's sharpest internal constraint mirrors `woh-claim-0009`'s:
the name's reference varies by passage and period, and the record
refuses uniform attribution. A YHWH passage may remember the individual,
the office, or the tradition's own theology, and which is which is
per-passage editorial work the record licenses without pre-deciding. The
composite-memory alternative — that even inside the framework the
biblical material composites multiple actors — is carried as a live
objection, not an inconvenience.

Excluded: the documentary hypothesis's strata as targets, any etymology
of the Tetragrammaton, and divinity claims — the referent is a mortal
individual.

## Alternatives and challenges

The mainstream development account (Smith); composite memory; the
narrative-device reading of the canon's speaker-identification.
