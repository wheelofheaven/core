# Evidence status

Evidence status applies to a precisely stated claim, not to a whole source,
page, tradition, or project.

| Status | Meaning | Minimum record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `unmapped` | Relevant evidence has not been systematically located | Search need and boundary |
| `scoped` | Candidate sources, alternatives, and disputes are identified | Search boundaries and anchor sources |
| `reviewed` | A reproducible search and structured appraisal are complete | Protocol, screening, extraction, limitations, reviewer sign-off |
| `replicated` | A relevant result has independent replication | Relationship among samples, methods, and assumptions |
| `contested` | Credible evidence or interpretation materially conflicts | Strongest competing accounts and discriminating questions |
| `unsupported` | The reviewed evidence does not support the stated claim | Review scope, uncertainty, and revision conditions |

A claim may be both `reviewed` and `contested`. `Unsupported` is not permanent:
the record must state what new evidence would justify reopening it.

## Evidence dimensions

Where relevant, mature evidence maps report:

- directness to the exact proposition;
- source access and textual reliability;
- dating, provenance, genre, and transmission;
- linguistic and translation uncertainty;
- historical and scientific validity;
- independence from shared sources or assumptions;
- robustness to alternative translations, chronologies, and models;
- known counterevidence and missing material.

No numeric confidence score is defined. A single score would conceal the
difference between canonical centrality, textual attestation, empirical
support, and scholarly agreement.

## Advancement rule

A claim moves beyond `scoped` only with a documented search protocol,
inclusion criteria, extraction record, appraisal, limitations, and dated
reviewer sign-off. Publication, repetition, or a six-source count is not a
substitute for review.
