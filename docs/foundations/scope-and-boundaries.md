# Scope and boundaries

## Research domain

Wheel of Heaven studies a particular interpretation of ancient creation
traditions: that accounts conventionally read as supernatural may preserve
records of a technologically advanced biological civilization and its
interventions in terrestrial life and history.

The project reads from a declared Raëlian canonical centre and compares that
framework with ancient primary texts, philology, history, archaeology,
astronomy, biology, comparative religion, and critical scholarship.

## Current scope

The core exists to:

- specify the framework's propositions and dependencies;
- distinguish source reports, observations, models, hypotheses,
  interpretations, and normative proposals;
- document how canonical commitment differs from empirical support;
- preserve evidence maps, objections, alternatives, limitations, and revision
  triggers;
- make source access and interpretive steps reconstructable;
- govern conceptual versions and compatibility with public outputs.

## Current non-goals

The core does not:

- claim that the Wheel of Heaven hypothesis has been empirically verified;
- treat project acceptance as scientific acceptance or religious authority;
- pretend the project lacks an interpretive stance;
- require readers or contributors to assent to the foundational canon;
- identify every deity, myth, motif, or tradition as the same referent;
- treat resemblance as proof of historical dependence or identity;
- replace primary texts, the bibliography, public content, or software repos;
- generate a website, database, production service, or translation pipeline;
- convert criticism into a weaker paraphrase for framework convenience.

## Units and scopes

Every claim must identify its relevant unit and scope where possible. Depending
on the question, the unit may be a lexical form, textual passage, source
tradition, narrative motif, historical event, proposed entity, technological
capability, chronological interval, or relationship among them.

Terms such as `Elohim`, `Anunnaki`, `civilization`, `tradition`, `identity`,
`creation`, and `evidence` must not be used as if their boundaries were
self-evident.

## Relationship to public content

The core controls conceptual meaning; `data-content` controls public prose.
Public pages may simplify a core record for readers but must not strengthen its
evidence status, erase material alternatives, or silently redefine the claim.
Public URLs and existing badges remain separate compatibility concerns.
