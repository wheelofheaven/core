# Epistemic principles

## Declared lens

Wheel of Heaven adopts the Raëlian source corpus as its foundational canon and
interpretive centre. This stance must be stated plainly. It must never be
smuggled into empirical, philological, or historical claims as if those fields
had already established the canon's account.

## Orthogonal status

Claim kind, framework relation, lifecycle, evidence status, and public label
answer different questions. No value on one axis determines another.

An accepted foundational premise can be contested and empirically unverified.
A reviewed observation can remain merely contextual to the framework. A
speculative public label can represent a carefully documented interpretation.

## Source–world distinction

That a source explicitly reports an event establishes a source report, not the
event. The core must preserve the inferential step between textual content and
claims about historical or physical reality.

## Evidence proportionality

Language must not exceed evidence. Record access level, provenance, locators,
uncertainty, conflicting evidence, alternative explanations, and scope. A
source congenial to the framework is not stronger because it is congenial.

## Comparison discipline

Resemblance, typological equivalence, historical dependence, common origin, and
referential identity are distinct. Cross-tradition work must preserve
differences in language, date, genre, transmission, function, and cultural
setting.

## Philological discipline

Morphology, syntax, lexicography, translation, etymology, reception history,
and project rendering are separate layers. A canonical gloss must be labelled
as such when it is not the consensus lexical derivation. Translation choices
must not be retrofitted into proof of the framework that motivated them.

## Scientific and historical discipline

Technological possibility, conceptual compatibility, analogy, and evidence of
actual occurrence are different claims. A scientific proposal such as directed
panspermia can show that an idea is scientifically formulable without showing
that the proposed historical event happened.

## Independence of evidence

Repeated citations are not independent confirmation when they reuse the same
text, translation, dataset, authorial tradition, or assumption. Evidence maps
should expose dependencies rather than count citations as votes.

## Revision and dissent

Preserve serious objections, failed interpretations, negative findings,
superseded meanings, and revision reasons. The strongest fair version of a
challenge belongs in the record even when Wheel retains its framework.

## Bounded public projection

Reader-facing badges and concise prose are useful publication tools. They are
not the research model. A projection must link back to the richer claim record
and must never overwrite it.
