## The strongest objection

The hit is made of material that any account of the book predicts, and the recorded rival says so in advance.

The three `supported` items are: a quotation of Ezekiel taken from a printed French Bible; a remark that Genesis speaks of sons of Elohim and daughters of men; and an allusion to Isaiah 43:6, half verbatim. Checking a French Bible quotation against the Hebrew tests the translator of that Bible. It does not test the speaker. The source-critical alternative, stated as its proponents would state it, holds that a French author composed the book with a French Bible open; on that account quotations copied from it will match the Hebrew exactly as far as the translation does. The rival note in the record already grants this: "the second book may say little about biblical wording, in which case the alternative predicts nothing observable here." That case is the one found. No recorded alternative predicts a different result for this material, so the discrimination is `none`.

The hit criterion ("at least one statement is classified `supported` and none `unsupported`") could be met by a single accurate Bible quotation. For a book that quotes the Bible at all it was close to certain, once quotations are admitted as statements of the kind.

Whether they should be admitted is the second objection. The question asks for statements "about the meaning of a Hebrew or biblical word, name or title, or about what the original (Hebrew) text says". The Locator's answer to that question is that there are none. The Deriver's declared prior was the same ("few or no such statements exist (partial)"), and the partial criterion names this case: "including the case where no such statement is found". The sister finding for woh-exp-0001 left plain quotations out of its count by rule ("Plain restatements of a quoted verse were not listed"). By that rule items 1 and 2 here are not listed, item 3 is at most an allusion, and the count is zero. The `meets_hit` assessment exists because this Locator listed what the other Locator excluded.

Independence. All three items rest on the same French Bible translation the first book quotes. They are not a second witness to anything.

## What the result does and does not show

It shows that the second book, in the voice of its speaker, makes no claim about what any Hebrew word means and never appeals to the Hebrew or the original; and that its few biblical quotations and allusions follow the transmitted text as far as a French translation does, with one wrong chapter number of unknown origin and one adapted clause.

It does not show that the speaker's statements about Hebrew are sound. There are none to be sound or unsound. It does not show agreement between the two books "in this respect", which the derivation expected: the first book comments on wording and the second does not.

Read with woh-exp-0001 (one family, one result): outside the excluded Elohim gloss, neither book offers a testable statement of what a Hebrew word means. The first book, where it comments on the wording of verses, misstates it once or twice; the second book does not comment on wording and therefore does not err. The hit here does not offset the miss there, and the two together do not amount to a mixed result. The difference between the two assessments is the difference between the two books' amount of exegesis and between two Locators' inclusion rules.

A point that cuts against the framework reading and should not be lost: at 2:104 and 2:123 the speaker attaches to the Genesis 6 reference the statement that the people of Israel descend from those unions. Genesis 6:4 names the offspring only as הַגִּבֹּרִים ... אַנְשֵׁי הַשֵּׁם. The speaker does not say Genesis states the descent, so the Locator rightly left it unclassified; but item 2 is `supported` only for the bare mention.

## What the ruling turns on

1. Whether plain quotations and allusions are "statements about what the original text says". If they are, the criteria are met but nearly any result would have met them, and the discrimination is `none`. If they are not, the registered outcome is the partial case.
2. Whether one inclusion rule is to govern both members of the family. The same choice that keeps this hit (admit every report of wording) is the one that keeps the miss in woh-exp-0001; the choice that removes this hit (only statements that invoke the original or assert something beyond the quotation) leaves woh-exp-0001 resting on its rows A3b and A4.
3. Whether a result of the form "the book makes no such statement" is worth recording under its own description, which the registered `hit` wording does not give.
