# Contest: woh-exp-0007

- Date: 2026-11-22
- Assessment contested: `meets_hit` (discrimination `none`, novelty `unrecorded`)
- Recommendation: sustain

The assessment stands as a mechanical application of the registered
criteria. It was registered in the class that is not progress, and nothing
found here moves it out of that class. Two points are left to the ruling:
whether the expectation belongs to `woh-claim-0001` at all, and whether the
1975 book's planet passages state an origin or only a residence.

## Verification

Source reopened: the French `text` field of all seven chapter files
(ETTMTTP 77 + 132 + 266 paragraphs; LWTE 201 + 235 + 88 + 312). `i18n.en`
was not used.

- **Quotations.** Every quoted string in the finding that is tied to a
  refId was compared by script with the paragraph it cites (119 strings
  match verbatim). The few non-matches are not errors of substance: the
  finding quotes "extra-terrestre" where the file has the line-break form
  "extra- terrestre" (ETTMTTP-1:63, which the finding itself notes), and
  some strings are section headings cited from a neighbouring paragraph
  (LWTE-4:148, 4:177, 4:194). No quotation is altered or misattributed.
- **Context read in full:** ETTMTTP-2:24–36, 2:44–56, 2:98, 2:123, 3:193,
  3:214–219; LWTE-1:1–18, 1:26–28, 1:57–70, 1:89–93, 1:104–108,
  1:144–153, 1:193–197, 2:117–124, 4:1–26, 4:79–96, 4:191–192.
- **Independent screen.** I searched both books for terms outside the
  protocol and the Locator's supplement (omnipresence, omniscience,
  invisibility, telepathy, angels, instantaneous appearance, miracle,
  surveillance, prayer, veneration). I found no passage that makes the
  Elohim immaterial or gives them a non-planetary origin. I did not read
  all 1,311 paragraphs continuously either.

Discrepancies and omissions in the finding:

1. **The mortal/eternal difference is not only between the books.** The
   finding sets ETTMTTP-2:53 ("Ils vieillissent et meurent comme nous …
   sept-cents ans environ") against LWTE-1:65 ("ils sont éternels"). The
   same speech in the 1975 book already has both: ETTMTTP-2:51 "nous vivons
   tous heureux et éternellement", two paragraphs before 2:53. LWTE-2:123
   says the mechanism was "expliqué dans le premier message". It is one
   doctrine stated twice (bodies age and die; persons are re-made from a
   cell), and the 1979 book did not introduce it.
2. **Two of the five ETTMTTP `origin_other_planet` passages do not state an
   origin, and a third names the wrong planet.** ETTMTTP-2:25 and 2:29 are
   general remarks about visitors from other planets (the finding marks
   2:25 "indirect"). ETTMTTP-3:216 says "la planète des Elohim" of the
   place of scientific reincarnation, which 3:214 calls "la planète des
   éternels" and 2:51 describes as a habitat they stocked themselves
   ("nous avons créé ici toute vie") and expect to leave. What remains is
   2:50 ("une résidence se trouvant sur notre planète") and 3:193 ("la
   planète de nos créateurs, les Elohim"). Both are possessive statements
   of where the Elohim live. Neither uses a word for origin. The 1979 book
   does ("leur planète d'origine", LWTE-2:6; "leur planète originelle",
   LWTE-2:22).
3. **Functional attributes are not reported together.** The finding files
   surveillance and healing under adjacent observations as "attributed to
   technique" and does not set them beside LWTE-1:195. They belong
   together; see the objection below.
4. **The reprint is dated too loosely.** The editorial footnotes
   (LWTE-1:21, 3:26) cite an "édition 2000", so the exemplar behind the
   digitization is from 2000 or later, not merely post-1998.

### The three `ambiguous` paragraphs

None should have been classed `immaterial_or_supernatural`.

- **LWTE-1:65.** The paragraph distinguishes two things that it says were
  wrongly merged under the word "dieu": the infinite and the Elohim
  (1:63–64). "Eternels" is granted to the Elohim as a property borrowed by
  confusion from the infinite, "en partie vrai". The same answer ends on
  the mechanism: "il y a le code génétique permettant d'avoir accès à la
  vie éternelle" (1:70; also 1:69). Eternity here is serial re-creation of
  a body. It is a claim about technique applied to organisms.
- **LWTE-1:67.** "Ceux pour qui «dieu» c'est nos créateurs, les Elohim,
  n'ont pas tort non plus" concedes the *reference* of the word, on the
  condition that they are "nos grands frères de l'infini" and not beings
  to venerate. It asserts nothing about their nature.
- **LWTE-1:195.** The sentence is built as a contrast. The deist concept
  is said to mix the infinite, "effectivement impalpable", with "des
  créateurs, les Elohim, qui sont eux bien palpables et tout-puissants
  dans ce secteur de l'infini". "Palpables" is the point of the sentence;
  "tout-puissants" is bounded by "dans ce secteur". It is the strongest
  divine attribute either book grants them in its own voice, and it is
  granted to material beings.

`ambiguous` was a fair class for all three. No passage qualifies as
`origin_other`: LWTE-2:28 and 1:93 have the Elohim made in a laboratory by
earlier beings, but "sur leur planète".

### Does mortal/eternal bear on "biological"?

Not against it. Every statement of the Elohim's eternity in these books
runs through a body: ageing and death (ETTMTTP-2:53), a surgical extension
to about seven hundred years (2:53), re-creation from a cell (2:30, 2:50),
"plusieurs corps" (LWTE-2:123), a genetic code (LWTE-1:69–70). An
intrinsically deathless being would need none of this. The difference
bears on *lifespan*, which `woh-claim-0001` excludes by name. Two cautions:
LWTE-1:65 says "ils sont éternels" of the Elohim without limit, while
ETTMTTP-2:50 confines eternity to the seven hundred council members; and
the word is conceded in a sentence about the properties of the infinite.
A reader who wants to argue that the 1979 book drifts toward divine
vocabulary has this sentence and 1:195 to work with, and nothing stronger.

## The strongest objection

**The test could not have come out otherwise, and it does not measure what
a critic means by divinisation.**

The source-critical alternative, put as its proponents would put it: the
1975 and 1979 books are the founder's continuation and defence of his own
1974 book. Their central rhetorical move is the denial of the
supernatural. "Il n'y a pas de dieu et évidemment pas d'âme" (ETTMTTP-2:30)
and "ni dieu, ni âme, mais Elohim et le code génétique" (LWTE-1:61) are
the thesis, and the 1979 book opens with a section in which the author
harmonises "contradictions apparentes" between his first two books
(LWTE-1:2–12). An author writing to that purpose will not describe his
creators as spirits. On this account the result is not something the
alternative is `silent` about. It predicts the `same`, and with more
confidence than the framework derivation does, because one author
controlling three texts is a sufficient cause of agreement on the
headline descriptors. The registered `discrimination: none` is therefore
correct, though the rival note understates the alternative.

The second half of the objection is about the classes. The criterion asks
only whether a passage *calls* the Elohim immaterial or supernatural. The
same books give them, by technical means, most of what a deity does:

- they observe every human being ("Tous les hommes, nous les observons.
  D'immenses ordinateurs assurent une surveillance permanente de tous les
  hommes vivant sur la Terre", ETTMTTP-2:98; "le grand ordinateur qui
  surveille tous les hommes de leur conception à leur mort", LWTE-2:154);
- they grade each life and award re-creation or suffering (ETTMTTP-2:98,
  3:214–217);
- they heal ("comme nous guérissons déjà à Lourdes", ETTMTTP-2:98);
- they are addressed by daily telepathic contact, called "le sens originel
  de la prière" (ETTMTTP-3:116);
- they can move planets and whole solar systems (LWTE-1:59) and are
  "tout-puissants dans ce secteur de l'infini" (LWTE-1:195).

LWTE-1:195 condemns as a false prophet anyone who teaches a god "surveillant
les hommes un par un afin de leur réserver un châtiment ou une récompense".
The 1975 book attributes exactly that function to the Elohim. The
questioner at LWTE-1:62 puts the point himself: "ne peut-on pas considérer
que les Elohim remplacent dieu dans l'esprit de bien des raëliens". A
scholar of religion would say the books relocate divine functions into a
technological idiom while refusing the label. The registered classes
record the refusal of the label and cannot see the relocation. The
`ambiguous` class, which the Locator used honestly, has no effect on any
criterion.

This does not make the classification wrong. The books do say, repeatedly
and without exception found, that these are material beings. It means the
hit is a report that the books keep their own vocabulary.

**Scope.** `woh-claim-0001` excludes "the report layer itself — what the
canonical source explicitly states — which is recorded by
`woh-claim-0070`", and "every detail reported by the Raëlian corpus". An
expectation of kind `canon`, whose every criterion is about what two books
state, tests the report layer. The Deriver charter says an expectation
resting on something the claim excludes belongs to another claim. But
`woh-claim-0070` does not hold it either as written: its scope is
`TBWTT-1:53` and `TBWTT-2:5`, and it excludes "every other statement in
the canon". The result has no claim record whose scope contains it.

The derivation tries to bridge this through decomposition part 4: "a real
civilization has one nature and one origin, so the books that continue the
report must agree". That inference needs a premise the claim does not
state and in fact disclaims, namely that the later books transmit the
report faithfully in detail. A real referent can be reported
inconsistently, and an invented one consistently. The record lists no
auxiliary assumption. So a miss would have counted against the fidelity of
the later books before it touched the hypothesis, and a hit says nothing
about a referent.

## What the result does and does not show

It shows, as a source report about the digitized French text of two
books:

- both books describe the Elohim as bodily, human-like beings who age,
  die, are sterilised, and have children with human women;
- both place them on another planet, the 1979 book with explicit origin
  wording, the 1975 book with possessive wording only;
- neither contains, within what was searched, a passage in its own voice
  that makes them spirits or gives them another origin;
- the 1979 book concedes "éternels" and a bounded "tout-puissants" to
  them while insisting they are palpable.

It does not show:

- that any such beings exist or that the report describes an event (the
  derivation says this itself);
- that the three books have one referent. Agreement among texts by one
  author on his central premise is the same text tradition counted again.
  The LWTE source note already rules that for source counting "the three
  books are one source";
- consistency of the report in detail. The six differences the Locator
  lists are real. Four of them (which planet, habitability, distance,
  lifespan) fall under what `woh-claim-0001` excludes; they are still
  inconsistencies of the report layer and belong in a record of it;
- anything about the 1979 printing. The digitization follows a reprint of
  2000 or later, and neither book's first printing has been collated.

### How much the three limits matter

- **Term screening.** The hit criterion is a universal negative, and a
  universal negative from a term-screened reading is only as good as the
  terms. Here the terms are broad (esprit, âme, dieu, corps, monde,
  planète) and my separate screen found nothing contrary. The residual
  risk is a description that uses none of the words. I rate it low. It is
  not zero, and "no passage" should be read as "no passage found by a
  screened reading".
- **Reprint.** This limit matters more for a consistency test than for
  most uses, because later editing is the usual way inconsistencies leave
  a text. Two things reduce the worry without removing it: the text still
  prints the questioners' "contradictions", and the footnotes that betray
  the reprint are page-reference updates, not doctrinal revision. The
  finding is good for the digitized state only.
- **`speaker` field.** It does not affect any quotation. It affects the
  judgement "in the book's own voice", which the Locator made from the
  text. The hit does not depend on chapter 4: remove the three testimony
  paragraphs from `biological_physical` and LWTE-4:192 from
  `origin_other_planet`, and LWTE still has Raël's commentary voice at
  2:6, 2:22 and 2:28. In ETTMTTP chapter 3 the field says "Yahweh" for
  text that speaks of "les Elohim nos créateurs" in a human first person;
  3:193, the better of the two origin passages, is in that chapter.

## What the ruling turns on

1. **Whether the expectation is scored under `woh-claim-0001`.** If the
   exclusion of the report layer is applied as written, the expectation is
   badly placed and the choice is between `void` for scope and recording
   the result as source-report content outside this claim's scoreboard.
   If the bridge through decomposition part 4 is accepted, the unstated
   premise about the later books' fidelity should be named as an auxiliary
   assumption.
2. **Whether residence counts as origin for the 1975 book.** If "notre
   planète" and "la planète de nos créateurs, les Elohim" satisfy
   `origin_other_planet`, the hit criterion is met. If an origin statement
   is required, the 1975 book lacks one and the registered `partial`
   criterion applies.
3. **Whether a refusal of the label is what was meant to be tested.** If
   the question was whether the books ever call the Elohim supernatural,
   the result answers it. If it was whether the later books keep them
   ordinary biological beings in function as well as in name, the classes
   did not test that, and the passages listed above are the material for a
   differently formed expectation.

In every case the class stays `discrimination: none`.
