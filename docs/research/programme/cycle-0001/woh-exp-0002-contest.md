## The strongest objection

**The test as registered measures the translator, and both sides predict what it found.**

The statement speaks of "the French words on which the explanation rests", and the derivation says an identification with the Hebrew referent "holds only where the feature it hangs on is in the Hebrew". The protocol turned this into a comparison of the French verse word with the Hebrew word at that place. For a literal scholarly translation that comparison comes out `carried` almost by construction: « flamme de feu » renders לַבַּת־אֵשׁ, « nuée » renders עָנָן. The features the readings in fact hang on are elsewhere: light without heat, emitted smoke, a sprayed synthetic food, a detector. The Locator records that none of these is a lexicon sense of the Hebrew word, and none is a sense of the French word either. The step from the word to the technology is made by the commentary, and the registered classes do not test that step.

The source-critical alternative, stated as its proponents would state it: the author worked from a French Bible (the one `woh-claim-0066` identifies), chose and abridged verses, and attached a technological scenario to each. On that account he never needs a word the translator did not supply, and the translator supplied what the Hebrew has. The account therefore expects `carried` at nearly every point the commentary touches. What it predicts distinctively is that the readings track the French page (its ellipses, its bracketed additions, its register) and nowhere show knowledge of the Hebrew that the French lacks. The record's rival note concedes most of this ("a clean result is compatible with a careful author"). On inspection the concession is the whole of it: for an all-`carried` outcome the alternative predicts the same, and the rating should be `none`. Only a `shifted` or `absent` result could have discriminated, and it would have counted against the expectation. The test could lose and could not win.

**The population the statement quantifies over is nearly empty.**

The statement begins "Where the book ... explains particular words of the quotation as physical objects or events". In this scope the book does that, strictly, once (hinge 4, and there on its own paraphrase). In six of ten quotations the commentary takes up no word. In three more it re-describes the thing the verse names without repeating the word. The Deriver's prior was that cloud, fire, thunder and trumpet at Sinai are read technologically; the three Exodus 19 quotations (TBWTT-3:25, 3:26, 3:28) have no hinge word at all, and their commentary (« Ils descendirent dans un engin volant ») stands before them. The expectation presupposed a word-by-word exegesis that the target does not contain.

**The count, and with it the outcome class, depends on reading decisions.**

The Locator states the alternatives candidly. Under the registered criteria they give:

| Reading of "explicitly takes up and explains" | Hinge words | Outcome under the criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verbatim uptake and gloss | 1 `carried` | partial |
| Paraphrase of the named thing counts (Locator's choice) | 4 `carried` | partial |
| Unglossed repetitions also count | 7 `carried` | hit |
| Locator's choice, with Ex 17:6 entered as `shifted` (the reservation in the finding) | 3 `carried`, 1 `shifted` | miss (one in four exceeds one in five) |

The third reading is excluded by the protocol's own wording ("explains as"), so `hit` is not fairly reachable. The fourth is a real judgement call: the French is causative with Moses as agent, the Hebrew has the water come out after the rock is struck. I think `carried` is the better entry, because the commentary relies on the staff and not on the causative, and the staff is in the Hebrew context. But a reader should know that one classification of one paraphrase separates `meets_partial` from `meets_miss` here.

**Independence.** The result is not independent of what core already records. `woh-claim-0066` characterises the quotation layer as "the most literal scholarly translation" with "literal operational vocabulary". That the French words of such a translation are ordinary renderings of the Hebrew is close to a restatement of that record. `novelty: unrecorded` is correct in the narrow sense that no record lists these verses; the outcome was implied by recorded ground.

## What the result does and does not show

It shows:

- The book quotes Exodus 3, 13, 14, 16, 17 and 19 ten times, all in chapter 3, and Exodus 20 never.
- At the four places where the commentary re-describes something the quotation names, the French is an ordinary rendering of the Hebrew.
- The commentary in this section is scenario set beside quotation. It rarely fastens on a word. This is a descriptive finding about the book's method that core did not record before, and it is the most useful thing the Locate stage produced. It was not what was registered and cannot be scored.

It does not show:

- That the readings have a footing in the Hebrew in any sense stronger than "the verse mentions a fire, a cloud, a crust". The same holds for any reading whatever of a literal translation.
- Anything that favours the claim over the source-critical alternative, or the reverse.
- Anything about the Sinai vocabulary (thunder, horn, smoke, trembling), which the commentary does not take up.

**The family taken together** (`woh-exp-0002`, `0003`, `0004`). One test, three targets, one result with three instances. In the registered scopes 22 quotations were examined; 13 have no hinge word. Eleven hinge words were classified, all `carried` (4, 5, 2). A borderline set outside the scope of `0004` adds two `carried` and one `shifted`. All three Locators, working apart, reported the same two things: the commentary seldom takes up a word of the verse, and where the French word is carried by the Hebrew the technological re-description is a lexicon sense of neither. The family result is therefore: the French of the quotations is faithful at the points the commentary touches, which was expected on every account, and the readings are not lexical. The one place in the family where the commentary uses a sense the French word has and the Hebrew word lacks (« science » → « livres scientifiques », Genesis 2:17) is the kind of case the source-critical alternative predicted "in tendency". The family supplies no discriminating hit.

## What the ruling turns on

1. Whether a result that every alternative predicted is to be entered with `discrimination: partial` as registered, or treated as `none`. If the latter, the instance is not progress whichever way it is ruled.
2. Whether "fewer than five hinge words" is ruled as the registered `partial`, or as `open` on the ground that the target did not contain enough of what the expectation presupposed. The second describes the situation more exactly: the miss is of the premise (word-level exegesis), not of the Hebrew.
3. Whether the Locator's entry `carried` for Exodus 17:6 is accepted. If it is not, the registered criteria give `miss`.
4. Whether the unregistered observation (the commentary is narrative, not lexical) is to be taken forward as a new expectation in a later cycle, with the test pointed at the step the readings rest on: from the verse's image to the technical object.
