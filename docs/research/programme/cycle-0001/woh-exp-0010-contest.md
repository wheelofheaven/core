## The strongest objection

A control cannot be faulted for being predictable. The objection is to what the hit could be taken to support.

The derivation runs: on the identification, "in our image" is bodily resemblance, "so the noun should be a word for visible physical likeness". The count shows something narrower: outside Genesis the noun mostly names made objects. The lexicon does not carry the further step. BDB puts the Genesis occurrences in a sense of their own, "image, likeness, of resemblance", apart from the concrete sense 1, and it records two occurrences where the noun means "a (mere) semblance". The class `concrete_object` counts artefacts (idols, cast figures, wall pictures, golden models). That an idol is a made thing says nothing by itself about whether the thing so called in Genesis 1 resembles its maker in body.

The alternatives, at their strength, use the same lexical fact for a different conclusion. The current theological and historical-critical readings take the statue sense and argue that a king's or a god's statue stands for its owner's presence and authority where it is set up; humans as the deity's "image" are then representatives, and bodily resemblance is not the point. On that reading a concrete majority is exactly what is expected. So the registered `same` for both alternatives is right, the registered discrimination `none` is right, and a hit here favours nothing. The strongest single datum for a resemblance reading is Gen 5:3, and it too is common ground for every reading.

Independence within the count. Twelve occurrences are not twelve witnesses. 1 Sam 6:5 (twice) and 6:11 are one episode. 2 Kgs 11:18 and 2 Chr 23:17 are the same sentence in parallel texts. Amos 5:26 is marked textually dubious by the lexicon. Counted by context: concrete in Num 33:52; 1 Sam 6; 2 Kgs 11:18 with its parallel; Ezek 7:20; 16:17; 23:14; and Amos 5:26 if kept: seven, or six without Amos, against two non-concrete. The criterion is still met on any of these counts.

Edition and lexeme. Only BDB was consulted. Lexica differ on whether the two Psalms occurrences belong to this noun or to a homonym from a root for darkness or shadow; the finding says so and HALOT was not reached. If they are a separate word, they leave the count and the majority rises. The hit is robust either way, which is itself a sign of how loose the criterion was: no reading of the evidence known to the Deriver could have produced a miss.

Novelty. `unrecorded` is true of core. The project's own Genesis edition already states this distribution in its note at GEN-WOH-1:27, and the Deriver declared full prior knowledge. In substance this is ground the project had already covered, in a selective form.

## What the result does and does not show

It shows that the pipeline returns a known answer when given one, with a complete occurrence list, exact quotations and the complicating data (the two Psalms, Dan 3:19, BDB's separate Genesis sense, the Amos doubt) reported without being asked. As a calibration of the Locate and Assess stages that is useful. It does not test whether the Locator was blind in any meaningful sense, since the answer is common knowledge.

It shows, as a by-product, that the project edition's note at GEN-WOH-1:27 ("The Hebrew lexicon supports physical resemblance directly, without interpretive extension") states more than the lexicon consulted here does: BDB separates the Genesis sense from the concrete one, and the note omits the two non-concrete occurrences. That is an observation about a published note; what follows from it is not for this file.

It does not show that "image" in Genesis 1:26 means bodily resemblance, and it does not favour the identification of the plural speakers over any recorded alternative.

## What the ruling turns on

1. Whether a control whose hit criterion no known reading of the evidence could fail is to be ruled at all, or recorded as a calibration and kept out of the scoreboard's counts. The record already sets discrimination `none`, so it is not progress on any ruling.
2. Whether novelty should stay `unrecorded` when the project's own published edition already states the datum, core's records apart.
3. Whether the gap between "mostly names made objects" (shown) and "a word for visible physical likeness" (the derivation's wording) should be noted in the ruling, so that the hit is not later cited for the stronger statement.
