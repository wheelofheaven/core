## The strongest objection

**1. The derivation does not follow from the claim.**
The record says so itself: "Without it the claim predicts nothing here."
The auxiliary assumption does not repair this. Granting that the designers left structure in the code beyond what function requires, three further premises are still needed and none is stated: that the structure is of the kind shCherbak and Makukov describe (decimal nucleon sums, multiples of 037); that it is detectable by their method; and that someone has by now confirmed it in a refereed journal.
The last is a fact about publishing behaviour. A design claim about events in deep time does not predict the contents of a citation index in 2026.
`woh-claim-0001` at 0.2.0 scopes in "the proposed design of terrestrial life" and says nothing about a signature.

**2. The criteria do not match the statement, and the hit was never in prospect.**
The statement is a conjunction: confirmed by an independent peer-reviewed analysis *and* not rebutted.
The first conjunct is false on the finding. A statement whose first half is false is not half true, yet the criteria label this outcome `partial`.
The Deriver's `prior_knowledge` reads "I do not expect a hit."
The only live question at registration was whether the result would fall under `partial` or `miss`, and that boundary depends on whether any critic took the trouble to publish in a journal, which is unrelated to whether the patterns are real.

**3. The observed outcome is the naturalistic alternative's prediction.**
The rival note reads: "an apparent artificial pattern is expected to dissolve under scrutiny or to remain an unreplicated curiosity ... the alternative does not require that anyone has bothered to publish a rebuttal."
Thirteen years, about thirty citations, no independent test, and a same-author follow-up: this is an unreplicated curiosity.
`discrimination: partial` was registered for a possible hit. For the outcome actually obtained, the framework reading (with its auxiliary) and the naturalistic reading are not separated at all; if anything the result is the one the rival named.

**4. "Unrebutted" carries no weight here.**
Formal rebuttals are written against claims a field takes seriously. Molecular evolutionists did not engage in journals; the engagement that exists is in blogs (Myers 2013, Neukamm 2021), and those posts are specific and uncontested in print.
The paper appeared in a planetary-science journal, outside the venue where genetic-code evolution is refereed.
Silence in a niche of thirty citations is the base-rate outcome for a paper that is ignored, and is no evidence that it withstood examination.

**5. The statistical objection, as a statistician or molecular evolutionist would put it.**

- *The test statistic was defined after the data were inspected.* The patterns were found by studying the one real code, and the simulation then asks how often random codes show those same classes of pattern (160 chosen balances, 9 ideogram versions). The P-value is conditional on the authors' choices: nucleon number over atom count, mass, volume or polarity; the side-chain/block split; decimal notation; the arrangements admitted as "straightforward". The space of patterns that would have been judged equally striking is not enumerated, so the reported figure is the probability of this result, where a P-value requires the probability of any result at least as remarkable. Panov and Filatov, who favour the patterns, concede the point: the probability "cannot be formulated correctly".
- *A free adjustment is applied to the data.* The balances do not hold for the actual molecules ("Physically, there are no strict balances in the code"). They appear after one nucleon in proline is moved from side chain to block. Random codes in the null were not each allowed their own best adjustment.
- *Part of the regularity is built in.* The standard amino-acid block has 74 nucleons, which is 2×37; proline's has 73 until the "activation key" makes it 74. Any block sum over n amino acids is then 74n, a multiple of 037 by construction. This is the substance of Neukamm's remark that "the authors get only results that they have already produced by their own rules."
- *The product is not a P-value.* p2 is the score of the euplotid variant, not of the standard code. p3 includes the probability of the single observed outcome (4:4, 0.375), which is not a tail probability; the observed split is not even the least likely of the three. Independence of the three factors is asserted from an unpublished study.
- *The null is not the evolutionary hypothesis.* Random assignment filtered by three ad hoc conditions does not model a code shaped by biosynthetic relatedness of amino acids, stereochemical affinity and descent. Amino-acid masses are not independent draws; they differ by recurring chemical increments, which produces arithmetic regularity without design.
- *The second criterion is unfalsifiable by the authors' admission.* They require patterns "inconsistent in principle with any natural process" and grant that this "might seem unverifiable".

I state these as the objection the relevant specialists would make. I have not re-run the simulation, and none of these points has been published in a refereed journal, which is exactly why the classification is what it is.

**6. The nearest thing to independent support is not independent and not support of the test.**
Panov and Filatov thank Makukov for "an extremely fruitful discussion"; Filatov works in the same small circle.
Their "confirmed" prediction, that variant codes lose the signatures, is not diagnostic. Signatures selected because they hold in the standard code will fail in any code that differs from it, whether they arose by design or by chance.

## What the result does and does not show

It shows that the claim of an artificial signature in the genetic code has stood for thirteen years without an independent peer-reviewed confirmation, and without a refereed rebuttal; that its one refereed extension is by the same authors; and that the only independent authors who examined it at length rejected its probability argument while remaining agnostic about the patterns.

It does not show that the genetic code contains an unexplained regularity. Nobody outside the authors' circle has tested that in print.
It does not show that the claim withstood scrutiny. It was not given any in the venues the protocol counts.
It does not bear on `woh-claim-0001`, which predicts nothing about this target without premises the record does not hold.
It must not be described anywhere as "an unrebutted peer-reviewed finding" without the second half of the classification.

A void must not make the finding disappear. The Locator's work is sound and is the first record in core of the state of this literature. It is usable as a source note, and it is consistent with what `woh-claim-0036` already records about design detection.

## What the ruling turns on

1. **Whether an expectation may rest on an auxiliary premise that the Deriver says carries the whole derivation.** The methodology provides `auxiliary_assumptions` for this case and reports such results apart. If the founder holds that the mechanism covers this record, the expectation is not void, and the next question applies.
2. **If it is not void, what the outcome is called.** The registered criteria say `partial`; the registered statement is false in its first conjunct; the registered rival predicted this result. A ruling that reads `partial` as a near-hit would misstate all three.
3. **Whether the state of a literature is a fit target at all.** If such targets are kept, the partial/miss boundary should not depend on publication venue. That is a question for the methodology, not for this record.
4. **Residual search risk.** Google Scholar and ADS remain unreached by Locator and Critic alike. Three indexes agree, so the risk of a missed refereed rebuttal or confirmation is small, but it is not zero; a rebuttal would move the mechanical assessment to `meets_miss`.
