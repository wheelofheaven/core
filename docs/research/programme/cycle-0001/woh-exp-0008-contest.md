# Contest: woh-exp-0008

- Date: 2026-11-22
- Assessment contested: `meets_hit` (discrimination `none`, novelty `unrecorded`)
- Recommendation: downgrade

The registered criteria do not settle two passages in Raël's own voice, so
`mixed` describes the finding better than `meets_hit`. What survives is
narrower than the registered statement: the books attribute *presently
living* terrestrial forms to the Elohim and attribute no new kind to an
undirected process. Discrimination stays `none`. Novelty is `unrecorded`
only in part, because the central 1979 passage is already quoted in core
under `woh-claim-0074`.

## Verification

`i18n.en` was not used.

- **Context read in full:** LWTE-1:1–18, 1:26–28, 1:57–70, 1:104–108,

- **Independent screen.** I searched both books for origin wording that

  did change my reading of one passage the Locator had seen and classed
  `designed`: LWTE-1:15.

### LWTE-1:107, in context

The paragraph is the third step of an answer to "A quoi cela sert-il de
vivre?" (1:104), which judges life at four levels: the infinite (1:105),
the Elohim (1:106), human society (1:107), oneself (1:108). At the second
level humans matter because the Elohim made them "à leur image". At the
third:

> nous sommes l'aboutissement d'une longue lignée de survivants qui ont
> échappé aux épidémies, aux guerres, et qui ont fait de nous le fruit
> d'une longue sélection naturelle.

This is Raël's commentary voice, which the founder's voice rule
(`source-notes/lets-welcome-the-extraterrestrials.md`, 2026-11-08) treats
as canon. It names natural selection, applies it to humans, and says it
made present humans what they are. It asserts no new kind and names no
trait. The Locator's `ambiguous` is defensible. So is a stricter reading:
the class definition in the brief is "origin *or transformation* of some
terrestrial organism attributed, in the book's own voice, to a process
without their intervention", and a population shaped by differential
survival of epidemics and wars is a transformation by such a process.

### Terrusse, LWTE-4:11, 4:13, 4:16, in context

The essay (LWTE-4:1–25, section I, "Obscurantisme évolutionniste et mythe
néodarwinien") argues from Rostand and Muller that mutations are
overwhelmingly harmful and never build anything new, and ends: "La vie
sur terre n'est pas le fruit du hasard et de la nécessité, mais le
résultat d'une intervention extérieure, de l'intervention des Elohim, nos
créateurs" (4:25). Within that argument it grants that mutations arise
"dans la population" (4:11) and "font varier la taille, la morphologie, la
couleur des mouches" (4:16), and that "les mutants restent toujours au
sein de l'espèce primitive" (4:16). 4:13 concerns laboratory mutants.

None of the three is an `undirected` transformation in the book's own
voice, for two independent reasons. The content is the standard
within-kind concession, made in order to deny that it goes further. And
the voice is not the book's: chapter 4 is titled "Commentaires et
Témoignages de Raëliens", the essay is signed, Raël closes his own text at
LWTE-3:88, and the source note records that "the weight of chapter 4's
signed contributions by other authors stays open". The finding's count of
39 `designed` passages for LWTE includes eight from chapter 4. They are
not needed for the hit, but the count should not be read as 39 statements
by the book.

### LWTE-1:15, which the finding classes `designed`

The question at 1:13 is why animal bones hundreds of thousands of years
old are found if the Elohim created life 25,000 years ago. The answer
(1:14–15) first states the creation ("ils y ont créé toutes les formes de
vie que nous connaissons, y compris l'homme") and then:

> Cela ne veut pas dire que dix ou vingt mille ans avant leur venue il n'y
> avait pas, sur Terre, une autre création qui fut détruite par une
> catastrophe naturelle ou artificielle. […] Car la vie qui se trouve
> actuellement Terre n'est pas la première qui y fut créée et n'est pas la
> dernière qui y sera créée. Il y a eu une infinité de créations sur notre
> planète et une infinité de destructions dues pour la plupart au manque
> de sagesse de ceux qui étaient l'équivalent de notre Humanité.

("actuellement Terre" sic in the file.) In Raël's own voice, terrestrial
organisms lived on Earth before the Elohim came, and they were made by
someone else: the creator is unnamed, the verb is passive, and the
illustration in the same paragraph has "des extra-terrestres" doing it.
The old bones are assigned to those earlier creations. The paragraph the
finding quotes as `designed`, scope "both", attributes the origin of some
terrestrial organisms to a creation that was not the Elohim's.

The registered classes have no place for this. `designed` is defined as
"origin attributed to creation by the Elohim", which 1:15 denies for
earlier life. `undirected` is defined as "a process without their
intervention", which a creation by others literally is, though the label
plainly means something else. `ambiguous` was available and was the right
class. The finding mentions the earlier creations under adjacent
observations and does not connect them to the question.

The wording elsewhere agrees with the narrower reading and shows it is not
a slip: "toutes les formes de vie *que nous connaissons*" (1:14); "Chaque
être vivant présent sur terre *actuellement* a été créé par les Elohim"
(1:149). LWTE-1:147 ("Les Elohim sont venus sur la terre alors qu'il n'y
avait pas de vie") is compatible only if the earlier creations had been
wholly destroyed, which 1:15 says.

### Other points

- ETTMTTP-2:51 ("comme sur terre, nous avons créé ici toute vie") carries
  the 1975 book's coverage of non-human life almost alone; 3:102 ("ceux
  qui l'ont créée", of nature) supports it. The 1975 book's leg of the hit
  is real but thin, and both passages sit in direct speech attributed to

- ETTMTTP-2:123 was rightly kept out of `undirected`. Descent from unions

- LWTE-4:192, a testimony, says the Elohim "ont eu une évolution

  and is not Raël's voice.

## The strongest objection

**The hit is scored on a statement the 1979 book does not make at the
registered breadth, and the one loose concession the Deriver named in
advance as the likely miss is in the text.**

The expectation says each book attributes "the origin of terrestrial
living things" to the Elohim, and that neither states "that any
terrestrial organism arose or was transformed into its present kind
without their intervention". The derivation gives the reason: a contrary
passage "would mean the canon itself does not carry the proposition at the
breadth the claim states".

LWTE-1:15 is such a passage on the first half. The 1979 book carries the
proposition for the forms of life now living, and expressly not for all
terrestrial life. It does so at the exact point where the creation account
meets the fossil record. The criteria missed it because they assumed the
only alternative to design by the Elohim was no design.

LWTE-1:107 bears on the second half. The Deriver wrote before looking: "a
miss could come from a concession about change within kinds worded
loosely." The text contains that concession in Raël's voice, about humans,
under the name "sélection naturelle". Yet the protocol tells the Locator
to report "change within created kinds" as an adjacent observation, and
the statement speaks of transformation "into its present kind". The record
points both ways about whether within-kind change counts. The Recorder's
charter provides `mixed` for a finding the criteria do not settle. Scoring
it `meets_hit` resolves the record's own ambiguity in the expectation's
favour.

The alternatives, stated as their proponents would. A source-critical
reader says: between 1974 and 1979 the author met the objection from old
bones and from radiometric dates, and the 1979 book answers it by
conceding prior life (1:15) and denying the dating methods (1:28); the
anti-evolution material is absent from the 1975 book (no occurrence of
singe, Darwin or mutation, as the finding reports) and arrives in 1979
with the distinction, familiar from creationist argument, between
variation inside a kind and the origin of kinds; the book itself
recommends a work titled *Evolution ou Création* (1:28). That reading expects a text that holds the creation thesis fixed
and adjusts around it, which is what is there. It is not `silent` in the
way the rival note says; it predicts the `same` headline result and it
explains the concessions. A biologist would add that the 1979 position on
mutation and selection is the special-creation position with fixed kinds,
and that agreement between an author's two books on it has no bearing on
whether it is correct. Neither alternative is embarrassed by the hit, so
`discrimination: none` stands.

**Novelty.** The record declares `unrecorded` and discloses that
`woh-claim-0074` cites LWTE-1:148–149. The disclosure is candid but
understates the overlap. The 0074 record quotes LWTE-1:149 for the very
proposition tested here: every living being on Earth was made by the
Elohim from one base "brique". The methodology counts a target as
`retrodiction` when core records it "under this claim or any other claim".
For the principal LWTE passage the expectation restates recorded ground.
The survey of the whole of both books, the `rejected_view` material and
1:15 and 1:107 are new to core.

**Scope.** The same defect as `woh-exp-0007`. `woh-claim-0001` excludes
"the report layer itself", and this expectation tests nothing else.
`woh-claim-0070` is scoped to `TBWTT-1:53` and `TBWTT-2:5` and excludes
"every other statement in the canon". No claim record's scope contains
the result. The derivation's bridge ("if the report has one referent, the
later books must not concede…") needs the unstated premise that the later
books transmit the report faithfully; the record lists no auxiliary
assumption. Here the scope question has more consequence than in 0007,
because the narrowing found at LWTE-1:15 touches the wording of
`woh-claim-0001` itself ("responsible for designing terrestrial life"),
while 0001 disclaims "every detail reported by the Raëlian corpus".

## What the result does and does not show

It shows, as a source report about the digitized French text of two
books:

- both books attribute humans and the non-human life now on Earth to
  creation by the Elohim, the 1975 book briefly and in general terms, the
  1979 book at length;
- neither attributes the origin of any kind of organism to chance or to
  evolution; the 1979 book calls those who do "faux prophètes" (1:196);
- the 1979 book, in Raël's voice, places earlier life on Earth before the
  Elohim and assigns it to other creations (1:15);
- the 1979 book, in Raël's voice, calls present humans the fruit of a long
  natural selection (1:107), and holds that species only reproduce the
  code of their first made exemplar (1:149);
- a signed essay by another author in the same volume grants mutation and
  variation inside a species and denies that it produces new organisms.

It does not show:

- that life was designed, or that the canon's position on evolution is
  right. The derivation says so, and RFC 0009 leaves the question open;
- that the canon carries "all terrestrial life" at full breadth. On the
  1979 book's own account it carries present life;
- independent support of any kind. Two books by the author of the first
  book are the same tradition again; the LWTE source note counts the three
  as one source;
- what the 1979 printing said. The digitization follows a reprint of 2000
  or later (the footnotes at LWTE-1:21 and 3:26 cite an "édition 2000"),
  and no first printing has been collated.

### How much the three limits matter

- **Term screening.** The Locator left 115 ETTMTTP and 180 LWTE paragraphs
  unread in full and screened them by sentence. For a negative result that
  is the weak point, but the twenty protocol terms plus the supplement
  cover the vocabulary in which French states an origin, and my separate
  screen found no missed passage. The actual failure was not one of
  search. The passage that matters most (1:15) was found, read and placed
  in a class it does not fit.
- **Reprint.** A real limit, of moderate weight. Later editions of a
  movement's texts are where awkward concessions are most likely to be
  cut or added. Nothing in the text suggests doctrinal revision of the
  passages at issue, and the 1:15 concession is the kind of thing a
  harmonising editor would remove, not add. The finding holds for the
  digitized state.
- **`speaker` field.** It decides nothing here by itself, since the
  Locator assigned voices by reading and the assignments I checked are
  right. It matters as a warning about counts: every LWTE paragraph is
  tagged "Narrator", so no count of "the book's own voice" can be taken
  from the field, and the weight of chapter 4 is an open founder question.
  Both passages this contest turns on are in chapter 1, in Raël's answers,
  where the voice is not in doubt.

## What the ruling turns on

1. **How LWTE-1:15 is read against the registered statement.** If "the
   origin of terrestrial living things" means the forms now living, the
   statement holds and 1:15 is a clarification. If it means terrestrial
   life as such, as `woh-claim-0001` words it, the 1979 book does not
   carry it and the result holds only in the narrower form.
2. **Whether within-kind change by natural selection counts.** The record
   is inconsistent on this (statement and protocol against the Deriver's
   declared expectation of where a miss would come from). If it counts,
   LWTE-1:107 meets the miss criterion. If it does not, 1:107 is an
   adjacent observation and should be recorded as the canon's own
   acknowledgement of selection inside the human kind.
3. **Whether chapter 4 is the book's voice.** If it is, Terrusse's
   within-species mutation joins 1:107 as a second concession of the same
   kind. If it is not, the essay drops out of both the `designed` count
   and the question.
4. **Whether the expectation is scored under `woh-claim-0001`.** As in
   0007: the claim excludes the report layer, and no record's scope holds
   this result. The choice is between `void` for scope, a narrowed result
   under 0001 with the fidelity premise named as an auxiliary assumption,
   and recording the content as source-report material outside this
   claim's scoreboard.

In every case the class stays `discrimination: none`, and the novelty is
`unrecorded` only for the survey and not for LWTE-1:149.
