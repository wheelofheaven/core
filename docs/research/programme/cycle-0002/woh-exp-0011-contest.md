# Contest: woh-exp-0011

- Date: 2026-11-22
- Assessment contested: `meets_miss` (two `sense_dependent` cases classified `french_only`: TBWTT-2:42, « science » at Genesis 3:22; TBWTT-2:62, « étages » at Genesis 6:16)
- Recommendation: downgrade

The miss stands on the registered criteria and cannot be removed by reclassification without also undoing the baseline the expectation itself fixed. It belongs in a weaker class than the record gives it. Of the two deciding cases, the firm one (Genesis 3:22) is the reading already recorded at TBWTT-2:30 occurring a second time, on a passage cycle-0001 had flagged and the Deriver expected to decide the result; the new one (Genesis 6:16) is the weaker of the two and sits on the line the protocol drew between a sense of a French word and a re-description of the thing. The miss falls first on the auxiliary assumption, and the framework reading, stated as its proponents would, expects the same result as the source-critical alternative.

## The strongest objection

**1. The firm case is the known reading again, and the result was foreseen.**

The expectation excluded TBWTT-2:30 "so that the result is not decided in advance". TBWTT-2:42 is twelve paragraphs later, in the same narrative, on the same French word from the same translator for the same Hebrew root, and it serves the same reading (the tree is scientific knowledge withheld). Cycle-0001's digest already lists it: "A parallel at TBWTT-2:42 (Gen 3:22) is unchecked." The record's `prior_knowledge` expects "a miss through TBWTT-2:42", and the protocol names the paragraph to the Locator ("Do classify TBWTT-2:42 if it qualifies"). The Deriver hid nothing and left a known counter-example in scope, which is the opposite of tuning. But a miss obtained this way adds a confirmation of an earlier observation and little that is new. The methodology's rule for families applies: 2:30 and 2:42 are one result with two instances. `novelty: unrecorded` is true of the passage's content and not of its existence or its expected outcome.

**2. The new case is the weaker one.**

Genesis 6:16, as `french_only`, stated at its strongest: the Hebrew has no noun at all, only "lower ones, second ones and third ones", which BDB reads as storeys in the ark. « Étages » is supplied by the translator, the singular « l'inférieur, le second et le troisième » is his as well, and French happens to use that same noun for the sections of a rocket. The book quotes the verse for no other purpose than to put « en étages » beside « la fusée à trois étages ». English ("three-stage rocket" against "storeys" or "decks") and German (*Stufen* against *Stockwerke*) do not allow the move. The verbal echo is French.

As `image_step`, stated at its strongest: the commentary has already replaced the ark by a rocket at 2:58. Given that step, three superposed divisions of the vessel become the three superposed sections of the rocket, and the Hebrew ordinals, having no noun to restrict them to floors, carry that as well as the French does. On this view the French word makes the remark neat and does none of the inferential work. The protocol's own definition of (b), "re-describes the thing the verse names as something else", covers it.

I think the Locator's class is defensible and the alternative is too. What is not defensible is to treat this case as carrying the same weight as a pun. It is a second-order detail of an image step, and the classification rule decides it more than the evidence does.

**3. The two « science » cases are classed on different grounds.**

At Genesis 3:22 the Locator's ground is syntactic: the Hebrew is a verb with an object, the French is a noun, and the noun stands alone only because of the ellipsis. At Jeremiah 10:14 the Hebrew is itself a noun standing alone (מִדַּעַת), and the Locator classes on the lexical ground that BDB gives "skill (in workmanship)" and "creative skill" under the word. Each decision can be defended. Together they are not one rule. On the lexical ground alone (does the commentary need TLFi sense II, systematised science, which no BDB sense of the root reaches?) the two cases go together, and the finding says so in its own caveats. Either both are `shared`, because "knowledge" is in the Hebrew and the narrowing to scientific knowledge is interpretation, or both are `french_only`.

The registered baseline settles which. The expectation's statement counts TBWTT-2:30 as an instance of a sense "the quoted French word has and the Hebrew word at that place lacks", and 2:30 is the same word for the same root. Holding 2:42 `shared` would require holding 2:30 `shared`, which contradicts the statement the test was built on. So consistency runs toward `french_only` for 2:42, and arguably for 3:276 too. The inconsistency is real but does not help the expectation.

The best case for `shared` at Genesis 3:22 deserves stating once more, because it is not weak. Hebrew has one root for knowing of every kind; a line of critical commentary takes "good and evil" as a pair standing for everything, so that the phrase means comprehensive knowledge; and Dhorme's « science » is, by his own notes, sense I. A reader with Segond's « connaissance » in front of him could reach "scientific knowledge" by the same interpretive step. Against it: the book removes the complement that fixes the Hebrew sense, and what remains, « grâce à la science », reads in modern French as sense II. The commentary's vocabulary (« un moyen scientifique », « un savant », « un bond scientifique », and at 2:45 « connaissances scientifiques ») follows that sense. The step is made easy by the translator's noun and the book's cut, and neither is in the Hebrew.

**4. Whether the outcome depends on the borderlines.**

It does not depend on the nine borderline `shared` cases or on the twelve movable `image_step` cases. Moving `shared` cases out lowers the `sense_dependent` count, which matters only to the hit threshold. Moving `image_step` cases in can only add: those the Locator checked would be `shared`, and the unchecked ones (2:7, 3:73, 3:104, 3:220) could add `french_only` cases but cannot remove one.

**5. A hit would have shown little, so the test is one-sided.**

The record says a hit "would count against" the source-critical alternative. That overstates it. Dhorme's translation is literal by design, so a writer working only from Dhorme would mostly lean on senses the Hebrew also has. The eight `shared` cases are what both accounts predict. Only `french_only` cases separate anything, and they separate only if the auxiliary assumption holds. `discrimination: partial` is fair as registered; in effect the test could count modestly against the claim's reading of the speaker and could hardly have counted for it.

## What the result does and does not show

**It shows** that in two further places, as in the two already known, a reading in the first book is carried by the wording of the French translation and by the book's own ellipsis, and that in one of them (Genesis 3:22) the translator's page can be laid beside the book and the cut seen. It shows that such places are rare: 2 of 166 classified quotations, 4 of 168 with the known pair, 2 of 13 among the cases where any word is leaned on. It confirms what cycle-0001 reported: 150 of 166 commentaries work by replacing the thing named, where no test of wording reaches.

**It does not show** a rate that either account predicted. No one registered what proportion a French composition from Dhorme would produce, or what a Hebrew-literate teacher using a French Bible for a French listener would produce, and there is no comparison text. The four instances are not independent: two are one reading of « science », and all four pass through one translator. The eight `shared` cases are not evidence that the commentary rests on the Hebrew, for the reason given in objection 5.

**The auxiliary assumption.** The miss counts against the claim only if the speaker's explanations are meant to rest on the original wording.

- As a defender of the framework reading would put it: the book never offers lexicography. The speaker asks the narrator to take up his own Bible (TBWTT-2:1, 2:2), says the text has been « un peu déformées par les copistes » and loaded with « phrases mystiques et inutiles » (2:2, 2:3), calls one passage among « les plus déformés » (3:69), and says of his own activity « les parties de la Bible que je vais vous traduire » (2:3). He is conveying what happened, to a Frenchman, through the Frenchman's Bible, and he picks the words in front of his listener that let the point land. « Sains » for « saints » at 3:192 is offered openly as what "they should have written". Wording that works in French is what a teacher in French produces. On this account the expectation asks of the book a philology it does not claim, and the miss falls on the added premise.
- As a proponent of the source-critical alternative would put it: the same book grounds its authority in the letter of the Hebrew. It says the Bible was copied « sans rien en changer, même pas le plus petit signe » so that « le sens profond est resté » (2:3); it says what Elohim « veut dire en Hébreu » (2:5) and « littéralement ... en Hébreu » (3:251); it may say of Genesis 3:22 itself that the sentence is « à peu près intacte du document originel » (2:41). A speaker who appeals to the Hebrew when it suits and whose readings elsewhere turn on a French translator's noun, a French homophone and the book's own ellipses is what composition in French from that Bible looks like. "The text is deformed" absorbs any mismatch whatever, and a defence that absorbs everything predicts nothing.

Both can be held on the text. The book's statements at 2:2 and 2:3 pull in both directions within two paragraphs, as the record's auxiliary note already says. The auxiliary was declared at registration and has textual support, so the derivation is not careless. It is not forced by claim 0001 either: the claim identifies the speaker's civilization and does not state how he quotes.

**Psalm 19:5.** Verified: the Masoretic verse ends בָּהֶם, "in them"; « sur la mer » has no counterpart, and the commentary at TBWTT-3:245 (the mass of land raised when ocean covered the Earth) depends on it. The UBS translators' handbook on Psalm 19:3-5, as reproduced at tips.translation.bible, reads: "Instead of 'in them' of the Masoretic text, some prefer to follow a text meaning 'in the sea' (see Weiser)." So the French follows a modern conjectural emendation; it is a text-critical decision by a translator and no accident of French vocabulary. I could not check Dhorme's Psalms or Weiser.

This does not belong to the expectation. The registered question asks whether a Hebrew word has the sense the commentary uses; here there is no Hebrew word, and the protocol rightly sent it to the adjacent list. It must not be added to the count. It belongs to the quotation layer (`woh-claim-0066`), whose `alt-0066-partial-mediation` asks for per-quotation collation: first whether Dhorme prints « sur la mer », then on what authority. It is also the datum in this finding that bears most directly on what the derivation was after, since here the reading depends on the French Bible against the transmitted Hebrew text, and the two accounts answer it in a way worth recording. The source-critical reader says the speaker is following a twentieth-century scholar's guess. The framework reader may say the guess restores what the copyists lost. Neither can be scored on the present record.

**The two unassigned cases.** The gloss of Elohim (TBWTT-2:5, 3:251) is the reverse situation: a sense asserted for the Hebrew word that BDB and Strong's do not give. The criteria have no class for it, the Locator was right not to force one, and it does not affect the assessment.

**Search.** Adequate for a miss, which needs one case: both chapters were read in full by one Locator, and every decision is stated so the count can be redone. Not adequate as a census of French-only wording, since the Hebrew of the 150 `image_step` quotations was not compared; the finding says so.

## What the ruling turns on

1. **The auxiliary assumption.** If the founder holds that the speaker's explanations are meant to rest on the original text, the miss touches the claim's reading of the speaker at subclaim B. If he holds that the book teaches through the listener's French Bible and says so, the miss touches the added premise only and leaves a plain record: the first book's exegesis is, in a small number of places, exegesis of Dhorme's French. Either way that sentence is true of the source and is a matter for `woh-claim-0066`, whose scope already says quotation-level readings "inherit Dhorme's philological choices".
2. **One reading or two.** Whether TBWTT-2:42 counts as a further instance or as the instance at 2:30 recurring. If the latter, the only new case is « étages ».
3. **Sense of a word or re-description.** Whether « fusée à trois étages » is a use of a French polysemy or a detail of the ark-as-rocket image. If the founder takes 2:42 as the known reading recurring and 6:16 as an image step, the registered statement ("no further reading ... depends on a sense ... the Hebrew word lacks") would hold only in a narrower form than the criteria as applied allow; if he takes either case as the Locator classed it, the criteria are met as assessed.
4. **Follow-up that does not wait on the ruling's direction.** The source note `dhorme-bible.md` should record the Genesis 3:22 wording and Dhorme's note on « Il faut éviter », and correct the extent of the extract. Psalm 19:5 and Genesis 6:16 need Dhorme's text before any use. These are records for the Recorder or a later cycle; I have not edited them.
