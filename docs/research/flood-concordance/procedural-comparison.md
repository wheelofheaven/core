# Decisions and preservation in five flood accounts

The five texts named in claim 0010 support comparison of destruction,
warning and continuity. They do not all attest the complete sequence of
assembly resolution, binding oath, internal dissent and detailed rescue
instructions. The differences concern who authorizes survival, when an
objection occurs, what an oath binds, and what is preserved.

This is a source comparison for the review of
[woh-claim-0010 v0.1.0](../../../model/claims/woh-claim-0010.json), its
[specification](../../framework/flood-as-reset.md) and
[evidence map](../../evidence/woh-claim-0010-evidence.md).
[Claim 0060 v0.1.0](../../../model/claims/woh-claim-0060.json) governs the
comparison's evidence discipline. Both records remain accepted at their
existing versions. This note does not revise them, the public concordance,
the library translations or the flood essay. It supplies the requested
feature comparison before a proposed claim revision.

## Scope and reading rules

The sample is the claim's five named texts: the Sumerian Flood Story,
Atrahasis, Gilgamesh XI, Genesis 6–9 and the Book of the Watchers. It is
not a new sample of independent worldwide traditions. For the Watchers,
the inspected narrative scope is 1 Enoch 6–16; no finding here describes
every flood passage elsewhere in 1 Enoch.

Read each cell as an observation about the named edition and passage.
**Attested** includes an explicitly reported action; **qualified** identifies
restoration, attribution or timing that changes its force; **not stated**
means that the inspected scope supplies no statement of the feature;
**damaged** means that loss obstructs the question. Neither silence nor
damage is proof of absence. A directive to warn someone is not automatically
a narrated delivery of that warning.

An oath requires its parties and object. An assembly requires more than a
decision by one speaker. Dissent against the flood must be distinguished
from regret, covert assistance, intercession for another party and criticism
after the event. A preservation instruction must be distinguished from an
outcome or a later description of the survivor.

## Instructions and preservation

| Account | Construction or escape instructions | What preservation refers to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sumerian Flood Story** | The construction scene is **damaged**. S1's gap summary cannot supply preserved instructions or animal pairs. | Ziusudra receives a preservation epithet **after** the event, concerning animals and human seed. S1, 180′–185′. This is not a surviving loading order. |
| **Atrahasis** | Construction directions, partly restored; animal and family boarding. A2, C2 i 22′–26′; C1 i 28′–37′; ii 34″–42″. | Living beings and family; the corresponding lines remain damaged. Do not substitute an unattested all-species formula for these particulars. |
| **Gilgamesh XI** | Boat dimensions and living cargo are specified. G1, 11.23–31, 11.80–86. | Living beings, kin, animals and craftspeople. G1 brackets the seed wording at 11.27; the instructions to keep life alive do not depend on that bracketed wording alone. |
| **Genesis 6–9** | Materials, dimensions, compartments, provisions and boarding. B6, 6:14–21; B7, 7:1–9. | Noah's household and male/female animals. The seed-preservation expression at 7:3 concerns the birds in that sentence; 6:19–20 gives the broader animal instruction. |
| **Book of the Watchers** | An order to instruct Noah in escape, without boat specifications or a loading list in 10:1–3. | **His seed**: Noah's continuing lineage. It is not an instruction to gather the seed of all living species. E1, 10:3. |

## Passage attribution requiring follow-up

The core specification and evidence map associate the named alternative
instruments with Atrahasis. The explicit lion, wolf, famine and pestilence
list is securely located in **Gilgamesh XI 187–195**, in Ea's speech after
the flood. That citation should travel with any use of the list.

The selected Sippar Atrahasis edition has a partly restored criticism and
an injunction to direct punishment at the offender at **C1 vi 20–26**,
followed by a gap at vi 28–37. It does not print that four-part list in
the inspected passage. This finding does not establish its absence from
every Atrahasis witness or reconstruction.

The project text adds a concrete reconciliation task:

- `www.wheelofheaven.world/data/library/atrahasis-woh/chapter-3.json`,
  **ATRA-WOH-III:vi.21–24**, prints lion and famine alternatives.
- The following segment, `the-population-limits`, has a
  `preceding_lacuna.text` describing lion, wolf, famine and plague as a
  compressed transition.
- These project locators do not align with the same numbered lines in
  A2. They must not be treated as equivalent references merely because
  the tablet and column numbers resemble one another.

This is a flagged edition/alignment discrepancy, not an adjudicated
original-language correction. Resolving it requires the particular
Lambert–Millard, Foster or other edition behind the project reconstruction.
The local project's metadata itself retains a verification-pending
qualification. The present comparison uses A1/A2 for Atrahasis and G1 for
the explicit four alternatives, without changing the library text.

## What the comparison permits

The comparison supports a narrower recurring narrative arrangement:
destruction is announced and human continuity is provided for. Even that
comparison has to preserve the Sumerian losses and the Watchers' brief
commission scene. The arrangement is realized through different relations
of authority: conflict and circumvention in some passages, authorized
preservation in others.

The strongest reading in favour of the existing claim is functional:
these different scenes may perform related narrative work despite their
different actors and timing. That is a comparative inference. It does not
make an authorized warning a dissenting warning, Noah's descendants an
all-species collection, or a post-flood promise an oath to cause the flood.

The opposite overreach would be to infer that no relationship between the
accounts remains once those distinctions are made. Shared motifs and
literary relationships can still be investigated. This exercise does not
re-establish a linear borrowing route or count the five texts as five
independent confirmations. The relationship controls in
[the concordance](concordance.json) continue to apply.

For a later revision of claim 0010, the issues are now specific: replace
the compressed sequence with passage-specific observations, relocate the
explicit alternative-disaster list, and distinguish the scale and timing
of preservation. The canonical reset reading is a separate layer; no
historical-event test or revision of that reading was undertaken here.
