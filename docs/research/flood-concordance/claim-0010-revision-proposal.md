# Proposed revision of woh-claim-0010 (0.1.0 → 0.2.0)

This is a proposal for founder review. It changes no record. The accepted
[record](../../../model/claims/woh-claim-0010.json),
[specification](../../framework/flood-as-reset.md) and
[evidence map](../../evidence/woh-claim-0010-evidence.md) stay at 0.1.0
until a ruling is recorded in an ADR.

It rests on two inputs:

1. the [procedural comparison](procedural-comparison.md) of 2026-11-20,
   which tested the record's procedural layer against the five named
   texts; and
2. a re-reading on 2026-11-21 of the canon's own Flood passages, which
   tests the record's canonical layer. The passages are quoted below from
   the project digitizations.

Both layers need revision. The record compresses the ancient texts into
one sequence that none of them states in full, and it compresses the
canon into a "reset decision within the Council's governance" that the
canon does not state either.

## 1. What the canon states

Two canon accounts are in hand. They are in different voices and should
be cited separately.

**TBWTT 2:55–68** (the Message of 1973, Yahweh speaking):

- The decision is taken by the home world against its own creation:
  "All this was becoming dangerous in the eyes of the distant planet.
  Scientific progress was enormous on Earth, and they decided to suppress
  their creation" (2:55). "So they decided, from their distant planet, to
  destroy all life on Earth by sending nuclear missiles" (2:58).
- The stated motive is the danger of human progress, not wickedness in a
  moral sense. The canon re-reads Genesis 6:5: "Evil — that is, the
  desire to become a people equal to its creators, a scientific and
  independent people" (2:57).
- The preservation is the exiles' act, done because they were forewarned:
  "But the exiles, forewarned of the matter, had asked Noah to build a
  rocket that was to orbit the Earth during the cataclysm" (2:58). The
  pairs are "an image"; "it is enough to have a living cell of each
  species, male and female" (2:58). The craft is three-staged and carries
  "a couple of each human race of the Earth" besides Noah (2:62).
- Afterwards "the creators ... promised never to try to destroy them in
  the future" (2:66); each race is returned to its place of creation and
  each animal "recreated from the cell preserved in the ark" (2:68). "The
  people of the planet were afraid" on learning that life had survived
  (2:74).

**LWTE 2:25–28** ("New Revelations", the 1975 account in Raël's
narration):

- Satan wants all created beings destroyed as dangerous "due to their
  violence" and accumulates proof of human aggression (2:25).
- "Seeing the proofs of such a slaughter, brought by Satan before the
  Council of the Eternals, Yahweh decides to do what Satan asks, that is
  to say, totally destroy the life that has been created on Earth" (2:26).
- "When Lucifer's group learns that their fabulous creation is soon to be
  destroyed, they decide that they cannot allow that"; they help Noah
  build a craft in orbit carrying "a few men and women and the genetic
  codes of some animal species" (2:27).
- Only then do the Elohim learn that they too were created scientifically.
  "They then decided never again to destroy humanity, and helped
  Lucifer's group to re-implant the life forms preserved in the 'ark'."
  Satan "bows before the majority of those who, behind Yahweh, think the
  contrary within the council of the Eternals" (2:28).

**Consequence for the record.** In the canon, the Council's decision is
total destruction, taken on an accusation and decided by its president.
The preservation is an act *against* that decision by the exiled
faction. The re-establishment becomes Council policy only *after* the
event, by a reversal. "Deliberate reset within the Council's governance"
merges the two factions and puts the outcome inside the decision. That
contradicts the canon and the accepted
[woh-claim-0008](../../../model/claims/woh-claim-0008.json), which sets
the Flood inside the conflict between the home-world Council and the
exiled-creator alliance. The wiki derivatives (`great-flood`, `noah`,
`noahs-ark`) and the essay's canon section already give the two-faction
account. The record is the outlier.

The two accounts give the motive differently: the danger of scientific
progress (TBWTT 2:55–57) and the danger of violence (LWTE 2:25–26). Both
are danger, not moral retribution. The record should register both and
not harmonize them.

## 2. What the ancient texts state

From the procedural comparison:

- **Assembly decision.** Attested or recalled in the Sumerian Flood Story
  (qualified by restorations), Atrahasis and Gilgamesh XI. Not narrated
  in Genesis 6–9 or the Book of the Watchers (1 Enoch 6–16), where a
  single authority commands.
- **Oath.** Before the flood in the Sumerian account (object not fully
  preserved), Atrahasis (X-MB witness) and Gilgamesh XI. Absent as an
  oath to destroy in Genesis, where 6:18 and 9:8–17 are covenant and
  promise. The Watchers' oath at 1 Enoch 6:3–5 concerns their own
  undertaking, not the flood.
- **Irrevocability.** A pre-flood formula is rendered only in the
  Sumerian S1 excerpt, and its S2 lines are restored. Atrahasis calls the
  verdict final *after* the destruction.
- **Dissent.** Before the flood only in Atrahasis (Enki against the oath,
  partly restored, A1 H9–16). In Gilgamesh, Ea gives covert help before
  and rebukes Enlil after (11.180–197). In the Sumerian account there is
  grief and private deliberation, not preserved protest. There is no
  dissenting rescuer in Genesis or the Watchers.
- **The alternative-disaster list** (lion, wolf, famine, Erra/plague) is
  securely located in **Gilgamesh XI**, in Ea's post-flood speech. The
  record attributes it to Atrahasis. The Sippar edition consulted does
  not print it there. The project's `ATRA-WOH-III:vi.21–24` does, but
  cannot be aligned with a known edition without the Lambert–Millard or
  Foster text, which the project does not hold. This proposal moves the
  citation to Gilgamesh XI and leaves the Atrahasis question open.
- **Preservation.** Living beings and family (Atrahasis, damaged);
  living beings, kin, animals, craftsmen (Gilgamesh); household and
  animal pairs (Genesis); Noah's own line, "his seed" (1 Enoch 10:3).
  "The seed of all living things" is not one shared formula.

## 3. Proposed record changes

### Title

From "The Flood as deliberate reset" to **"The Flood as a contested
decision"**. *For ruling (Q1).*

### Statement

> The canon presents the Flood as a decision of the Elohim home world to
> destroy all life on Earth: in *The Book Which Tells the Truth* taken
> "from their distant planet" because human progress had become
> dangerous, in the *New Revelations* decided by Yahweh before the Council
> of the Eternals on Satan's evidence of human violence. The exiled
> creators, Lucifer's group, acted against that decision: they had Noah
> build a craft that orbited through the cataclysm carrying a few humans
> and a living cell of each species. After the event the Council resolved
> never again to destroy humanity and helped re-implant the preserved
> life. The reset is therefore the outcome of a decision and its
> circumvention, not the content of the decision. The ancient flood
> narratives preserve parts of this arrangement unevenly. The Sumerian
> Flood Story, Atrahasis and Gilgamesh XI keep an assembly decision, an
> oath and a god who saves the survivor around the decree, with explicit
> objection before the flood only in Atrahasis and the list of targeted
> alternatives only in Gilgamesh XI, after it. Genesis 6–9 and the Book of
> the Watchers put destruction and rescue under one authority.

### claim_kind, framework_relation, public_label, evidence_status

No change: `interpretation`, `derived`, `framework`, `scoped` +
`contested`. The canonical layer is still framework. The comparative
layer is still an inferred reading.

### Scope — includes

1. The canonical layer (framework): the home-world decision to destroy
   (TBWTT 2:55–58; LWTE 2:25–26), the exiles' circumvention
   (TBWTT 2:58, 62; LWTE 2:27), the post-event reversal and
   re-implantation (TBWTT 2:66–68; LWTE 2:28), with the two motive
   statements kept apart.
2. The textual layer (inferred): the passage-specific distribution of
   assembly, oath, objection, warning and preservation across the five
   named texts, as tabulated in the procedural comparison, with the
   alternative-disaster list cited to Gilgamesh XI.
3. The comparative observation (inferred, *for ruling, Q3*): the
   Mesopotamian accounts separate the destroying authority from the
   rescuing god, as the canon separates the Council from the exiles;
   Genesis and the Watchers do not.

### Scope — excludes

Keep the four existing exclusions, with one wording change in the
geological one (Q4). Add:

- Treating the five texts as five independent confirmations. The
  literary stream and the concordance's relationship controls apply.
- Attributing the lion/wolf/famine/plague list to Atrahasis until the
  project's ATRA-WOH-III:vi alignment is resolved against a named
  edition.
- Reading the post-flood promise (Genesis 8:21–22, 9:8–17; TBWTT 2:66;
  LWTE 2:28) as an oath that bound the original decision.

Keep the four. Revise `alt-0010-canon-retrofit` so it targets the canon's

it postdates; the resemblance is reception, not memory."

### Revision triggers

Keep the existing four. Add:

- An edition of Atrahasis (Lambert–Millard 1969, Foster, or the edition
  behind the project text) resolves ATRA-WOH-III:vi.21–24.
- A registered Sumerian Flood Story source and an inspected English
  edition change the qualified cells.

### Dependencies

## 4. Consequential changes after a ruling

- **Specification and evidence map** (`flood-as-reset.md`,
  `woh-claim-0010-evidence.md`): rewrite to match. The evidence map's
  five-feature table is replaced by the comparison's cells. Its
  unlocated "Present" cells (Atrahasis seed formula and irrevocability,
  the Watchers' seed formula "in frame") are not supported as stated.
- **Bound pages** (all at `woh-claim-0010 = "0.1.0"`): the essay *The
  Flood Was a Reset, Not a Punishment*, `wiki/great-flood`, `wiki/noah`,
  `wiki/noahs-ark`, `wiki/noahic-covenant`, `datasets/flood-myths`,
  `wiki/pangaea`. `great-flood`, `noah` and `noahs-ark` already give
  the two-faction canon account and need a version bump plus a check;
  `noahic-covenant`, `pangaea` and the dataset page were not read for
  this proposal. The essay needs real
  edits:
  - its summary and opening (lines 18, 79) state assembly + oath +
    irrevocability as shared by "the oldest flood stories";
  - its summary says the list of targeted alternatives belongs to "the
    oldest flood stories", while the body correctly quotes Gilgamesh XI;
  - its frontmatter `description` ("a method its planners regretted")
    and closing "managed reset" framing read the outcome as the planners'
    intent, which the canon section in the same essay contradicts.

  The essay's title ("a Reset, Not a Punishment") can stand: in the canon
  the event is not moral retribution, and the outcome is a reset. Changing
  the title is not proposed.
- **Translations**: nine languages per changed page, after English sign-off.
- **Mirrors**: unaffected by this record.

## 5. Questions for ruling

1. **Title.** "The Flood as a contested decision", or keep "The Flood as
   deliberate reset" with a statement that makes clear the reset is the
   outcome, not the decision?
2. **Canonical layer.** Adopt the two-step canon account above (Council
   decision to destroy → exiles' circumvention → post-event reversal) in
   place of "a deliberate reset decision within the Council's
   governance"?
3. **Comparative observation.** Admit the inferred observation that the
   Mesopotamian accounts and the canon share the separation of destroying
   authority and rescuer, while Genesis and the Watchers merge them? This
   is new to the record; it is a reading, not something any source
   states. Leaving it out is the narrower choice.
4. **Geology exclusion.** The record excludes "historicity or dating of a
   global flood as a geological claim". Since 0071 (0.3.1) carries the
   canon's geological reading, change the exclusion to "the geological
   reading, which woh-claim-0071 carries"?
5. **Sumerian source ID.** Register a bibliography ID for the Sumerian
   Flood Story now, or cite it through the concordance keys until an
   English edition is inspected directly?
