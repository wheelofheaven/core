# Checkpoints — public, datable, placeable statements in the canon's autobiographical and narrative frames, compared with the public record

Key: `checkpoints` · accessed dates for all outside sources: 2026-11-12 · status: research note for founder review, not a core record.

## Exact question

Which statements in the canon about datable, placeable, public facts — in Raël's own autobiographical chapter (ETTMTTP ch. 1) and in the encounter narratives (TBWTT ch. 1 and the narrative frames of all three books) — can be compared with the public record, and what does the public record say?

This is a register for later citation. It is not a verdict on the narrative, and it does not touch private life: only public matters and published sources.

## Method

1. Read ETTMTTP-1 (all 77 paragraphs), TBWTT-1 (all 64), the narrative frames of TBWTT-2…7, ETTMTTP-2:1–19 and 2:131–132, and grepped all three French dumps for years, month names, place-names, named persons, media, publishers. Every canon statement below carries a REF and a verbatim French `QUOTE[...]` line (machine-verified, see end).
2. For each checkpoint, searched for independent published sources and **opened** them. Only opened pages are cited (list in "Outside sources"). Outside wording is quoted as returned by the fetch tool, which limits excerpts to short strings; table values (weather) are transcribed values, not prose quotations.
3. Classified each checkpoint:
   - **A** corroborated by a source independent of Raël's account;
   - **B** corroborated only by sources that derive from Raël's own account (said so);
   - **C** contradicted by, or divergent from, an outside source;
   - **N** no independent source found (queries recorded);
   - **P** partly corroborated (the checkable part matches; the rest is N).
4. Three layers kept apart: (a) canon says; (b) outside source says; (c) comparison. Computations (weekdays, day counts, mean moon age) are mine and marked "computed".

Access failures that shape the result (null results in their own right): the BnF catalogue (`catalogue.bnf.fr`, `data.bnf.fr`) did not resolve from this environment; SUDOC looped on redirects; VIAF returned 403; relbib returned a bot wall; the Library of Congress catalogue page rendered empty; WorldCat rendered no results; Slate.fr returned 403; HAL returned a bot wall; Palmer's *Aliens Adored* exists on archive.org (`aliensadoredrael0000palm`) but its text could not be opened. So **no BnF/SUDOC record and no page of Palmer's book was inspected**; Palmer is represented only by her World Religions and Spirituality Project (WRSP) profile, and by Wikipedia's page-cited paraphrases of her book (marked as secondary citation).

## Findings (ranked)

### F1. The first television appearances are dated by the INA; one canon month is off by a week, the order is confirmed

(a) Canon: the Chancel programme came after the manuscript went to a publisher and before the book; the Bouvard programme is placed "au mois de juin".

QUOTE[ETTMTTP-1:67]: je reçus peu de temps après un télégramme me demandant de venir à Paris pour participer à l'émission télévisée de Jacques Chancel, Le Grand Échiquier ENDQUOTE
QUOTE[ETTMTTP-1:72]: J'avais, au mois de juin, participé à une émission de télévision animée par Philippe Bouvard : Samedi-Soir ENDQUOTE

(b) INA (S1, institutional archive article by Florence Dartois, 2024): « Le public le découvre pour la première fois dans le très prestigieux « Grand échiquier » de Jacques Chancel le 13 mars 1974. » and « Il s'agit de son passage dans l'émission « Samedi soir » du 25 mai 1974 »; « Le ton du programme présenté par Philippe Bouvard se veut léger ». The same page carries an image alt-text reading « Raël interrogé par Jacques Chancel au Grand Echiquier le 7 mars 1974. » against a caption and body giving 13 March.

(c) Both appearances are **A** (independent archive holds the broadcasts). The Bouvard date is **C in the month**: INA 25 May 1974 (a Saturday, computed) against the canon's "au mois de juin". The INA page is internally inconsistent on the Chancel date (7 vs 13 March 1974); 13 March is the body-text and caption date. The canon gives no date for the Chancel programme, so nothing is contradicted there.

### F2. The French text in the project dumps is not the 1974/1975 text: it already carries the book-2 place-name correction and the post-1976 name of the movement

(a) Book 2 states that book 1 named the wrong volcano:

QUOTE[ETTMTTP-1:63]: J'avais d'ailleurs appelé à tort ce lieu le Puy-de-la-Vache, qui est le nom du volcan situé juste à côté du Puy-de-Lassolas. ENDQUOTE

But the TBWTT dump already reads:

QUOTE[TBWTT-1:3]: m'étais fixé pour but d'aller jusqu'au centre du cratère du «Puy-de-Lassolas» où je venais souvent pique-niquer en famille, l'été ENDQUOTE

and its closing pages invite readers into a body that, on the outside record, did not yet carry that name in 1974:

QUOTE[TBWTT-7:81]: Dans ce cas écrivez-moi et nous vous accueillerons au sein du Mouvement Raélien ENDQUOTE

while book 2 (1975) speaks throughout of the MADECH:

QUOTE[ETTMTTP-1:4]: car quoi qu'il puisse m'arriver le MADECH1 est en route et rien ne pourra plus l'arrêter ENDQUOTE

(b) Palmer, WRSP profile (S2, scholarly): "1974 Raël founded MADECH (Movement to Welcome the Creators of Humanity)." and "1976 In response to internal power struggles, Raël dissolved MADECH and founded the Raelian Movement." The geography (S3): the two cones are twins, « son jumeau le puy de la Vache ».

(c) Analysis: the dump of TBWTT is a later, revised state of the text (place-name silently corrected; "Mouvement Raélien" in place of whatever the 1974 printing said; heading "Mouvement Raélien" at TBWTT-7:73; a note at TBWTT-3:305 speaking of Raël in the third person). The LWTE dump likewise carries editorial notes dated after 1998 (LWTE-1:21). **Any checkpoint that depends on the 1974 wording — above all the original place-name — cannot be checked from the dumps**; it needs a first printing. This agrees with the note in `woh-claim-0070-evidence.md` that no 1973/1974 first printing is held. The book-2 correction itself is geographically sound: Lassolas and la Vache are adjacent twin cones (A).

QUOTE[TBWTT-1:64]: l'appareil s'éleva doucement jusqu'à environ 400 mètres puis disparut dans la brume ENDQUOTE

QUOTE[ETTMTTP-2:9]: Je m'habillai chaudement, car il faisait assez frais ENDQUOTE

(b) Infoclimat archive, station Clermont-Ferrand–Aulnat (07460, alt. 331 m) (S4), page headed « jeudi 13 décembre 1973 »: 06h 3.0 °C, 8/8, « pluie faible et intermittente », visibility 10 km; 09h 5.4 °C, 8/8, « pluie », 12 km; 12h 9.0 °C, 7/8, 15 km; daily Tn −3.4 °C, Tx 9 °C, sunshine 1 h 36 min, precipitation total 0 mm. Meteor shower (S7, encyclopaedia): Draconids maximum « 6-10 octobre », « les meilleures nuits étant celles du 8 au 10 ».

(c) 13 Dec 1973: grey sky **A** (8/8 at 06h and 09h at the plain station). "Frais/froid": 3–5 °C at 331 m at 06–09h; the crater lies some 800 m higher (S3 gives 1 183 m), so colder — consistent, not measured there. "Brume": the airport reports 10–12 km visibility, i.e. no mist at 331 m; whether cloud base sat on the cones at about 1 100 m cannot be read from this record — **N** for the mist itself. The station reports light rain at 06h and 09h; the canon mentions no rain — not a contradiction (different place and altitude, daily total 0 mm) but an element the narrative does not contain. Day of week: Thursday (computed; matches the archive page heading).
7 Oct 1975: cloudless sky **A** at regional scale (0/8 at 21h and 00h at Limoges, about 60 km from the Brantôme area — distance is my estimate, not sourced). "Assez frais": about 12 °C. Shooting stars: the date falls inside the Draconid window (compatibility only; the shower is usually weak, and no 1975 rate was found). Moon (computed from mean lunation, ±0.6 d): age about 2.4 days at 23h on 7 Oct 1975, i.e. a thin crescent already set — a dark sky; not contradicted. Day of week: Tuesday (computed).

### F4. The singing career is documented record by record, and matches the canon's "first record … second record" exactly

(a) QUOTE[ETTMTTP-1:38]: Je me produisais alors sous le pseudonyme de Claude Celler ENDQUOTE
QUOTE[ETTMTTP-1:40]: Mon premier disque connut un succès honnête et le deuxième grâce à une chanson qui s'appelait "Le miel et la cannelle", fut encore plus apprécié. ENDQUOTE
QUOTE[ETTMTTP-1:40]: Le directeur de cette maison de disques était Lucien Morisse, directeur de la station de radio "Europe N° 1" ENDQUOTE
QUOTE[ETTMTTP-1:51]: un ami fortuné me poussa à refaire un disque qu'il financerait lui-même ENDQUOTE

(b) Encyclopédisque (S8, discographic database with sleeve scans), artist page "Claude Celler", « Date de naissance : 30/09/1946 », five records: *Sacrée sale gueule*, 1966, Disc'AZ EP 1026; *Le miel et la cannelle*, mai 1967, Disc'AZ EP 1096 (tracks: Le miel et la cannelle; Monsieurs, votre femme me trompe; Madam' Pipi; Quand on se mariera; orchestra Michel Colombier); *Dans un verre de vin*, décembre 1967, Disc'AZ EP 1156; *Sur mon amour*, juillet 1969, Del Sol DS 16003; *Mon amour Patricia*, décembre 1970, Disc'AZ SG 234.
Lucien Morisse (S9, fr.wikipedia, sentences unsourced there): « directeur des programmes, avant Claude Agnely, puis directeur général à Europe no 1 »; « le directeur général du label discographique français « Disc'AZ » »; « Lucien Morisse se suicide par arme à feu, le 11 septembre 1970 ».

(c) Stage name **A**; Disc'AZ as label **A**; *Le miel et la cannelle* as the second record **A** (EP 1096 follows EP 1026); birth date as carried by the discographic database **A/B** (provenance of that field unknown). Morisse's two roles **A** in substance (the canon says "directeur de la station"; the outside wording is programme director, then director general — weak source). A record on another label in July 1969 fits the canon's privately financed record (my inference; the label's ownership was not checked). Two records are in the discography and not in the canon's narrative: the third Disc'AZ EP (Dec 1967) and a Disc'AZ single of December 1970, three months after Morisse's death and five months before the magazine's first issue. Not a contradiction — the canon does not say how many records there were — but the narrative's sequence (records → sales job → accident → journalism) is more compressed than the discography.
"Passait très souvent à la radio", the "nombreux concours radiophoniques", and the Rose d'or selection and withdrawal (ETTMTTP-1:46, 1:48): **N**. The competition itself existed (S10: « concours de chansons francophones organisé chaque année de 1962 à 1979 », no 1968 edition « en raison des événements de mai »); the page opened does not name Celler, and does not say who organised it.

### F5. A dating tension inside "à neuf ans": the Tour de France automobile did not run in 1955

(a) QUOTE[ETTMTTP-1:15]: Mais à neuf ans (encore le neuf) tout allait changer. ENDQUOTE
QUOTE[ETTMTTP-1:17]: Quelques mois plus tard, j'assistais par hasard au passage du tour de France automobile et ce fut le "coup de foudre" ENDQUOTE
QUOTE[ETTMTTP-1:19]: C'est également à neuf ans que je connus pour la première fois la pension. ENDQUOTE
QUOTE[ETTMTTP-1:23]: je me retrouvais au collège municipal d'Ambert en classe de huitième ENDQUOTE

(b) S11 (fr.wikipedia, Tour de France automobile): « seule l'édition de 1955 étant annulée pour cause d'interdiction du sport automobile en France »; for 1956: « 103 équipages au départ, 37 à l'arrivée le 23 septembre », Nice to Paris. No route detail naming Ambert was found for any 1950s edition.

(c) Analysis. Born 30 September 1946 (canon), he was nine from 30 Sept 1955 to 29 Sept 1956. The only edition in that span ended on 23 September 1956, in the last week of his tenth year. The canon then places, still "à neuf ans" and as consequences of the racing vocation, a boarding year at Le Puy (with "six mois de communions quotidiennes", ETTMTTP-1:21) and a return to Ambert in huitième. These cannot all follow a September 1956 passage and still fall at age nine. Either the Tour he saw was another edition (1954, age 7–8; 1957, age 10–11), or the "nine" is schematic — the text itself flags the number (« encore le neuf »). **C (internal chronology against a public calendar)**, with the caveat that whether any edition passed through Ambert is **N**.

### F6. The two publishing figures are who the canon says they are, and they were at the same house

(a) QUOTE[ETTMTTP-1:64]: Marcel Jullian, qui dirigeait cette maison d'édition me fit venir à Paris ENDQUOTE
QUOTE[ETTMTTP-1:67]: Ce dernier, directeur d'une collection dans la maison d'édition où j'avais envoyé mon manus-crit, l'avait lu ENDQUOTE

(b) S12 (fr.wikipedia, Marcel Jullian, citing *Le Nouvel Observateur*): « En 1974, alors directeur littéraire des Éditions Julliard, depuis 1971 ». S13 (fr.wikipedia, Jacques Chancel, citing *Qui est qui en France* 2008, p. 516): « En 1967 il dirige aux Éditions Julliard la collection « Idée fixe », sous la direction de Marcel Jullian ».

(c) **A** for the relationship the canon describes (Chancel a series editor in the house where Jullian was in charge of the literary side). The canon never names the house; on these two sources it was Julliard (inference). "Dirigeait cette maison d'édition" is looser than the outside title (directeur littéraire). The episode itself — the manuscript, the request to add a life story, the missing manuscript, Courrèges's intervention (ETTMTTP-1:64–68) — is **N**: nothing found on Courrèges and Vorilhon (query recorded below).

### F7. First editions: an independent national-library record confirms 1974 / Clermont-Ferrand and 1975 / Brantôme, and gives a subtitle the canon does not

(a) QUOTE[ETTMTTP-1:70]: à l'automne 1974 le livre sortait des presses de l'imprimerie ENDQUOTE
QUOTE[ETTMTTP-1:69]: Marie-Paule me proposa de quitter son emploi d'infirmière pour se consacrer à l'édition et à la diffusion de ce document exceptionnel ENDQUOTE

(b) Open Library edition records built from Library of Congress MARC (S14): *Le livre qui dit la vérité*, subtitle "j'ai rencontré un extra terrestre", Édition du Message, Clermont-Ferrand, 1974, "160 p.", LCCN 78373980, note "Errata slip inserted."; *Les extra-terrestres m'ont emmené sur leur planète*, subtitle "le 2ème message qu'ils m'ont donné", Édition du Message, Brantôme, 1975, "160 p.", LCCN 76456635; *La Géniocratie*, Éditions du Message, "Brantôme (La Nègrerie, 24310)", 1977.

(c) Year and self-publication **A** (publisher "Édition du Message" at the author's own town, moving from Clermont-Ferrand to Brantôme between the two books, which matches the canon's move to the Périgord, ETTMTTP-2:8). "Automne" is **N** at library level; a critical ex-member blog (S15, weak) gives 5 November 1974 without documentary support. The LoC record's subtitle and errata slip are facts about the first printing not visible in the dumps. *Accueillir les extra-terrestres* (1979): only Palmer's profile (S2: "In 1979, Raël revealed his messianic role in his book, Accueiller les extra-terrestres") — no catalogue record opened. Date divergences in secondary literature for book 2: S2 "his 1976 book"; Cherblanc (S16) cites a 1977 Brantôme edition; the LoC-derived record says 1975.

### F8. The magazine: dates agree with the canon; the canon never names it; the reason for its end is told differently outside

(a) QUOTE[TBWTT-1:1]: si j'ai créé une revue spécialisée dans cette branche voilà trois ans ENDQUOTE
QUOTE[ETTMTTP-1:59]: je créai une maison d'édition afin de publier une revue "à mon idée" ENDQUOTE
QUOTE[ETTMTTP-1:70]: Je cessai immédiatement de m'occuper de la revue automobile, occupation incompatible avec le sérieux de la mission qui m'avait été confiée ENDQUOTE

(b) Palmer, WRSP (S2): "in 1971 he launched a sports car magazine, Autopop, which was published until 1974". UNADFI fact sheet (S17, anti-cult association, no references): « crée sa propre revue dont la santé financière ne résiste pas au choc pétrolier de 1973 ». The Race (S18, motorsport journalism, relying on a photographer's recollection): "Vorilhon founded a magazine called Auto Pop in 1971" … "often participating in low level national and regional race and rally competitions"; "He had graduated from the renowned Winfield racing school in 1968". Wikipedia (S19, S20) gives first issue May 1971 and last issue no. 34, September 1974, citing an archived page I did not open.

(c) Existence, 1971 start, 1974 end: **A** (scholarly profile; whether Palmer checked issues or relied on Raël is not stated — treat as A-/B). Name "Autopop": absent from all three books. Reason for stopping: canon = incompatibility with the mission; UNADFI = finances after the 1973 oil shock — **divergent accounts**, the outside one unreferenced. No library record for the periodical could be opened (BnF/SUDOC unreachable): **N** at catalogue level. Racing results ("une dizaine de coupes", TBWTT-1:1; "nombreuses victoires", ETTMTTP-1:60): **N**; the one outside characterisation is "low level national and regional". The Winfield school (1968) appears outside and not in the canon. The circuit named in ETTMTTP-1:60 exists as described (S21: Saint-Avit-de-Tardes, Creuse, « créé par Pierre Bardinon en 1963 ») — **A** for the place.

### F10. MADECH: the three books give no founding date or place

(a) The canon dates the resignation, one meeting and the address, not the founding:

QUOTE[ETTMTTP-2:1]: C'est au mois de juin 1975 que je décidai de démissionner du poste de président du MADECH1 ENDQUOTE
QUOTE[ETTMTTP-2:7]: La réunion du 6 août eut finalement lieu comme prévu dans le cratère du Puy-de-Lassolas ENDQUOTE
QUOTE[ETTMTTP-1:3]: Sept cents, ils sont sept cents à l'heure où je mets ces lignes au propre ENDQUOTE

(b) S2: "1974 Raël founded MADECH". S20 (en.wikipedia "History of Raëlism", **secondary citation** of Palmer 2004 p. 37, not opened): on 13 December "170 MADECH members gathered at Puy de Lassolas and formed an organization"; and, citing the movement's own website, a first public conference on 19 September 1974 "which attracted more than 2000 people". INA (S23): a France Régions 3 Le Mans news item dated 9 January 1975 shows Vorilhon interviewed about the encounter.

(c) Founding year 1974: **A-/B** (scholarly profile; basis not stated). Founding day and place (13 Dec 1974, Puy de Lassolas, 170 members): secondary citation only — **not verified**. Salle Pleyel (ETTMTTP-1:74–75: 150-seat room, « plus de trois mille personnes », second lecture « plus de deux mille personnes »): the only date found (19 Sept 1974) comes from the movement's own site — **B**; no press notice found, including the France-Soir « entrefilet » the canon mentions — **N**. The 700 members, the forty lectures, the June 1975 resignation and the successor "Christian, physicien": **N**. The regional TV item of January 1975 is independent evidence (**A**) that he was publicly presenting the account in the provinces that winter. Full name of MADECH in an academic source (S16): « Mouvement pour l'accueil des Élohim créateurs de l'humanité ».

## Limitations

- No library catalogue of record (BnF, SUDOC) and no page of Palmer's monograph could be opened; the LoC data come through Open Library's copy of the MARC records. Wikipedia pages are tertiary and several of their sentences are unsourced; they are used for neutral facts (who a person was, a cancelled race edition) and flagged.
- Outside quotations passed through a fetch tool that returns short excerpts; they should be re-read on the page before being printed anywhere. Weather values are transcribed from the tool's rendering of the Infoclimat tables; hour labels may be local or UTC (one page came back with shifted hours).
- Weather stations are distant from both sites (Aulnat: plain, about 800 m below the cones; Limoges: about 60 km from the Brantôme area, my estimate).
- Weekdays, day counts and moon age are my computations; moon age uses a mean lunation (±0.6 day).
- "Derived from Raël's account" is judged from each source's own citations (Cherblanc cites Raël 1988; Wikipedia cites *Intelligent Design*). Palmer's WRSP profile gives no citations for the biographical sentences, so its independence on any single point is unknown.
- Scope: public matters only. Names of private persons in the chapter (family, friends, first names) were not searched.
