# Published scholarship on the text of Raël's three books (1974, 1975, 1979)

Literature review, compiled 2026-11-12 from public sources only.

## How to read this report

Every statement about a source carries one of three access marks:

- **[read in full text]** — I opened the full text and read the passage myself.
- **[abstract only]** — I saw only a publisher or library abstract, or a bibliographic record.
- **[search snippet only]** — I saw only a search-engine snippet or a search tool's summary; wording and page are unverified.

Page numbers are given only where I read them in the full text.
No quotation here is reconstructed from memory.
Where a page fetch was summarised by an automated reader, I say so, and those items are marked no higher than their real access level.

## Headline finding

**One work already does systematic source criticism of the first two books, in French, from the first printings: Steve Maridor's 2009 Neuchâtel doctoral thesis.**
It is open access, and I read the relevant chapters in full.
It anticipates our observations 3, 7, 9 and 10 closely, 4 and 8 partly, and it does not make observations 1, 2, 5, 6 or 11.
The English-language literature (Palmer, Sentes, Gallagher, Chryssides, Bigliardi, Thomas) is sociological or thematic and works almost entirely from the 2005 English compendium; I could not read most of it in full (see "Access failures").

## Part B. Our eleven observations against the literature

### 1. Genesis "day" = about 2,000 years; no word "precession", no cycle length

**Not found in any source I could read.**
Maridor never mentions the 2,000-year day, precession, the zodiacal definition of the day, or any cycle length (keyword search of the whole thesis: no occurrence of "précession", "équinoxe", "zodiaque"; "deux mille ans" occurs only in unrelated senses).
He does note the Age of Aquarius: the Earth entered "l'ère du Verseau" in 1946, treated as the age of the Apocalypse (ch. 1.3.4–1.3.5, pp. 61–70) [read in full text].
Cherblanc gives the same (p. 134: "l'humanité est entrée depuis 1946 dans l'ère du Verseau"; the bomb of 1945 "marque la fin de l'ère du Poisson") [read in full text].
Flaherty quotes the same from Raël 2005, p. 81 [abstract only].
None of them connects the ages to the length of a creation day or remarks on the absence of precession vocabulary.
Unchecked places where it could appear: Chryssides 2000 (*Culture and Cosmos*), Gallagher 2010, Bigliardi 2015 and 2017.
**Provisional status: new.**

### 2. Chronology figures (25,000; 22,000; about 13,000 = 666 × 20 to 1945; Flood about 5,000)

**Only fragments are in the literature; the system as a whole is not.**
Maridor [read in full text]:
- 25,000 years appears repeatedly as the Elohim's scientific lead ("une avance de vingt-cinq mille ans au plan scientifique", p. 96) and as the age of the first immortal (n. 127 in §1.3.5, citing LDV p. 152; printed page not verified), and in book 2's "il y a vingt-cinq mille ans lorsque nous avons débarqué sur la terre" (quoted p. 140, EEP p. 67).
- 22,000 years is given once, in a footnote, as the date of the creation of humans: "Il y aurait 22000 ans (p. 111 LDV)" (p. 66 n. 113).
- He reports Raël's own reply in *Accueillir* to the objection that bones older than 25,000 years exist (p. 141 n. 267).
- No occurrence of 13,000 years, of 666 generations, or of a date for the Flood.
No other source read gives any of these figures.
**Provisional status: the 13,000 / 666 × 20 computation, the Flood date, and the relation between the 25,000 and 22,000 figures appear to be new. Maridor reads "22,000" as the creation of humans, which differs from our "decision" reading and should be checked against LDV p. 111.**

### 3. Book 2 explicitly corrects book 1 on Elohim intervention

**Already in the literature, in detail: Maridor.** [read in full text]
- p. 129: the extraterrestrial "rectifie d'abord (p. 53 EEP) un élément mal compris dans le premier ouvrage (pp. 143-144 LDV) : il annonce que les hommes se détruiront eux-mêmes, et que les extraterrestres n'auront pas à le faire." He adds: "Le deuxième livre révélé modifie le premier, sans que celui-ci ne soit retouché dans les éditions suivantes."
- pp. 142–144 (§4.1.3 "Annonce de modifications"): this is the only correction of book 1 that book 2 makes; Vorilhon announces at EEP p. 14 that "certains points du message ont besoin d'être éclaircis" but himself clarifies nothing, and it is the extraterrestrial who makes the one change; later editions carry no note and no correction of the passage, although Vorilhon freely alters other wording. Maridor's conclusion: a single requested modification seals the rest of the text.
- Annexe A, p. 258: the Eloha begins "en reprenant un point du premier message que Vorilhon a mal retranscrit".
Conflicting, unverified report: a search summary attributes to Palmer 2004 the statement that the apocalyptic passages were "deemed apocrypha and expunged from future printings of the first book" [search snippet only]. If Palmer does say this, she and Maridor disagree on whether the book 1 passage was ever altered. Maridor compared first printings with the web edition, so his statement is the better attested.
Cherblanc (pp. 134–135) simply reports both outcomes side by side ("soit en s'auto-détruisant, soit par une intervention des Élohim") without noticing the correction [read in full text].
**Status: not new. What may be new is the exact wording comparison and any check of intermediate printings.**

### 4. Raël's paternity changes between the books

**Partly in the literature.**
Maridor [read in full text]:
- p. 147: the recentring on Vorilhon continues after book 2: "Vorilhon écrit dans *Accueillir les Extra-Terrestres* (pp. 83-84) que Iahvé lui a annoncé qu'il est son fils et donc le demi-frère de Jésus. Mais Raël n'a le droit de divulguer cette information que trois ans après l'avoir reçue."
- p. 150 and n. 291: book 2's autobiography tries to show that Vorilhon "a toujours été l'élu, que les extraterrestres l'ont guidé dès sa naissance", which Maridor says contradicts book 1, pp. 21–22 LDV, where the selection criteria (born in France, free-thinker without being anti-religious, Jewish father and Catholic mother) cannot apply before adulthood: "Il y a donc contradiction entre le premier et le deuxième ouvrage aussi sur ce point."
- He also notes the father is described both as Jewish and as unknown (p. 150 n. 291; p. 148).
- pp. 162–163: the 1997 message identifies Raël with "notre Fils Bien-Aimé" and applies John 14:6 to him.
So Maridor places "followed since birth" in book 2, not book 1, and treats it as contradicting book 1's adult-selection criteria. That differs from our formulation (book 1: chosen and followed since birth) and should be checked against LDV pp. 21–22.
Other scholars harmonise without comment: Cherblanc p. 133 places the discovery that "Iavhé est son véritable père" inside the 7 October 1975 encounter; Palmer's WRSP profile places it in the 1973 encounter; Flaherty quotes Raël 2005, p. 290 [the last two: abstract only].
**Status: the three-year withholding and the book 1 / book 2 contradiction on election are in Maridor. A three-stage account running through book 3 as a deliberate development is only sketched by him (one sentence).**

### 5. Attitude to proof changes (promised, refused, offered only by "Satan" as a test)

**Not found.**
Maridor discusses argument from authority and the absence of proof in general (ch. 1.4, pp. 72–78; he cites Perelman on the argument from authority in n. 87) and notes that Jesus is said to have been sent so that the scriptures "servent de preuves" in the age of science (p. 96, quoting LDV p. 93) [read in full text].
He does not trace a change of stance on proof across the books, and he does not treat book 3.
**Provisional status: new.**

### 6. Satan and Lucifer only in later books; Flood motive differs by book

**Not found, and one point of Maridor cuts against the first half.**
Maridor [read in full text]:
- Satan is already in book 1: a sub-chapter titled « Satan » exists in ch. III of the first printing (LDV p. 82), although it is missing from both tables of contents (p. 39 and n. 57; Annexe B, p. 268). In book 1 Satan is "un « créateur » sceptique" whom the temptations of Christ are meant to convince (p. 94), "un extraterrestre, opposé à la création d'une autre civilisation intelligente" (Annexe A, p. 253).
- The word "Lucifer" does not occur anywhere in the thesis.
- The Flood in book 1 is summarised as nuclear missiles, with Noah in an orbiting craft (Annexe A, pp. 252–253); no comparison of motives across books.
**Status: the Lucifer observation and the Flood-motive comparison appear new. The claim that Satan appears only later needs rewording: Maridor documents a « Satan » section in the July 1974 first printing. What may be new is the change in Satan's role between book 1 and book 3.**

### 7. Dependence on the French translation (Dhorme, Grosjean/Léturmy) and on ellipses

**Largely in the literature: Maridor, ch. 2.1–2.2 (pp. 81–86).** [read in full text]
- Raël's bibliography names one title: « La Bible d'Edouard Dhorme Collection La Pléiade » (p. 83). Maridor notes that Vorilhon "ne signale pas que le Nouveau Testament n'a pas été traduit par Dhorme, mais par Grosjean" (p. 83 n. 149). He cites Grosjean alone, Gallimard 1956; he does not name Léturmy.
- The reading depends on the French: the pun saints/sains on Ezekiel 42:14 works only in French, has no basis in the Hebrew (he gives the roots), and is untranslatable; the English renders "holy" and "pure or sterile" (pp. 81–82). His verdict: a rereading from the French text not in agreement with the Hebrew "constitue une imposture" (p. 83).
- Quotations do not always match the Pléiade: Judges 13:5 "voué à Dieu" against Pléiade "un naziréen de Dieu"; a pseudo-quotation built from Matthew 6:24 and 6:19; 2 Kings 4:44 with the preceding text "fortement modifié"; Matthew 13:4 "graines" against Pléiade "semences" (pp. 83–84). He adds that citations are "souvent exactes ou très proches".
- Two hypotheses for the deviations: a second, unnamed French translation, which he could not identify; or second-hand quotation from ancient-astronaut authors, who favour the Pléiade because it leaves "Elohim" untranslated and who modify it. He could not verify the second "malgré de nombreuses lectures" (pp. 84–85).
- Ellipses: "Quand il ne cite pas une partie d'un verset, Vorilhon l'indique de temps à autre par des points de suspension. Cette règle n'est pourtant pas respectée à chaque fois." References are rarely exact; examples of wrong chapter and verse (p. 85 and nn. 153–155).
- Selectivity: the extraterrestrial extracts passages without explaining the choice (p. 85); compared with Eco's hermetic semiosis (p. 86 n. 157).
- The Kabbalah quotation in book 1 is judged second-hand: he could trace only half of it in the *Shi'ur Qomah* (Cohen 1985) (pp. 93–94).
- Annexe D (pp. 274 ff.) prints each citation as Raël gives it. For Exodus 14:21 it records Raël's text as « Il mit ainsi la mer à sec et les eaux se fendirent » (exode XIV-21), with no remark on what is omitted.
Not in Maridor: a verse-by-verse comparison showing what each ellipsis removes and how the removal serves the reading. He lists; he does not analyse individual omissions. The east wind in Exodus 14:21 is not remarked on.
A Connecticut College honours thesis on the Raëlian reinterpretation of Genesis was reported by a search tool as noting that Raël omits lines that would diminish his picture [search snippet only]; the repository returned 403 (https://digitalcommons.conncoll.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1002&context=relighp). Author and title not established.
**Status: the Dhorme/Grosjean dependence, inexact citation and irregular ellipses are not new. The systematic demonstration that specific ellipses carry the interpretation (Exodus 14:21 and the like), and the Léturmy attribution, appear new.**

### 8. Dependence on Sendy and other French ancient-astronaut authors

**Partly in the literature; no scholar I could read demonstrates textual dependence on Sendy.**
Maridor, ch. 2.3.1 and excursus « Raël et l'astroarchéologie » (pp. 87–98) [read in full text]:
- Places book 1's commentary sections in the "genre astroarchéologique" of 1960–1970 and names "Charroux, Sendy, Bergier et bien d'autres" (p. 88), citing Charroux 1965 and 1967, Sendy *Ces dieux qui firent le ciel et la terre* (Laffont 1969) and *La lune clé de la Bible* (J'ai lu, 1968), Bergier & Pauwels *Le matin des magiciens*, von Däniken 1969, 1971, 1974 in French translation; his bibliography also lists Sendy's *Les cahiers de cours de Moïse* (J'ai Lu, 1963). *L'Ère du Verseau* is not in his bibliography.
- Shared features he documents: use of the Pléiade Bible; the rendering of Elohim ("êtres divins" in Charroux; "les venus-du-ciel" or "les Célestes" in Sendy; "ceux qui sont venus du ciel" in Vorilhon) (pp. 89–90); Genesis 1:1–2 and Ezekiel as preferred texts; Sodom as an atomic explosion and Jericho as ultrasound, compared in detail with von Däniken (pp. 91–92); Charroux on Jesus as son of an extraplanetary being (p. 95); telepathy; appeal to unspecified science.
- Differences: the others prefer archaeological evidence and never quote the Bible so systematically; Raël quotes in canonical order and uses Ezekiel 11–44, which the others hardly use (pp. 86, 91).
- His judgement on plagiarism: "La correspondance entre les théories astroarchéologiques et celles de Vorilhon ne constitue pas une preuve de plagiat de la part de ce dernier." He asks why the first edition does not mention these authors and why Vorilhon names them only on his website (pp. 97–98).
- The contact narrative is compared in detail with Adamski (Leslie & Adamski, *Les soucoupes volantes ont atterri*, J'ai Lu 1971) (pp. 98–100).
- The yellow-on-black covers resembled ancient-astronaut paperbacks such as von Däniken's (p. 154).
Other sources: Volokhine (2006, 2014) links Vorilhon to von Däniken and Charroux [search snippet only]; Renard 1980 lists Vorilhon and Sendy in the same thematic bibliography [search snippet only]; Palmer 2004, p. 28, is reported to name Sendy 1968, Hutin 1970 and Bergier 1970 as precursors [search snippet only]; Bigliardi 2017 is the only academic study of Sendy, and whether it compares him with Raël is unknown [abstract only].
Specific Sendy-to-Raël textual parallels are asserted in ex-member and anti-cult sources (Péloquin 2004; blogs; an anonymous site cited by French Wikipedia which also names Brinsley Le Poer Trench), not in scholarship I could read.
Misraki / Paul Thomas: not mentioned in any source read.
**Status: genre dependence and the von Däniken, Charroux and Adamski parallels are not new. A documented passage-level dependence on Sendy's three books, and any use of Misraki, would be new to the scholarly literature, subject to reading Bigliardi 2015/2017 and Palmer 2004.**

### 9. Edition history

**Substantially in the literature for books 1 and 2: Maridor, §0.5 and ch. 5 (pp. 23, 153–167).** [read in full text]
- Printings of book 1: first printing 15 July 1974; second printing "achevée d'imprimer le 5 novembre 1974", text and pagination unchanged (p. 23; p. 158 n. 313). Differences he records between the two 1974 printings: first printing has red lettering and no cover photo of Vorilhon, later ones yellow on black with photo (p. 154 nn. 302, 304); the photo on p. 7 is replaced and captioned with "Puy de Lassolas" in bold with a note admitting the earlier error, while the body text p. 17 still reads "puy de la Vache" (p. 40 n. 62; p. 158); drawings on pp. 13 and 27 swapped (n. 66); table of contents consolidated but errors kept (n. 60).
- Publisher statements: book 1 "Édition du message (édité par l'auteur) : Clermont-Ferrand, 1974"; book 2 "Édition du message : Brantome, 1975" (p. 14 nn. 3–4).
- Separate editions continued until 1997 (p. 23). Combined editions: *Les messages que m'ont donnés les Extra-Terrestres* (Fondation raëlienne, Vaduz, 1998), subtitle "ils sont ce que nous avons appelé « Dieu »"; *Le Vrai Visage de Dieu* (Vaduz, 1999), subtitle "Ni dieu surnaturel, ni évolution, La vérité sur nos origines extraterrestres"; *Le message donné par les Extra-Terrestres* (2000, web, copyright 1998), subtitle "La science remplace enfin la religion !" (pp. 153–155). He notes the return from plural "messages" to singular, echoing the 1974 subtitle. (On p. 14 he refers to the combined edition as "l'édition de 1997"; on p. 153 he dates it 1998.)
- English: now published as *Intelligent Design*, which additionally contains *Accueillir*; he comments on the appropriation of the term (p. 153 n. 297) and on the different cover (p. 155 n. 307).
- MADECH: at LDV p. 141 the first edition reads "Votre mouvement vous l'appellerez le MADECH, le Mouvement pour l'Accueil des Elohim Créateurs de l'Humanité qui porte en ses initiales un message « Moïse a devancé Elie et le Christ »"; the combined edition reads "Votre mouvement vous l'appellerez le MOUVEMENT RAËLIEN" (MDE p. 92) (p. 158). MADECH is replaced only five times, all in book 1 (LDV pp. 141, 155, 157 three times); all occurrences in book 2 are kept with a note (p. 159 n. 314). Inside the combined edition "religion raëlienne" replaced "mouvement raëlien", which had replaced "MADECH" (p. 154 n. 301).
- "Claude Raël" (three times in part 2 of book 2, eight times in part 3) becomes "Raël" in later editions, including in text said to be written by the extraterrestrial through Raël's hand (p. 159 and n. 317).
- Removed: the preface of book 1 and the avertissement of book 2 (which introduced "ADN" and "l'âge de l'apocalypse", absent from book 1) (pp. 157). All images are gone from the web edition (p. 40 n. 69). Mailing addresses and the 13 December meeting place changed (p. 160).
- Added: a new avertissement calling the two books "la nouvelle Bible de l'Humanité" (p. 156); footnotes on the change of name and on the symbol no longer containing a swastika (p. 160, MDE p. 142); the new message (MDE pp. 207–210) and an author's post-scriptum.
- The new message: Maridor dates it to 1997 and analyses it at pp. 161–164 (calls the 1973 message the "Message final"; near-rupture with the Jews; embassy may be built elsewhere; Buddhism closest to the truth, against book 1's Kabbalah; Raël as "Prophète Bien Aimé" and "notre Fils Bien-Aimé"). He does not give the date 13 December 1997.
- Post-scriptum: written 1997; Raël suggested the symbol change in 1990; Elohim to come within 38 years of 1997 (pp. 164–165).
- Principle he draws: Vorilhon alters narration, dictated speech and "directly written" text alike, yet never makes the one correction the extraterrestrial requested (pp. 159–160).
- Explicit limit: "Le but n'étant pas l'exhaustivité" (p. 158 n. 312); he compares first printings with the web edition only and says differences among the combined editions are "minimes".
Other sources: Flaherty gives the English chain (1986, 1989, 2005) and the symbol dates 1992 and 2007 [abstract only]; Palmer's WRSP profile gives 1992 for the symbol [abstract only]; Cherblanc cites a 1988 Brantôme reprint of book 1 and a 1977 printing of book 2 [read in full text].
Not found anywhere: collation of intermediate French printings (1975–1997), the 1979 first edition of *Accueillir* against later editions, the English 1986/1989 texts against 2005, print runs.
**Status: MADECH to Mouvement Raëlien, title changes, removed preface, added 1997 message and post-scriptum, symbol note are not new. A complete collation, the intermediate printings, book 3, and the English editions are open.**

### 10. « Les Clés » reads as human teaching; who speaks where

**Closely related analysis exists in Maridor, with a different emphasis.** [read in full text]
- He spells the title « Les clefs » (EEP pp. 95–153) and notes Vorilhon uses both spellings (p. 134 n. 256).
- Status of the text (pp. 110–112, 133–135): book 2 has three parts of different status: autobiography wholly assumed by Vorilhon; a hybrid part like book 1; and « Les clefs », "la plus directe", which the author "découvre … en même temps qu'il l'écrit" and for which the extraterrestrial bears entire responsibility, written "par la main de Raël" (EEP p. 93).
- He applies Ducrot's distinction between locuteur and énonciateur (p. 39 n. 61) and lists places where the grammar betrays the claimed speaker: EEP p. 72 "comme nous" including an immortal among mortals; EEP p. 85 "Vous qui lirez ce message" inside speech addressed to Vorilhon; EEP p. 135, first-person singular for Vorilhon inside part 3 after third person on p. 134; EEP p. 154, third then first person for Raël; the avertissement where Vorilhon speaks of himself in the third person (pp. 110–112).
- Synthesis (p. 135): "il est souvent difficile de comprendre si c'est Vorilhon qui écrit ou l'extraterrestre. Les pronoms ne correspondent souvent pas". Because part 3 is declared to be written by the extraterrestrial yet contains errors and incoherences, it "pose la question de la compétence de l'extraterrestre".
- Function: the keys are practical rules meant to structure the movement after the MADECH crisis (pp. 134–135, 146–147).
- In the combined edition the confusion grows: where the extraterrestrial is speaking and Vorilhon is in the third person, the address of the movement is given in the first person (p. 160).
- Book 1 as well: 61 occurrences of "Iahvé", all but two inside biblical quotations, never referring to a single being and never to the speaker, although book 2 (EEP p. 71) names the speaker Iahvé; his hypothesis is that Vorilhon gave the extraterrestrial a name only in book 2 (pp. 138–140).
Maridor does not conclude that the chapter is human teaching rather than Yahweh's speech; he takes the declared status at face value and shows that the text fails to sustain it.
**Status: the speaker confusion and pronoun evidence are not new. A positive attribution of the chapter's voice, section by section, would go beyond Maridor.**
