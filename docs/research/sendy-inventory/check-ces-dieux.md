# Sendy, *Ces dieux qui firent le ciel et la terre* (1969) vs. TBWTT commentary

**Counts (226 items):** same 10 · similar 34 · mentioned 14 · absent 168.

**Scope.** The book is a "roman de la Bible" that works almost entirely on Genesis 1–11. It adds a short survey of Abraham, Lot and Moses and a few zodiacal asides on the New Testament. Sendy explicitly rules out Celestial contact in historical times: no radio between the prophets and the Celestials (l. 1204), no saucer at the burning bush (l. 1306), and no return of the Celestials (l. 1203). That is why the Message's material on Exodus through Revelation (Ezekiel's wheels, Elijah, Jericho, the ark, the Gospels) is almost all absent.

**Pattern of the `similar` items.** Sendy frames creation as the *restoration* of an Earth hit by a glaciation Cataclysm c. 21,500 BCE, not as creation from scratch. In that frame:
- The serpent is a mutant native, not a dissident faction of creators.
- The Flood is the departing Celestials blowing up their own Eden and its nuclear plants, not missiles from the home planet.
- The ark (tebah) is a capsule holding "semences de vie" on Earth, not a rocket in orbit.
- Babel is a launch tower built by Noah's doubting heirs to reach the Moon, and nobody intervenes to stop it.
