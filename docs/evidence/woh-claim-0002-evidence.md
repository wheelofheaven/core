# Evidence map: Anunnaki–Elohim identity

This map serves claim [`woh-claim-0002`](../framework/anunnaki-elohim-identity.md).
It is `scoped` and `contested`. The claim is a comparative `interpretation`, so
every row must name which relationship it supports — resemblance, typological
parallel, historical dependence, common inheritance, convergence, or
referential identity (see [comparative method](../methodology/comparative-method.md)).
Resemblance rows do not support the identity conclusion on their own.

Locators: `ATRA-WOH` = the in-house *Atra-ḫasīs* reconstruction (Lambert–Millard
line numbering); `GEN-WOH` = the in-house Genesis translation; Black & Green
page numbers are from the inspected scan (see the
[source note](../../source-notes/black-green-mesopotamia.md)).

## Comparison matrix

## Lexical / naming layer (Black & Green, inspected)

| Observation | Locator | What it supports | What it does not support |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| "Anuna (Anunnakkū) … used in earlier, especially Sumerian, texts as a general word for the gods … the early gods who were born first and were not differentiated with individual names." | Black & Green, p. 34 (s.v. *Anuna*) | The Anunna are a collective, not a fixed roster — consistent with a collective-level reading. | It gives no bridge to Hebrew `Elohim`; the collectives are internal to Mesopotamian religion. |
| "Anu … is described as king of the Anunnakku … In the Epic of Creation the multitude of gods are called the 'Anunnakki of heaven and earth.'" | Black & Green, p. 34 | The term's scope shifts across periods (heaven/earth/underworld). | A stable referent that could be identified with anything external. |
| "Igigū or Igigi … introduced in the Old Babylonian Period … from Middle Babylonian times on … generally used to refer to the gods of heaven collectively, just as the term Anunnakki (Anuna) was later used to refer to the gods of the underworld." | Black & Green, p. 103 (s.v. *Igigū*); p. 179 (600 Anunnakkū of the underworld) | The Anunnaki/Igigi distinction is a *late, shifting* convention — the roster is period-dependent. | Any claim that "Anunnaki" names one constant group across Mesopotamian history. |

## Mainstream alternatives (all currently at least as economical)

| Alternative | Why it explains the matrix |
| --- | --- |
| Distinct divine collectives within distinct traditions | The names are internal to their own religious systems; nothing lexical connects Anunna to `Elohim`. |
| Ancient Near Eastern literary inheritance / borrowing | The flood dependence in particular is standard scholarship (Atra-ḫasīs → Gilgamesh XI → Genesis stream) and needs no shared beings. |
| Typological convergence | Creation-and-flood narratives recur because they answer the same cultural questions. |
| Selective harmonization | The framework selects congenial parallels from internally diverse corpora and reads them against the changing Anunnaki sense the lexica document. |

## Critical / source-history note

The Anunnaki referent is not stable: Black & Green document the term migrating
from "undifferentiated early gods" (Sumerian) to "gods of the underworld"
(later Babylonian), with the Igigi/Anunnaki heaven/underworld split a late
development. An identity claim must therefore specify *which* period's Anunnaki
it identifies with the Elohim — the matrix currently does not. Zecharia
Sitchin's Nibiru/gold-mining reconstruction is explicitly excluded by the
claim record and is not part of this map.

## Dependency summary

- The strongest row (flood) supports **historical dependence between texts**,
  which is the alternative-family reading, not referential identity.
- Every other row supports **typological parallel** or **shared topos** at
  most.
- No inspected source moves from any of these relationships to **referential
  identity**; that step is supplied by the framework (claim `woh-claim-0001`),
  on which this claim explicitly depends.
- Black & Green (now inspected) constrains rather than supports the claim: it
  shows the Mesopotamian side has no fixed roster to identify.

A discriminating review would need a criterion capable of separating
"typological parallel / literary dependence" from "same underlying beings" —
the matrix does not yet contain one.
