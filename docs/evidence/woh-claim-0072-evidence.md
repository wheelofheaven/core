## Sendy's apparent accusation

In *Les temps messianiques*, ch. 17, `OEBPS/PL21.xhtml`, Sendy refers to an unnamed young man who has read his books and uses the Elohim name for purported extraterrestrial pronouncements.
Bigliardi's September 2016 draft identifies the target as Raël (section 14, printed pp. 20–21; his citation gives Sendy's print p. 233).
The accusation is present in the French EPUB.
The sentence leaves the young man unnamed; Bigliardi supports identifying him as Raël.
No documentary basis for Sendy's knowledge of the young man's reading is supplied there.
Sendy subsequently describes changing his mind about the Hill case.
His view of Raël remains unretracted in this passage.
The surrounding chapter explicitly discusses June 1975 and 1975 publications: do not repeat Bigliardi's claim that this passage was written in 1974 without further verification.
This later book can witness reception and Sendy's developing thought, but cannot simply be treated as a source preceding the first Message.

## Dependence assessment

| Hypothesis | Present support | Missing discriminator |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct selective use of Sendy | Priority of relevant works; specific shared interpretations; apparent authorial accusation | Pre-composition reading evidence; first-edition French passage alignment; distinctive non-biblical wording or shared unusual errors |
| Indirect influence through intermediaries | Compatible with shared French-language milieu and transformed motifs | Identified intermediary texts, conversations, and dates |
| Independent recombination of shared sources | Both interpret Genesis and draw on technological and esoteric materials | A control corpus showing the particular cluster already circulating independently of Sendy |
| Wholesale transcription from Sendy | Some common material | Does not account for substantial transformations; no sustained verbal-copying demonstration was performed |
| Authentic reported contact | Asserted within the Messages | Literary difference does not independently verify an encounter; this record does not adjudicate the claim |

There is no justified percentage estimate.
The evidence gives a substantive reason to investigate adaptation and does not warrant a verdict of proven borrowing, proven independence, or fraud.
Assess each feature for distinctiveness and dependence on common sources before counting it as evidence of borrowing.

## Expansion requested by the founder, 14 October 2026

The founder commissioned approximately 10,000 words of close comparison, quotations from both corpora, no naming of the original testimonial author in prose, preservation of references, and inspection of further testimonials. The existing bounded proposition remains unchanged.

Additional inspected passages:

- *Ces dieux*, ch. 35 (`PL37.xhtml`): Babel is a primitive launch installation seeking the Moon. Failure follows inadequate technology, recrimination, and dispersal. Sendy explicitly says the original visitors are dead and suggests descendants live in a nearby planetary system. This supports a more precise distinction than the previous ch. 34-only account: original visitors deceased, lineage possibly surviving; no claim that all Elohim are extinct.
- TBWTT-2:72–76: Babel is an immense rocket intended to reach the creators' planet; direct intervention disperses the knowledgeable builders and destroys equipment. Same spaceflight motif, differing destination, agency, and cause of failure.
- *Ces dieux*, ch. 24 (`PL26`): slow interstellar journeys, closed ecological cycles, and delayed communications supply context for lineage continuity; TBWTT-1:54–55 gives a radically compressed journey in reported dialogue. This is a literary comparison, not a physical adjudication.
- *Ces dieux*, ch. 18 (`PL20`): the celestial researchers learn indigenous names; TBWTT-2:31–32 has creators teaching names and uses to their creations. Direction of instruction follows each biological premise.
- *L'ère*, ch. 18 (`PL34`): biological compatibility and microbes, then the fish sentence; 2,160-year days and an Aquarius beginning around 1950. TBWTT-5:4 gives Aquarius since 1946 and 22,000 years since a creative decision; 5:56 gives a 25,000-year knowledge advantage. Different quantities must remain separate.
- *Nous autres*, opening: medievalists described as including spiritualists and materialists; natural evolution explicitly forms part of the materialist position. Its sweeping religious and civilizational polemics are attributed to Sendy, never adopted as historical fact.

Additional testimonials: [Woodgate source note](../../source-notes/testimony-of-mark-woodgate.md) and [Parent source note](../../source-notes/testimony-of-marie-helene-parent.md). The first circulates textual comparisons and repeats the second's alleged admission of acquaintance. The original report was inspected in French as well as English. It gives no date for the alleged acquaintance; repetition is not independent corroboration. No personal allegations outside the comparison are adopted.

French excerpts in the expanded article are transcribed from inspected witnesses, with adjacent English translations made for this article and explicitly labelled. ### Independent review correction

The Reviewer identified a closer serpent parallel in *L’ère*, ch. 18 (`PL34.xhtml`), paragraphs beginning “J’espère vous avoir convaincu” and “Et c’est à ce point”. Lucifer wants to bring light immediately; Satan wants humans to know everything immediately. Sendy identifies the figure as a Théosite and treats disclosure as premature. The expanded article now quotes this passage and confines the vanity-versus-love contrast to *Ces dieux* versus the Message. The broader proposition is unchanged; this further supports the existing warning about variation within Sendy.

The comparison table now asks who remains available for contact, avoiding an inference that living creators in the Message must be identical to the original personnel.
