# Evidence map: The Elohim plural and the canonical gloss

Serves [`woh-claim-0009`](../framework/elohim-plural-gloss.md). `scoped`
and `contested`. The map's discipline is the claim model's named invalid
transition: morphological form does not yield full syntactic meaning.

## Philological layer (uncontested)

| Observation | Locator | What it supports | What it does not support |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Elohim is morphologically plural (plural of Eloah) | Lexical; uncontested | The word *can* take plural reference | That it *does* in any given passage |
| Plural cohortative "let us make" | GEN-WOH-1:26 | Plural deliberation in the creation narrative | Plural referent over the heavenly-court reading |
| "As one of us" | GEN-WOH-3:22 | Same | Same |
| benei ha-elohim | GEN-WOH-6:2 | A class of divine beings | Their identity |
| The assembly and its sentence | PSA-82:1, 6–7 | A council of elohim under judgment | That the council is remembered rather than theological |

## Canonical layer

| Item | Locator | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The gloss "those who came from the sky" | TBWTT, opening narrative and throughout | Canonical rendering, **labelled as not the consensus derivation** — the consensus derives Elohim through Eloah/El |
| The singular-God translation observation | TBWTT-5:21 | Canon-explicit; the translation-history point is independently checkable and correct in outline |

## Critical layer

| Account | Force |
| --- | --- |
| Plural of majesty / abstraction | The standard grammatical resolution; fully adequate to the singular-verb usage |
| Heavenly-court reading | The standard scholarly resolution of the council texts; plurality of beings without plurality of gods |
| Diachronic development (Smith) | The strongest: every plural trace explained as edited polytheism; the referential reading adds a referent without adding a prediction |

## Dependency summary

- The philological rows are `direct` and carry no framework weight alone.
- The referential resolution is supplied by the framework (`woh-claim-0001`).
- The gloss is canonical and labelled; any artifact presenting it as
  etymology violates the record and the epistemic principles.
