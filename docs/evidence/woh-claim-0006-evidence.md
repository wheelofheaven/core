# Evidence map: Post-Homeric emissary transfer

This map serves claim
[`woh-claim-0006`](../framework/post-homeric-emissary-transfer.md). It is
`scoped`. The claim is canon-derived; the map records the textual basis, the
chronological arithmetic, and the mainstream readings that stand against it.

## Canonical layer

| Locator | What it reports | What it does not report |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `TBWTT-3:214` | Domination of Israel by Persians and Greeks read as punishment; the creators "placed men of theirs, 'angels', among these peoples, in order to have them accomplish technical progress" | Names of emissaries beyond Michael; content or mechanism of the transfer; any engagement with Greece before this era |
| `TBWTT-3:215–216` | Michael as head of the delegation to Persia, quoting Daniel | — |

## Primary-text layer

| Locator | Content | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daniel 10:13 | Michael comes to help against "the prince of the kingdom of Persia" | The verse the canon quotes |
| Daniel 10:20 | The "prince of Greece" announced as next | Extends the same frame to Greece — the canon's Greek emissary reading rests here |

## The chronological corollary

| Fact | Basis |
| --- | --- |
| Persian domination of Judea from 539 BCE; Hellenistic domination from 332 BCE | Standard chronology, uncontested |
| Homeric and Hesiodic crystallisation in the eighth–seventh centuries BCE | Mainstream consensus range (recorded in the `woh-claim-0004` apparatus) |
| Therefore: the emissary era begins ~two centuries after the poems | Arithmetic on the two rows above; this is the claim's load-bearing derivation |

## Critical layer (held at full strength)

| Reading | Force |
| --- | --- |
| Daniel's "princes" as patron angels of the nations (Second Temple angelology) | The standard scholarly reading; no transfer content |
| Daniel's final form dated to the second century BCE | The text the canon reads post-dates the era it describes; the canon does not address this |
| Technology-transfer as modern retrofit | The category-substitution objection applied to this specific passage |

## Dependency summary

- The canonical layer is explicit; the claim's `framework` label reflects
  that it is canon-explicit and not mainstream-endorsed.
- The chronological corollary is the exportable content: it *constrains* the
  framework (no emissary explanation for archaic material) rather than
  extending it. Its two inputs are uncontested.
- No inspected source supports the transfer as history; the alternatives
  are currently at least as economical, exactly as recorded.
