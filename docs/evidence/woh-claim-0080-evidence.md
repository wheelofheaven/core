## B. Observations offered for the claim (not inspected)

Each needs a source note and a reading before any use. Descriptions are
from general knowledge and may be wrong in detail.

## C. Observations against the claim (not inspected)

| Observation | What it shows on the standard reading | What the claim would have to hold | State |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Concordant ages from independent decay systems on the same rock | Hundreds of millions of years for fossil-bearing strata. | That decay behaviour differed in the past, for every system, in step. | To inspect. Raël argues inconstancy (`LWTE-1:28`); non-canonical. |

| A fossil order that is the same worldwide | Descent with modification over deep time. | Order of creation and of burial (`TBWTT-5:62`; `woh-claim-0071`). | The order itself is common ground; its cause is the dispute. |

## Independence summary

- Section A is one source.
- Sections B and C are physical observations independent of the canon and
  of each other in varying degrees. Radiocarbon in fossils, and the
  annual-layer records, are the places where the two accounts predict
  different numbers.
- Catastrophist and creationist authors who assemble section B's items
  mostly draw on each other; several citations of one case are one
  observation.
