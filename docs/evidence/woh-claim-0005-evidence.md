# Evidence map: Near Eastern transmission to archaic Greece

This map serves claim
[`woh-claim-0005`](../framework/near-eastern-transmission-to-greece.md). It is
`scoped`. The claim is an `empirical_observation` recording mainstream
scholarship's position. The map preserves the difference between an unusually
articulated dependence case, a cumulative supporting pattern, a plausible
route, and a generic resemblance.

## Verified layer

| Locator | What it establishes | Qualification retained |
| --- | --- | --- |
| West, preface vii–ix; ch. 1, pp. 59–60 | The book's case is cumulative and spans many levels of poetry and myth; West does not generally claim that a Greek poet copied a specific written original | Individual parallels carry unequal weight; differences between traditions remain evidence to explain |
| West, ch. 4, pp. 168–174, 190–191; ch. 5, pp. 220–226 | Comparanda extend below whole myths into poem openings, procedural narrative, assemblies, speeches, messenger sequences, formulaic epithets, and idiom | West does not derive the Greek hexameter from an eastern metre; the Muses are characteristically Greek; some phrases can arise independently |
| West, ch. 6, pp. 276–304, 332 | The Ouranos–Kronos–Zeus succession and the Anu–Kumarbi–Teshub succession form an unusually dense sequence; Babylonian succession material is broader but less close; Typhoeus belongs to a wider divine-combat family | West separates the Typhoeus story from the succession sequence and preserves differences among Ullikummi, Illuyanka, Baal–Yamm, Marduk–Tiamat, and the Greek combat |
| West, ch. 8, pp. 402–417, 437 | The wanderings strand of the *Odyssey* has a strong literary relationship with *Gilgamesh*: proem, far-traveller frame, island women, Phaeacian transition, underworld, wakefulness, and sacred cattle | Character identity is explicitly denied; the *Odyssey* overall is judged less thickly eastern than the *Iliad* |
| West, ch. 12, pp. 586–630 | Several contact periods and human carriers are historically available: Mycenaean contact, Cyprus, Crete, Anatolia, the Levant, Pithekoussai, traders, craftsmen, interpreters, migrants, mercenaries, and bilingual singers | Exact routes are usually unrecoverable; a bilingual immigrant poet is a reasoned scenario, not a documented biography |

## The boundary

| Invalid step | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| Borrowing → civilizational origination | Contact and prestige asymmetry are what literary dependence shows; Mesopotamian priority in writing and urbanism is a separate archaeological fact |
| Relationship → hero identity | Denied by the verified source itself |
| Contact field → known itinerary | Historical routes and multilingual communities make transmission possible; they rarely identify the route taken by a particular story |
| Transmission → shared historical referent | Literary contact fully explains why related stories appear in both corpora; any deeper referent is a separate interpretive claim |
| Eastern material → Greek passivity | Reception entails selection, recomposition, and invention; Greek difference is part of the evidence, not noise |

## Dependency summary

- The verified layer supports the claim's cumulative form and the
  *Odyssey*–*Gilgamesh* clause without crossing into identity.
- The scoped layer must be inspected before this claim's evidence status can
  move beyond `scoped`.
- Nothing in this map supports an Elohimian conclusion. Its discipline is the
  support it lends neighbouring claims: it shows exactly where scholarship
  ends and framework interpretation begins.
